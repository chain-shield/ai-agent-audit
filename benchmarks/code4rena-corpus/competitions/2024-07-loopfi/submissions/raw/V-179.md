# Vulnerability details

There is an issue in the implementation of `liquidatePosition()` that leads to an inconsistency between the `totalDebt` state variable and the actual sum of all positions' debts. While individual positions' debts are adjusted, the global `totalDebt` remains artificially inflated, no longer representing the sum of all outstanding debts.

The `liquidatePosition()` function is defined in CDPVault.sol:501-574. It takes the owner of the position to be liquidated and the repayAmount as parameters. The function calculates the debt data of the position, determines the amount of collateral to seize and debt to repay, and updates the position's debt accordingly.

The fact is that while the function updates the individual position's debt (`newDebt`), it fails to update the `totalDebt` state variable to reflect the change. This can be seen here: [CDPVault.sol#L561-L563](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-07-loopfi/blob/57871f64bdea450c1f04c9a53dc1a78223719164/src/CDPVault.sol#L561-L563)
```solidity
position = _modifyPosition(owner, position, newDebt, newCumulativeIndex, -toInt256(takeCollateral), totalDebt);

pool.repayCreditAccount(debtData.debt - newDebt, profit, 0); // U:[CM-11]
```
After updating the position with the new debt value, the function proceeds to repay the credit account. However, it does not update the `totalDebt` state variable to account for the repaid debt amount (`debtData.debt - newDebt`).

## Impact
This issue can lead to a violation of the invariant `newDebt <= newTotalDebt`, which ensures that a position's debt does not exceed the total debt after executing `liquidatePosition()`. In a scenario where a position's entire debt is repaid during liquidation, `newDebt` becomes 0, but `totalDebt` remains unchanged, resulting in `newTotalDebt` being greater than `newDebt`.

> the invariant `newDebt <= newTotalDebt` in the `liquidatePosition()` function is a specific application of the contract invariant `Debt >= Borrowed`. It ensures that the debt accounting remains consistent and accurate after the liquidation process, maintaining the integrity of the `CDPVault` contract.

The inconsistency between `totalDebt` and the actual sum of all positions' debts can have several consequences:

1. The contract's state will not accurately reflect the true total debt, leading to discrepancies in financial calculations and reporting.
2. In extreme cases, if the inconsistency is significant and left unchecked, it could lead to the loss of funds for the contract or its users.

`newDebt` represents the updated debt of the position being liquidated after the liquidation process. It is calculated based on the repayment amount and the position's current debt.

`newTotalDebt` represents the total debt of the system after the liquidation process. It should be the sum of all positions' debts, including the updated debt of the liquidated position.

The invariant `newDebt <= newTotalDebt` ensures that the debt of an individual position does not exceed the total debt of the system after the liquidation process. In other words, it guarantees that the total debt remains greater than or equal to the debt of any single position.

This invariance is important for maintaining the integrity and consistency of the debt accounting in the `CDPVault` contract. It ensures that the total debt accurately reflects the sum of all positions' debts and that no position has a debt greater than the total debt.

Now, let's relate this to the contract invariant `Debt >= Borrowed`:

* `Debt` represents the total debt of the system, which is the sum of all positions' debts. In the context of the `liquidatePosition()` function, `newTotalDebt` corresponds to the Debt after the liquidation process.
* `Borrowed` represents the amount borrowed by an individual position. In the context of the `liquidatePosition(`) function, `newDebt` corresponds to the Borrowed amount for the liquidated position after the liquidation process.

The contract invariant Debt >= Borrowed ensures that the total debt of the system is always greater than or equal to the borrowed amount of any individual position. This invariant holds true for all positions in the system.

The invariant `newDebt <= newTotalDebt` in the `liquidatePosition()` function is a specific case of the contract invariant Debt >= Borrowed. It ensures that after the liquidation process, the updated debt of the liquidated position (`newDebt`) does not exceed the updated total debt of the system (`newTotalDeb`).

By maintaining the invariant `newDebt <= newTotalDebt`, the `liquidatePosition()` function ensures that the contract invariant `Debt >= Borrowed` holds true after the liquidation process. It guarantees that the total debt remains consistent with the sum of all positions' debts and that no position's debt exceeds the total debt.

## Proof of Concept
Consider a scenario with a single position that has a debt of 1000 and a `totalDebt` of 1000. When the entire debt is repaid during liquidation:

1. The `liquidatePosition()` function is called with the position's `owner` and a `repayAmount` of 1000.
2. The function calculates the debt data and determines that the entire debt should be repaid.
3. It updates the position's debt to `newDebt = 0`.
4. The function repays the credit account with the repaid debt amount (`debtData.debt - newDebt = 1000`).
5. However, it fails to update the `totalDebt` state variable, which remains at 1000.

As a result, `newDebt` becomes 0, but `newTotalDebt` is still 1000, violating the invariant `newDebt <= newTotalDebt`.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
The `totalDebt` state variable should be updated after the debt repayment in the `liquidatePosition()` function. 
```diff
position = _modifyPosition(owner, position, newDebt, newCumulativeIndex, -toInt256(takeCollateral), totalDebt);

+ uint256 repaidDebt = debtData.debt - newDebt;
pool.repayCreditAccount(debtData.debt - newDebt, profit, 0); // U:[CM-11]
+ totalDebt -= repaidDebt;
```
By subtracting the repaid debt amount from `totalDebt`, the contract ensures that the total debt remains consistent with the actual sum of all positions' debts, maintaining the invariant `newDebt <= newTotalDebt` after liquidation.
