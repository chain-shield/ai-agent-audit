# Vulnerability details

## Impact
 Protocol Insolvency:

  - Repeated unauthorized liquidations lead to a significant outflow of 
    collateral from the protocol. As a result, the protocol becomes unable to 
    cover the outstanding debt with the remaining collateral. This insolvency can 
    cause the protocol to collapse, leading to a total loss of user funds and the 
    failure of the entire system.
 
## Proof of Concept
 - [Source Code Line 526](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-07-loopfi/blob/57871f64bdea450c1f04c9a53dc1a78223719164/src/CDPVault.sol#L526)
- [Source Code Lines 535-536](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-07-loopfi/blob/57871f64bdea450c1f04c9a53dc1a78223719164/src/CDPVault.sol#L535-L536)


The vulnerability lies in the `liquidatePosition` function of the `CDPVault` contract, where the validation logic for determining whether a position should be liquidated is flawed. 
Specifically, the checks used to assess the safety of a position do not accurately account for the true collateral and debt status. This can result in positions being liquidated even when they have sufficient collateral to cover their debt, leading to unauthorized and unfair loss of user assets.

```solidity
if (calcTotalDebt(debtData) > wmul(position.collateral, spotPrice_)) revert CDPVault__BadDebt();
```
```solidity
if (_isCollateralized(calcTotalDebt(debtData), wmul(position.collateral, spotPrice_), config.liquidationRatio))
            revert CDPVault__liquidatePosition_notUnsafe();

``` 
This improper validation can be exploited by malicious actors to trigger liquidations on otherwise healthy positions, causing significant financial harm to users and potentially leading to the insolvency of the protocol.

### Detailed Step-by-Step Exploit Scenario
#### Initial State

*   **User Position:**
    
    *   The user has a debt of 1000 units.
        
    *   The user has 20 units of collateral.
        
    *   The actual spot price of the collateral is 100 units.
        
*   **Collateral Value:**
    
    *   The total value of the user's collateral is 20 \* 100 = 2000 units.
        
*   The user's position is safe and well-collateralized.
    

#### Step 1: Trigger Liquidation

*   **Action:** The attacker calls the liquidatePosition function with a repay amount that exploits the improper liquidation validation.
    
*   **Condition Check:**
    
    *   The contract checks if the total debt exceeds the collateral value at the current spot price.
 ```solidity
if (calcTotalDebt(debtData) > wmul(position.collateral, spotPrice_)) revert CDPVault__BadDebt();
```   
  
   *   Given:
        
        *   totalDebt = 1000
            
        *   collateral = 20
            
        *   spotPrice = 100
            
        *   collateral value = 20 \* 100 = 2000
            
   *   The condition evaluates to false, allowing the liquidation to proceed.

#### Step 2: Liquidation Process
#### Collateral Taken

- **Calculation of Collateral:**  
    The contract calculates the amount of collateral to take based on the given repay amount and the liquidation discount.

    - Assume the repay amount is 1000 units and the liquidation discount is 10%.
    - The discounted price of the collateral is:  
      `discountedPrice = spotPrice × (1 - liquidationDiscount) = 100 × 0.9 = 90`
    - The amount of collateral to take is:  
      `takeCollateral = repayAmount / discountedPrice = 1000 / 90 ≈ 11.11`

- **Action:**  
    The contract takes approximately 11.11 units of collateral.

#### Debt Repaid

- **Collateral Used to Repay Debt:**  
    The contract uses the collateral taken to repay the debt.

    - The user's debt of 1000 units is repaid using the 11.11 units of collateral valued at the discounted price.

#### Outcome

- **Final Impact:**  
    - The user's position is liquidated, and they lose 11.11 units of their collateral.
    - The liquidation occurs even though the user's position was initially safe and well-collateralized.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
*   Refine the condition used to determine if a position should be liquidated.
    
*   Consider additional checks to ensure that the position is genuinely unsafe before proceeding with liquidation

* Add a buffer to the collateralization ratio to provide an additional safety margin before triggering liquidation.
