# Vulnerability details

## Proof of Concept
In the function for calculating the borrowable amount, [the following line](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-07-loopfi/blob/57871f64bdea450c1f04c9a53dc1a78223719164/src/PoolV3.sol#L470) of code is used:
```solidity
borrowable = Math.min(borrowable, _borrowable(_creditManagerDebt[creditManager]));
```
Here, borrowable is being constrained by the result of `_borrowable(_creditManagerDebt[creditManager])`. This function call returns an effective limit based on the credit manager's current debt. The issue arises because this effective limit might be less than the maximum allowable limit defined for the credit manager. As a result, borrowable may be reduced more than necessary if the effective limit is low.

The purpose of this calculation is to ensure that the amount available for borrowing does not exceed the credit manager's capacity. However, by using the effective limit, the calculation may fail to account for cases where the maximum limit `(_creditManagerDebt[creditManager].limit)` is higher but not reflected in the current effective limit due to the credit manager's existing debt.
## Impact
This can lead to a situation where the borrowable amount is restricted more than needed. If the effective limit is lower than the actual maximum limit set by [_creditManagerDebt[creditManager].limit](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-07-loopfi/blob/57871f64bdea450c1f04c9a53dc1a78223719164/src/PoolV3.sol#L462), users may not be able to borrow as much as they are theoretically allowed to, which can impact the overall flexibility and utility of the credit system.


## Tools Used
Manual
## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Update the calculation to use the maximum limit directly rather than the effective limit:
```solidity
borrowable = Math.min(borrowable, _creditManagerDebt[creditManager].limit);
```
