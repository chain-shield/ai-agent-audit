# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The incorrect debt calculation in the `calcDecrease` function can lead to improper liquidations and asset loss. Specifically, the formula used to calculate the new cumulative index does not correctly account for partial repayments, leading to inaccurate debt balances. This can result in:

- Positions being incorrectly marked as safe or unsafe.
- Improper liquidations, either failing to liquidate when necessary or 
  liquidating when not required.
- Potential asset loss for both the protocol and its users due to incorrect debt 
  and collateral management.

#### High Severity 
- Incorrect debt calculations can lead to improper liquidations, resulting in 
  direct asset loss for users.
- Valid attack path: The incorrect formula provides a clear and direct path for 
  exploitation, where users' positions be incorrectly liquidated or not 
  liquidated when they should be, leading to financial loss.

The `calcDecrease` function uses the following formula to calculate the new cumulative index:
```solidity
newCumulativeIndex =
    (INDEX_PRECISION * cumulativeIndexNow * cumulativeIndexLastUpdate) /
    (INDEX_PRECISION * cumulativeIndexNow -
    (INDEX_PRECISION * profit * cumulativeIndexLastUpdate) / debt);
```
This formula adjusts the cumulative index based on the profit and the debt.
The use of `profit * cumulativeIndexLastUpdate` can introduce inaccuracies, especially in partial repayments.

Let's say: 
- CumulativeIndexNow (CI_Now) = 1.05 (105% in Ray)
- CumulativeIndexLastUpdate (CI_Last) = 1.00 (100% in Ray)
- Profit (P) = 0.01 (1% in Ray)
- Debt (D) = 1,000,000 units
- INDEX_PRECISION (IP) = 10^9

Now, let's walk through how this bug leads to the liquidation and potential loss of collateral.

1. Incorrect Debt Calculation
The problematic current coded formula is:

```plaintext
newCumulativeIndex = (IP * CI_Now * CI_Last) / (IP * CI_Now - (IP * P * CI_Last) / D)

Substitute the example values:

newCumulativeIndex = (10^9 * 1.05 * 1.00) / (10^9 * 1.05 - (10^9 * 0.01 * 1.00) / 1,000,000)

Numerator:

10^9 * 1.05 * 1.00 = 1.05 * 10^9

Denominator:

10^9 * 1.05 - (10^9 * 0.01 * 1.00) / 1,000,000
= 1.05 * 10^9 - 10,000
= 1,050,000,000 - 10,000
= 1,049,990,000

Final Calculation:

newCumulativeIndex = 1.05 * 10^9 / 1,049,990,000 ≈ 1.000009525

2. Comparison of Debt Before and After 

Correct newCumulativeIndex (without bug): 1.05

Incorrect newCumulativeIndex (with bug): 1.000009525

Difference:
1.05 - 1.000009525 = 0.049990475
```

The debt is underestimated by approximately 0.05% in this scenario.


3. Loss in Collateral Due to Liquidation

Let's say: 

Debt: 1,000,000 units
Collateral: 500,000 units valued at $100 each (total $50,000,000)

With the correct calculation, the debt would be fully repaid without liquidation. With the incorrect calculation, liquidation is triggered due to the debt being underestimated.

Amount of Collateral Taken:

Liquidation discount: 10%
```plaintext
collateralTaken = (1,000,000 / (100 * 0.9)) = 11,111 units
```
#### Final Loss:

The user stands to lose $1,111,111 worth of collateral due to unnecessary liquidation, even though their position was safe and well-collateralized.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
 
- correct the formula used to calculate the new cumulative index to ensure it accurately accounts for partial repayments.
- The corrected formula to calculate the new cumulative index is:

```plaintext
 newCumulativeIndex = (INDEX_PRECISION * cumulativeIndexNow * cumulativeIndexLastUpdate) / 
                     (INDEX_PRECISION * cumulativeIndexNow - (INDEX_PRECISION * amountToRepay) / debt);

```
- This formula adjusts the cumulative index based on the amount repaid `(amountToRepay)` and the total debt.
- It directly accounts for the repayment amount, making it more intuitive and potentially more accurate.
