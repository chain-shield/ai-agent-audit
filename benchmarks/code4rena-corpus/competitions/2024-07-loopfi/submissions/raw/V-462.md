# Vulnerability details

## Impact

If the liquidation parameters (repayAmount, discountedPrice, etc.) are not correctly validated or if there's a flaw in the calculation of takeCollateral, a malicious actor might exploit this to take more collateral than intended.


## Proof of Concept
In the liquidatePosition function, several parameters and calculations determine how much collateral a liquidator can take, how much debt they need to repay, and how penalties are applied. These include:

1)repayAmount: The amount of debt the liquidator wants to repay.
2)discountedPrice: The price of collateral used during liquidation, which is a discounted value of the spot price.
3)takeCollateral: The amount of collateral that will be transferred to the liquidator based on the repay amount and discounted price.


If the liquidation parameters and calculations are not correctly validated or if there are flaws in these calculations, a malicious actor might exploit these issues to gain more collateral than intended. Here’s how this can happen:


1)Incorrect Calculation of takeCollateral:
The calculation for takeCollateral is derived from:

uint256 takeCollateral = wdiv(repayAmount, discountedPrice);

Here, wdiv is a function that performs a division operation. If the discountedPrice is too low (whether due to incorrect pricing or manipulation), it could result in takeCollateral being disproportionately high.

Example:

Suppose the discountedPrice is calculated as 1, but in reality, it's supposed to be much higher (e.g., 5).
If repayAmount is 100, then:
 
takeCollateral = wdiv(100, 1) = 100

Instead of taking 20 units of collateral (if the discountedPrice was 5), the liquidator ends up with 100 units.


2) Manipulation of repayAmount:

The liquidator might attempt to exploit the system by choosing a repayAmount that maximizes their gain while keeping other parameters within acceptable ranges. If the repayAmount is not properly validated or if there are no checks against unreasonable values, this can be exploited.


3)Validation and Handling of Parameters:

Proper Validation Missing:

If repayAmount is too high, the system might not properly handle or cap this value, leading to excessive collateral being taken.

Example of Exploitation:

If the liquidation function allows repayAmount to be very high without sufficient checks, an attacker might exploit this by repeatedly triggering liquidation with large repayAmount values, extracting more collateral than what’s intended or available.


How This Could Impact the System:
1)Financial Loss-
If a liquidator is able to take more collateral than what they should have based on the repay amount, this could lead to significant financial losses for the system. The system might not recover enough debt, and the excess collateral provided might be higher than what’s warranted.

2)Market Manipulation:

An attacker could manipulate the system to extract more collateral by exploiting flaws in how discountedPrice is calculated or how repayAmount is handled, disrupting the balance and fairness of the liquidation process.



## Recommended Mitigation Steps

1)Rigorous Parameter Validation:
Ensure that all input parameters like repayAmount and discountedPrice are rigorously validated. Implement checks to prevent excessively high values or manipulative inputs.

2)Accurate Pricing and Discounts:
Validate that the discountedPrice reflects the true market conditions and is not subject to manipulation or errors.

3)Capping and Limits:
Implement caps and limits on repayAmount and the maximum amount of collateral that can be taken to prevent excessive liquidation.

4)Comprehensive Testing:
Conduct extensive testing and simulations to ensure that the liquidation process behaves correctly under various scenarios, and that any potential edge cases are handled appropriately.
