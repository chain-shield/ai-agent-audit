# Vulnerability details

## Impact

The current implementation of the BalancerOracle is vulnerable to sandwich attacks, allowing malicious actors to manipulate the reported price. This can lead to severe consequences in the protocol that rely on this oracle for pricing, including:

1. Incorrect liquidations: Positions may be liquidated prematurely or not liquidated when they should be.
2. Mispriced loans: Users could borrow more or less than they should be able to based on their collateral.
3. Arbitrage opportunities: Attackers could profit from the price discrepancies between the manipulated oracle price and the actual market price.

The vulnerability could potentially lead to large-scale theft of funds or protocol insolvency in extreme cases.

## Proof of Concept

The vulnerability stems from the BalancerOracle's `update()` function, which calculates the current price based on the pool's state at a single point in time, making it susceptible to manipulation.

Here's a proof of concept attack scenario:
**Let's assume;**
   - Balancer Weighted Pool: 1000 wETH (50% weight) and 2,000,000 USDC (50% weight)
   - Pool Invariant: 1,414,213,562
   - Total Supply of BPT: 1,000,000
   - Oracle Price: 2000 USDC per wETH (interpreted)

**Attacker actions:**
   a. Monitor the mempool for incoming "update" transactions to the BalancerOracle.
   b. Front-run the update by swapping 500 wETH for USDC, changing the pool composition to approximately 1500 wETH and 1,333,333 USDC.
   c. Allow the oracle update to execute, which will now report a BPT price of 1,414.21.
   d. Back-run by swapping USDC back to wETH, returning the pool to its original state.

**Result:**
   - The oracle now reports a BPT price of 1,414.21, which could be misinterpreted as 1 wETH = 1,414.21 USDC.
   - The actual market price remains close to 2000 USDC per wETH.
   - If a lending protocol uses this oracle, the attacker could now borrow against their wETH at the manipulated lower price, effectively borrowing more than they should be able to.
   - Alternatively, existing loans that should be liquidated might not be, allowing borrowers to avoid liquidation.

This attack can be repeated and scaled up, potentially leading to significant profit for the attacker and losses for the protocol and its users.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

- Implement Time-Weighted Average Price (TWAP):
   Instead of using the current pool state, calculate the price based on a time-weighted average over a specific period. This makes it much more costly and difficult for attackers to manipulate the price.

- Implement price deviation checks:
   Add logic to detect and potentially revert significant price changes between updates. This can help prevent sudden, manipulated price swings from being reported.

- Add a delay mechanism:
   Implement a delay between price updates and their use in the protocol. This gives time for the pool state to stabilize and makes sandwich attacks less effective.
