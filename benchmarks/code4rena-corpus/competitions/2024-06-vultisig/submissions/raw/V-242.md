# Vulnerability details



## Impact
`ILOManager` relies on the `slot0`(heavily frowned at) of the `IUniswapV3Pool` interface to retrieve the current price (`sqrtPriceX96`) of a trading pair in the Uniswap V3 pool. This exposes the contract and its users to significant risks of price manipulation that can cause losses.

By using the most recent price data from `slot0`, the contract becomes vulnerable to price manipulation attacks executed through techniques such as MEV bots and flash loans. Malicious actors can artificially inflate or deflate the price of a token pair momentarily, causing the `ILOManager` contract to use the manipulated price for its internal calculations and transactions.

The consequences of using a manipulated price can be severe:
1. Users may receive unfair token swap rates.
2. The contract's internal calculations for token allocations, fee distributions, and other critical operations may be based on the manipulated price.
3. Malicious actors can profit from the price manipulation by back-running transactions and exploiting the contract's reliance on the manipulated price.

Furthermore, the use of `slot0` for price retrieval is not recommended by the Uniswap V3 documentation, as it provides only a single data point and does not account for price fluctuations over time. This approach fails to ensure a fair and stable price for the contract's operations.

In both cases, the contract directly uses the price obtained from `slot0` without any safeguards against price manipulation. An attacker can exploit this vulnerability by following these steps:

1. The attacker identifies a trading pair with low liquidity on Uniswap V3.
2. Using MEV bots or flash loans, the attacker manipulates the price of the trading pair by creating artificial demand or supply, causing a significant price swing.
3. The attacker triggers the execution of the `_initUniV3PoolIfNecessary` or `launch` function in the `ILOManager` contract, which retrieves the manipulated price from `slot0`.
4. The `ILOManager` contract uses the manipulated price for its internal calculations and transactions.
5. The attacker back-runs the transaction, reversing the price manipulation and profiting from the contract's use of the manipulated price.

This process can be repeated multiple times, allowing the attacker to drain funds from the contract and its users.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Use the `TWAP` to get the value of `sqrtPriceX96` instead of relying on the most recent price from `slot0`. TWAP provides a more stable and reliable price estimation by considering the average price over a specified time period.
