# Vulnerability details

## Impact
Uniswap's oracles use time-weighted average prices (TWAP) based on on-chain data. This means they calculate prices based on the average price over a set period, which makes it harder to manipulate the price with short-term spikes or dips. However, if an attacker can influence the price significantly for a sufficient period, they can manipulate the TWAP. This is because Uniswap prices are determined by the ratio of the assets in the pool (constant product formula: `x * y = k`)

## Proof of Concept
1. Acquire Capital: The attacker needs a significant amount of capital to manipulate the liquidity pool
   
2. Initial Swap: The attacker performs a large swap to drastically change the price of the token pair
   
3. To maintain the manipulated price long enough for the oracle to update, the attacker might need to continue performing additional swaps or provide/remove liquidity to keep the price at the desired manipulated level
   
4. The attacker waits for the TWAP to update, reflecting the manipulated price. This period depends on the implementation but typically spans several blocks to minutes
   
5. The attacker can now exploit contracts that rely on the manipulated Uniswap price. For example, they might trigger liquidations, arbitrage opportunities, or loans in DeFi protocols that use this oracle for price feeds.
   
6. After profiting from the manipulated price, the attacker may revert the price manipulation by swapping back to the original state or close to it. This step aims to avoid prolonged losses from the initial manipulation trades.

## Tools Used
Solidity 

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
5 Different options to proceed with:

1. Longer TWAP Periods: Using longer TWAP periods can reduce the impact of short-term price manipulations.
   
2. Multi-source Oracles: Relying on price feeds from multiple sources can mitigate the risk of manipulation in a single source.

3. Price Bands: Implementing checks that compare the TWAP price against a broader market price can prevent outliers.
   
4. Circuit Breakers: Deploying mechanisms that halt trading or other sensitive operations when unusual price behavior is detected.
   
5. Decentralized Oracles: Using decentralized oracle networks like Chainlink, which aggregate data from multiple sources to provide a more reliable price feed.
