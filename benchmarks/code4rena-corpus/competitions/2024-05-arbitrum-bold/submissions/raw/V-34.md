# Vulnerability details

## Impact
Users whose stakes were used to create the pool move can exit the pool with the excess stakes of other users by frontrunning them.

## Proof of Concept
The total deposited amount can exceed the required stake amount. If the total deposited amount exceeds the required amount, any depositor can withdraw some stake early even after the protocol move has been made.

This means that excess stakes are not being refunded back to the users who staked them after the pool move. They are left in the pool. This creates a race condition between the users whose stakes were used to create the pool move and the actual owner of the excess stake. The users whose staked funds were used to create the pool move can withdraw the excess stake of other users by frontrunning them. 
Consider the following scenarios.

Scenario 1:
If an assertion is wrong, the stakers on that assertion will lose their stake. Alice realizes that the assertion they are staking on is wrong after a pool move. She quickly exits the pool using the excess stake of Bob. Now, only Bob's funds are staked and allice has successfully evaded losing her stake on a wrong assertion. 

Scenario 2:
Malicious users discover that they can exit the pool with the excess stake of other users after a pool move. They exploit this to create risk free wrong assertions by leaving with the excess stakes of other users after each pool move.
This defeats the purpose of staking on assertions which is to discourage users from creating wrong assertions. Currently, a malicious user can create wrong assertions that are risk free by leaving with the excess stake of ether users after the pool move.

Consider this example. Alice and Bob are both stakers in the pool. The required stake amount is 10 ether. Alice deposits 6 ether and Bob deposits 10 ether. After the pool move, Bob's remaining deposit of 6 ether is left in the pool. Alice frontruns bob's partial stake withdrawal transaction to withdraw her entire staked balance with Bob's excess staked funds. All of Bob's deposits are now staked. Bob bears all the staking risks and Alice gets her deposited amount back and a risk free assertion when the assertion is created.

The following test can be added to AbsBoldStakingPool.t.sol to demonstrate the described scenario.

```
    function testWithdrawOtherStakersFunds() external {
        vm.prank(staker1);
        pool.depositIntoPool(6 ether);

        vm.startPrank(fullStaker);
        pool.depositIntoPool(10 ether);
        pool.createMove();
        vm.stopPrank();

        // Frontrun fullStaker to withdraw the excess funds 
        vm.startPrank(staker1);
        pool.withdrawFromPool(6 ether);
        assertEq(token.balanceOf(staker1), 6 ether, "excess stake wasn't withdrawn");
        vm.stopPrank();

        // fullStaker can't withdraw his excess staked funds and he is now the only staker in the pool.
        vm.startPrank(fullStaker);
        vm.expectRevert();
        pool.withdrawFromPool(6 ether);
        vm.stopPrank();
    }
```

## Tools Used
Manual Review, Foundry.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
The protocol should refund users who have excess stake back their funds as opposed to leaving the excess stake in the pool.
