# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The `RollupUserLogic` contract does not include explicit gas optimizations or mechanisms to prevent excessive transaction execution. The absence of safeguards against low gas fees opens up the possibility for an attacker to exploit the system by executing a large number of transactions at a low cost, potentially leading to Denial of Service (DoS) attacks and resource exhaustion.

The impact of this vulnerability can be significant and wide-ranging, affecting the overall performance, availability, and security of the protocol:

Denial of Service (DoS) Attacks:

-An attacker can take advantage of low gas fees to flood the network with a high volume of transactions, causing congestion and degrading the system's performance.
-Legitimate transactions may face increased confirmation times, delays, or even fail to be processed altogether.
-The protocol's throughput and responsiveness can be severely impacted, rendering it unusable for normal operations.

Resource Exhaustion:

-Excessive transaction execution can consume a substantial amount of computational resources, including gas, storage, and memory.
-If the protocol is not optimized to handle a large influx of transactions efficiently, it may lead to resource depletion and potential system failures.
-The increased load on the network can cause nodes to struggle with processing and storing transactions, leading to performance degradation and potential outages.

Exploitation of Gas-Dependent Vulnerabilities:

-If the protocol contains any vulnerabilities that are sensitive to gas costs, such as integer overflows or underflows, an attacker can exploit them by executing a large number of transactions at a low cost.
-These vulnerabilities can be triggered to manipulate the system's state, bypass access controls, or perform unauthorized actions.
-The attacker may be able to steal funds, corrupt data, or disrupt the protocol's intended functionality.

Financial Losses:

-The exploitation of low gas fees can lead to financial losses for the protocol and its users.
-Attackers may leverage the vulnerability to execute fraudulent transactions, manipulate market conditions, or drain funds from the system.
-The protocol may incur increased operational costs due to the need to process and store a large volume of transactions.

## Proof of Concept
Identify the absence of gas optimizations and transaction limits in the `RollupUserLogic` contract.

Monitor the gas prices on the target chain (Ethereum or Arbitrum, most likely Arbitrum) and wait for a period of low gas fees.

Prepare a large number of transactions that interact with the vulnerable functions in the `RollupUserLogic` contract, such as `newStakeOnNewAssertion`, `stakeOnNewAssertion`, or `reduceDeposit`.

Set the gas price for these transactions to a low value to minimize the cost of execution.

Deploy a script or a bot that automatically submits the prepared transactions to the network in a rapid succession.

As the network is flooded with a high volume of low-cost transactions, the protocol's performance starts to degrade.

Legitimate transactions face increased confirmation times or fail to be processed, causing disruption to normal operations.

The excessive transaction execution consumes a significant amount of gas, storage, and memory, leading to resource exhaustion on the network nodes.

If the protocol contains any gas-dependent vulnerabilities, the attacker can exploit them by triggering the vulnerable code paths with the low-cost transactions.

The attacker may manipulate the system's state, bypass access controls, or perform unauthorized actions, resulting in financial losses or data corruption.

Evaluate and mitigate potential attack vectors associated with gas fees.

Some Mitigations include:
Introduce gas limits and transaction thresholds to prevent excessive gas consumption

Implement rate limiting mechanisms to restrict the number of transactions that can be submitted by a single account
