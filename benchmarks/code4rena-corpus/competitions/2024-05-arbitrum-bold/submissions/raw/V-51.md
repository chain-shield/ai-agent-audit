# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The `MerkleTreeLib` library does not explicitly handle the case of duplicate proofs within the Merkle tree. The vulnerability is present throughout the library, as there are no specific checks or mechanisms to enforce the uniqueness of proofs.

The absence of proper handling of duplicate proofs within the Merkle tree can have severe implications for the security and integrity of the system. Attackers can exploit this vulnerability to engage in double-spending attacks, where they submit the same proof multiple times to claim rewards or perform actions repeatedly. The impact of this vulnerability includes:

Financial losses, as attackers can drain funds from the system by submitting duplicate proofs and claiming undeserved rewards.

Inconsistencies in the system's state, leading to errors and discrepancies in accounting and tracking of actions.

Disruption of the protocol's functionality, as the system may become overloaded with duplicate proofs and face performance issues.

Loss of trust in the system, as users may lose confidence in the fairness and reliability of the protocol.

## Proof of Concept
The attacker obtains a valid proof for a specific leaf in the Merkle tree. This proof represents a legitimate action or reward that the attacker is entitled to.

The attacker submits the same proof multiple times to the system, each time claiming the associated reward or performing the corresponding action.

Due to the lack of uniqueness checks in the Merkle tree implementation, the system accepts the duplicate proofs without detecting any anomalies.

The attacker successfully claims the reward or performs the action multiple times, receiving undeserved benefits.

The attacker can repeat this process with different proofs, accumulating rewards or performing actions beyond what is intended by the system's design.

As a result, the attacker can drain funds from the system, disrupt the protocol's functionality, and cause inconsistencies in the system's state.

The attacker can continue exploiting this vulnerability until the system's resources are exhausted or the inconsistencies become apparent, leading to potential system failures or loss of trust from users.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Ensure the Merkle tree construction and verification process detects and prevents the use of duplicate proofs.

Enforce the uniqueness of proofs within the Merkle tree implementation. This can be achieved by:

Maintaining a record of previously processed proofs and checking against it before accepting new proofs.

Implementing proper accounting and reconciliation mechanisms to ensure that actions or rewards associated with a proof are only granted once, even if the proof is submitted multiple times.

Considering the use of a bloom filter or a similar data structure to efficiently check for the existence of a proof before processing it.
