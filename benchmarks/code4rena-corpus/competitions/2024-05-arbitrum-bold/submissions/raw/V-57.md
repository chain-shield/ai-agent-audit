# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The `verifyPrefixProof` function in the `MerkleTreeLib` library contains a while loop that iterates until the size reaches the `postSize`. If the `postSize` is significantly larger than the `preSize`, the loop could potentially consume a large amount of gas, leading to Out-of-Gas exceptions or Denial-of-Service (DoS) conditions. 

The unbounded loop in the `verifyPrefixProof` function can have the following impacts:

Gas exhaustion: If the `postSize` is significantly larger than the `preSize`, the loop may iterate many times, consuming a large amount of gas. This can lead to Out-of-Gas exceptions, causing the transaction to revert and potentially making the contract unavailable.

Denial-of-Service (DoS): An attacker can intentionally provide a large `postSize` value to force the loop to consume excessive gas, leading to a DoS condition. This can prevent legitimate users from interacting with the contract or cause the contract to become unresponsive.

Increased transaction costs: Even if the loop completes successfully, a large number of iterations can result in high gas consumption, making transactions more expensive for users.

## Proof of Concept
Construct a Merkle tree with a small `preSize` and a large `postSize`.

The attacker calls the `verifyPrefixProof` function, providing the necessary input parameters, including the large `postSize` value.

The function enters the while loop and starts iterating until `size` reaches `postSize`.

Due to the large difference between `preSize` and `postSize`, the loop iterates many times, consuming a significant amount of gas.

If the gas limit is reached during the loop execution, the transaction will revert, potentially leading to a DoS condition.

Even if the loop completes successfully, the high gas consumption can make the transaction expensive and discourage users from interacting with the contract.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Impose a reasonable limit on the maximum allowed difference between `preSize` and `postSize` to prevent excessive loop iterations.

Implement a gas-efficient mechanism to verify the prefix proof, such as using a more optimized data structure or algorithm.

Consider breaking down the prefix proof verification into smaller chunks or batches to distribute the gas consumption across multiple transactions if necessary.
