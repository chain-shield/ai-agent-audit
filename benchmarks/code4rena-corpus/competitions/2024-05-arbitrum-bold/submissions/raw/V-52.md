# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The `MerkleTreeLib` library does not include any specific functionality for hashing leaves with claimable addresses. The vulnerability is present throughout the library, as there are no explicit mechanisms to include claimable addresses when hashing the leaves of the Merkle tree.

The absence of including claimable addresses when hashing the leaves of the Merkle tree can have significant consequences for the security and ownership of assets within the system. Attackers can exploit this vulnerability to claim assets that they are not entitled to, leading to unauthorized access and financial losses for rightful owners. The impact of this vulnerability includes:

Theft of assets, as attackers can construct valid proofs for leaves without including their own addresses and claim the associated assets.

Violation of ownership rights, as the system fails to establish a secure and verifiable link between the proofs and the rightful owners of the assets.

Loss of trust in the system, as users cannot rely on the claim process to protect their assets and ensure fair distribution.

Disruption of the protocol's functionality, as the system may face disputes and challenges regarding the ownership of assets.

The impact of missing claimable addresses in leaf hashing goes beyond just the immediate unauthorized access to assets. It undermines the fundamental purpose of using a Merkle tree for secure and verifiable asset distribution. Attackers can exploit this vulnerability to bypass the intended ownership verification and claim assets that rightfully belong to others.

## Proof of Concept
The attacker analyzes the Merkle tree implementation and identifies that claimable addresses are not included when hashing the leaves.

The attacker constructs a valid proof for a leaf in the Merkle tree that represents a valuable asset. However, instead of including their own address in the leaf data, the attacker uses an arbitrary or fake address.

The attacker submits the proof to the system, attempting to claim the asset associated with the leaf.

The system verifies the validity of the proof against the Merkle tree but does not check if the claimable address in the proof matches the address of the user making the claim.

Since the proof is valid and the system lacks proper verification of the claimable address, the attacker successfully claims the asset, even though they are not the rightful owner.

The rightful owner of the asset is unaware of the unauthorized claim and loses access to their asset.

The attacker can repeat this process with different proofs and leaves, claiming assets that belong to various rightful owners.

The system's integrity is compromised, and users lose trust in the claim process, as their assets are vulnerable to unauthorized access.

Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Ensure that the Merkle tree construction includes the hashing of claimable addresses within the leaves.
