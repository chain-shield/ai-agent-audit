# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The `RollupAdminLogic` contract has a `setOwner` function that allows the current owner to directly set a new owner without a confirmation step. While the code emits an event (`OwnerFunctionCalled`) when the owner is changed, it lacks a two-step confirmation process that would provide an extra layer of security against unintentional or malicious owner changes. Transfer of critical privileges must be done in two-step process. A two-step transfer process, usually involving a request followed by a confirmation, adds an extra layer of security against unintentional or malicious owner changes.

The lack of a two-step confirmation process for transferring critical privileges, such as contract ownership, can lead to several risks:

Accidental ownership changes: If the current owner mistakenly calls the `setOwner` function with an unintended address, the ownership will be transferred immediately without any opportunity to confirm or revert the action.

Malicious ownership changes: An attacker who gains access to the current owner's private key can exploit this vulnerability to transfer ownership to their own address, effectively taking control of the contract and its associated privileges.

Irreversible actions: Once the ownership is transferred, the new owner has full control over the contract, and any malicious actions performed by the new owner may be irreversible.

Loss of trust: If the ownership is transferred to an untrusted or malicious entity, it can erode the trust in the protocol and potentially lead to the loss of assets or damage to the protocol's reputation.

## Proof of Concept
The admin, who is the current contract owner, intends to transfer ownership to a trusted address (e.g., a multisig wallet or a dedicated admin account).

The admin prepares a transaction to call the `setOwner` function, but accidentally pastes or types in the wrong address as the `newOwner` parameter. This could happen due to human error, copying the wrong address, or a typo.

The admin signs and submits the transaction to the network without realizing the mistake.

Since there is no confirmation step or delay, the ownership of the contract is immediately transferred to the unintended address.

The admin realizes the mistake but is unable to revert the ownership change because the `setOwner` function does not have any mechanism to verify the correctness of the new owner address.

The unintended address, which could be an invalid, non-existent, or even malicious address, now has full control over the contract.

If the unintended address is a non-existent or invalid address, the contract may become permanently stuck without an owner, rendering it unable to perform any owner-only functions or accept a new owner.

If the unintended address belongs to a malicious actor, they can exploit the accidentally transferred ownership to perform unauthorized actions, such as changing critical protocol parameters, updating contract logic, or other exploits.

The original admin loses control of the contract and may need to resort to external measures, such as community voting or a hard fork, to regain control or mitigate the consequences of the accidental ownership transfer.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement a two-step transfer mechanism that requires the new actor to accept the transfer, ensuring better security and intentional ownership changes.
