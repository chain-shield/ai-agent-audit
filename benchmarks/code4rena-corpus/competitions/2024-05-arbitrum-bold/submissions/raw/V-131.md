# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The `initialize` function in the provided contract ensures that the contract is correctly initialized after deployment, particularly in the context of upgradeable contracts. However, it lacks proper implementation of the `initializer` modifier to prevent re-initialization attacks. Re-initialization can lead to severe vulnerabilities such as unauthorized access, inconsistent state, and potential loss of funds. Ensuring the initialize function is correctly protected is critical for maintaining the security and integrity of the contract.

## Proof of Concept

* The `bridge` property is crucial for the contract's operation. An attacker can re-initialize the contract, setting the bridge to a malicious address. This can lead to unauthorized control over the bridge functionality, enabling the attacker to intercept transactions, redirect funds, or disrupt normal operations.

* If the `initialize` function is called again and `isDelayBufferable` is true, an attacker can reconfigure the buffer settings with malicious values. This can disrupt the contract's operations or make it vulnerable to other attacks.

* Re-initializing the contract with malicious `maxTimeVariation_` parameters can compromise the contract’s timing constraints. This can lead to either overly permissive or restrictive time variations, disrupting the contract's intended behavior or creating security vulnerabilities.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
To mitigate the risk of re-initialization attacks, implement the `initializer` modifier from the `OpenZeppelin` library. This ensures that the initialize function can only be called once.
