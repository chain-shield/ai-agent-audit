# Vulnerability details

## Description

The `ERC20Handler` contract is designed to handle cross-chain ERC20 token settlements. It supports multiple settlement modes, including `MintBurn`, `LockUnlock`, `LockMint`, and `BurnUnlock`. The contract's behavior in these modes is defined in the [`cross_chain_erc20_settlement`](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-08-chakra/blob/abef77d95866f2fec93270491fc5abc9ab14f86d/cairo/handler/src/handler_erc20.cairo#L167-L232) and [`receive_cross_chain_msg`](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-08-chakra/blob/abef77d95866f2fec93270491fc5abc9ab14f86d/cairo/handler/src/handler_erc20.cairo#L108-L137) functions. However, there are inconsistencies in how tokens are handled across these modes, which can lead to significant issues.

### Summary of Functionalities
- **MintBurn Mode**: Tokens are transferred to the contract on the sending side and minted on the receiving side.
- **LockUnlock Mode**: Tokens are transferred to the contract on both the sending and receiving sides.
- **LockMint Mode**: Tokens are transferred to the contract on the sending side and minted on the receiving side.
- **BurnUnlock Mode**: Tokens are burned on the sending side and transferred on the receiving side.

### Root Cause
The root cause of the issue is the inconsistent handling of tokens across different settlement modes. Specifically:
- In `MintBurn` mode, tokens are transferred to the contract on the sending side but minted on the receiving side, leading to token inflation.
- In `BurnUnlock` mode, tokens are burned on the sending side, but the contract might not hold these tokens on the receiving side, leading to failed transfers.

### Highest Impact Scenario
1. **MintBurn Mode**: An attacker could continuously send tokens cross-chain, inflating the total supply.
2. **BurnUnlock Mode**: Cross-chain transfers might fail on the receiving end due to insufficient balance.

## Impact
The inconsistencies in token handling across different settlement modes can lead to significant issues, including token supply mismatches and potential fund losses. In `MintBurn` mode, an attacker could inflate the total token supply, undermining the token's value. In `BurnUnlock` mode, cross-chain transfers might fail, leading to loss of funds for users.

## Proof of Concept
1. **MintBurn Mode**:
   - User sends tokens in `MintBurn` mode.
   - Tokens are transferred to the contract on the sending side.
   - Tokens are minted on the receiving side.
   - Total token supply is inflated.

2. **BurnUnlock Mode**:
   - User sends tokens in `BurnUnlock` mode.
   - Tokens are burned on the sending side.
   - Contract does not hold sufficient tokens on the receiving side.
   - Cross-chain transfer fails.

## Tools Used
Manual review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
1. Implement proper token burning in `MintBurn` mode on the sending side.
2. Maintain separate token pools for each connected chain in `LockUnlock` mode.
3. Implement a mechanism to track and validate available tokens for unlocking in `BurnUnlock` and `LockUnlock` modes.
