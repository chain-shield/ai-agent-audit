# Vulnerability details

## Impact

Users may lose their funds permanently if an admin removes a handler on the destination chain while a cross-chain transaction is still pending, leading to an incomplete and failed bridging process.

## Proof of Concept

Consider the following Scenario:

1. Alice initiates a cross-chain transaction:

    * Alice wants to bridge 100 Chakra Tokens from Ethereum to BSC using the `BurnUnlock` mode.
    * She calls the `cross_chain_erc20_settlement` function, specifying the destination handler as `X` on the BSC chain from `Y` handler on the Ethereum chain.


2. The transaction remains pending in the Ethereum mempool.

3. While the transaction is pending, the protocol admin decides to remove handler `Y` on the BSC chain by using the `remove_handler` function.

4. When Alice's transaction is finally executed, 100 Chakra tokens are burned on Ethereum as part of the bridging process.

5. Upon receiving the cross-chain message, the BSC chain attempts to process the transfer.

6. Since handler `Y` has been removed from the BSC chain, the following check in `receive_cross_chain_msg` fails at (@->):


[Link to code](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-08-chakra/blob/main/solidity/handler/contracts/ChakraSettlementHandler.sol#L311)

7. **Result:** The transaction fails, the status is marked as `CrossChainMsgStatus.Failed`, and Alice's tokens are burned on Ethereum without receiving her 100 Chakra tokens on BSC.

This results in a permanent loss of Alice’s funds with no means to recover them.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

Instead of Checking Validity of Handler in the Destination chain, Check the validity of Handler at the Source chain itself if bridging is possible on that route or not.

This way, if the Chakra team ever needs to remove a handler, the removal will be initiated and checked on the source chain, ensuring no user transactions are processed for a handler that has already been removed.
