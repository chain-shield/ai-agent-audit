# Vulnerability details

Calling `_erc20_lock` instead of `_erc20_burn` in the `MintBurn` settlement mode introduces a significant vulnerability in the contract’s functionality. In this scenario, the intended behavior is to burn tokens on the source chain (to reduce the supply) and mint new tokens on the destination chain. However, calling `_erc20_lock` merely locks the tokens on the source chain rather than burning them, which can lead to double-spending vulnerabilities, inflated token supplies, and loss of user funds.
If tokens are locked instead of burned on the source chain, they remain in circulation, which can result in excess tokens being minted on the destination chain without properly reducing the token supply on the source chain. This could lead to severe economic imbalances across the token ecosystem, undermining the security and trust of the cross-chain settlement process.

## Proof of Concept
In the [`cross_chain_erc20_settlement`](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-08-chakra/blob/d0d45ae1d26ca1b87034e67180fac07ce9642fd9/solidity/handler/contracts/ChakraSettlementHandler.sol#L111-L223), the function call for `_erc20_burn` is incorrectly replaced with `_erc20_lock` under the `MintBurn` settlement mode:

In this case, instead of burning the tokens, the contract locks them, meaning the tokens are transferred to the contract's address but are still part of the circulating supply.
Risk Scenarios:
Double-Spending: Since tokens are not actually burned on the source chain, users could potentially spend the same tokens twice, once through the cross-chain transaction and again from their original balance.

Inflation: Tokens locked on the source chain would still exist, and minting new tokens on the destination chain would inflate the total supply, leading to unaccounted tokens in the ecosystem.

User Fund Loss: If cross-chain transactions are not properly settled due to this bug, users may lose their funds, as their locked tokens on the source chain would be inaccessible, and the newly minted tokens could become economically invalid.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Correct the implementation by replacing the incorrect `_erc20_lock` call with `_erc20_burn` to ensure that tokens are properly burned on the source chain.
