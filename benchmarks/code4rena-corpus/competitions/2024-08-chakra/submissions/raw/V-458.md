# Vulnerability details

## Impact

There are ERC20 tokens that have different decimals on Starknet and on BSC, such example is the USDC which has 18 decimals on BSC and 6 decimals on Starknet. Here are links

BSC USDC:
https://bscscan.com/token/0x8965349fb649a33a30cbfda057d8ec2c48abe2a2

Starknet USDC:
https://starkscan.co/token/0x053c91253bc9682c04929ca02ed00b3e423f6710d2ee7e0d5ebb06f3ecf368a8

The problem is that the protocol does not handle this which makes 2 scenarios possible. If the person is bridging from BSC to Starknet the protocol will try to give him enourmous amount of USDC and the transaction will fail or if the person is bridging from Starknet to BSC he will receive amount that is super small compared to what he had to receive.

## Proof of Concept

Let's explore the scenario where the user is bridging from Starknet to BSC and he will receive super small amount. The mode of the settlement will be LockUnlock to simplify the explanation but the fix has to be applied for all modes.

The problem is that the amount in the payload is with 6 decimals, meaning it will send the user 20e6 instead of 20e18 (BSC USDC has 18 decimals) so the user will receive much less than he should.

## Tools Used
Manual review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement a standardization function that makes the decimals of the amount 18 when the user deposits his tokens, no matter if the tokens have more or less decimals then when they are transfered to the destination chain before being sent just normalize the decimals that correspond to the correct ones in the current chain
