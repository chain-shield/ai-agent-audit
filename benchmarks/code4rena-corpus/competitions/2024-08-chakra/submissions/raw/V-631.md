# Vulnerability details

## Description:
In `ChakraSettlementHandler::receive_cross_chain_callback` function, if transaction went successful on source chain and destination chain and when callback functionality hits then even if a `from_handler` from `from_chain` is not whitelisted on source chain. The funds minted to `to` address on destination chain and not burned from source chain.

The vulnerability lies in the `receive_cross_chain_callback` function of `ChakraSettlementHandler`. The issue occurs when a cross-chain transaction is successful on both the source and destination chains, but the callback logic checks whether the `from_handler` from the `from_chain` is whitelisted. If the `from_handler` is not whitelisted, the protocol allows funds to be minted on the destination chain without burning the corresponding amount from the source chain. This breaks a key invariant of the protocol, which expects the funds on the source chain to be burned when they are minted on the destination chain, and leads to locking of funds on the source chain.

## Impact: 
Amount of funds minted successfuly on destination chain even if `from_handler` detected not whitelisted on source chain callback functionality, breaks Main invariant of protocol and locking of funds as funds on source chain not burned.

## Proof of Concept:
1. User initiates a cross-chain transfer of 100 tokens from SourceChain to DestinationChain. Both the source chain and destination chain contracts are configured, but the `from_handler` on SourceChain callback functionality is not whitelisted.
2. The transaction is marked as Success on the source chain. The source chain processes the transaction. The destination chain is already minted the corresponding amount for the user.
3. The callback is triggered on the source chain (`receive_cross_chain_callback`). The callback logic detects that the `from_handler` from destination chain is not whitelisted. However, the code does not revert or prevent the minting of tokens on the destination chain.
4. Despite the `from_handler` not being whitelisted, the destination chain successfully mints 100 tokens to the user’s address on DestinationChain.
5. On the source chain, the funds are not burned because the callback function detects that the `from_handler` is not whitelisted. The transaction is left in an inconsistent state, where the user’s funds are locked on the source chain, but they also have successfully minted tokens on the destination chain.
6. The user now has 100 tokens on the destination chain (minted), while the same amount is still locked on the source chain, breaking the protocol's invariant of burning tokens on the source chain when they are minted on the destination chain.

## Tool Used:
Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation: If the `from_handler` is not whitelisted, the transaction should revert on both chains or initiate a refund.
