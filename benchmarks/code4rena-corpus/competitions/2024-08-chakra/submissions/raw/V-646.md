# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The system relies on `cross-chain` functionality, but it lacks a critical `pause-unpause` mechanism, which is essential for maintaining security and stability during emergency situations. In the absence of this functionality, the system is exposed to increased risks, as it cannot be halted promptly in the face of potential exploits, bugs, or other unforeseen issues. This creates a scenario where the system could continue operating under malicious or faulty conditions, potentially leading to significant financial losses, cascading failures, or exploitability by attackers.

Cross-chain systems, particularly those that involve the transfer of assets or data across different chains, are inherently more complex and prone to a wide variety of threats, including bugs in the protocol, security vulnerabilities in underlying infrastructure, and external attack vectors. Without a mechanism to pause the system when such issues arise, there is no way to mitigate these threats in real time, making the system volatile in critical scenarios.

## Proof of Concept
In most cross-chain infrastructure, such as bridges and multi-chain systems, the implementation of a `pausability` feature is considered a best practice. This feature allows the system operators to temporarily halt specific operations or the entire system in response to threats, giving them time to analyze and address the issue without further damage. For example, if an exploit is detected that could drain assets or compromise security, administrators could pause the system to prevent further transactions until a fix is deployed.

  

Currently, the system lacks such a functionality. For instance, in cases where there is a need to suspend cross-chain settlements due to security concerns, administrators have no ability to pause these transactions. This omission leaves the system vulnerable to ongoing attacks or technical failures, increasing the chances of financial loss or network instability. The lack of a pausing mechanism severely limits the administrators’ capacity to respond to emergencies in real-time.
