# Vulnerability details

## Impact

The Chakra protocol is said to support all ERC20 tokens, some of which charge a fee for every `transfer()` or `transferFrom()`. However, with the current implementation in `ChakraSettlementHandler.sol` and `handler_erc20.cairo`, these tokens are not properly considered, which could lead to discrepancies between the chains and lead to potential losses.

## Proof of Concept

Whenever `cross_chain_erc20_settlement(...)` is invoked on either an L1 or an L2 chain, it transfers tokens from the user to the handler. However, when it creates the internal transaction bookkeeping and events it presumes that the transferred amount is always the same as the one the user sent. This is not the case for fee-on-transfer tokens.

We could follow the issue as follows:

1. Alice invokes `cross_chain_erc20_settlement` on L1, sending 100 tokens which charge for example 1% fee on each transfer.
2. The `ChakraSettlementHandler` will receive 99 tokens, but will create a `CreatedCrossChainTx`, an `ERC20TransferPayload`, a `Message`, and a `CrossChainLocked` event all with amounts of 100.
3. The L1 contract will also be invoked with a payload of 100 tokens as an amount.
4. In the L1 settlement the new cross-chain message `CrossChainMsg` event will be emitted with 100 as an amount.
5. On the L2 side the `settlement.cairo` contract will be invoked by a Chakra validator with an invalid payload of 100 tokens.
6. The L2 settlement contract will invoke the `handler_erc20.cairo` and invoke the `receive_cross_chain_msg` with 100 amount payload.
7. Depending on the handler mode it will either `mint` or `transfer` more tokens to the receiver, than what the Alice actually transferred in the L1 domain.


The same issue can be observed from L2 to L1 as well.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

Consider comparing before and after balances to get the actual transferred amount before creating cross-chain transactions and events.
