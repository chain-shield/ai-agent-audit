S-404
Excessive Constraints in `setMaxWhitelist` Function Result in Potential Loss of Functionality
y51r
00
Sufficient
383d ago
Finding description and impact

The setMaxWhitelist function in the contract is overly restrictive due to redundant require statements. Specifically, the condition _maxWhitelist > maxWhitelist imposes unnecessary constraints on reducing the maxWhitelist value.

The issue arises because:

The _maxWhitelist > totalWhitelist check is already sufficient to ensure the whitelist capacity cannot be set lower than the number of currently whitelisted users, preserving functionality.
The additional _maxWhitelist > maxWhitelist constraint prevents the lotOwner from reducing the maxWhitelist value after it has been increased.
If the lotOwner sets an excessively high value (e.g., uint256.max), they lose the ability to adjust the value downward, effectively locking this functionality.

Impact:

Denial of Functionality: The function becomes unusable for reducing the whitelist limit.
Reduced Flexibility: The protocol cannot dynamically adjust the whitelist capacity to meet changing requirements.
While this issue does not directly compromise security, it creates a usability issue that could impact the protocol's operational efficiency.
Proof of Concept

Steps to Reproduce:

Deploy the contract and initialize maxWhitelist to a small value (e.g., 10).
Call setMaxWhitelist with a higher value (e.g., 20) to increase the limit — this works as expected.
Attempt to reduce the maxWhitelist value by calling setMaxWhitelist with 15 — this fails due to the _maxWhitelist > maxWhitelist condition.
Recommended mitigation steps

To resolve this issue, remove the redundant require(_maxWhitelist > maxWhitelist) condition. This ensures the function remains flexible while maintaining security and operational requirements.
