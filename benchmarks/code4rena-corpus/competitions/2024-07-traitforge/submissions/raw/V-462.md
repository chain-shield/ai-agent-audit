# Vulnerability details

## Impact
In the `EntityForging` contract, when a buyer pays ETH for forging, the contract ensures `msg.value >= forgingFee`. However, if the amount of ETH paid exceeds the required `forgingFee`, the excess ETH is neither refunded nor distributed. Additionally, the contract lacks a withdrawal function to reclaim the excess ETH, causing these funds to be locked in the contract.

## Proof of Concept

Buyers pay `msg.value` of ETH, but the contract distributes `forgingFee` of ETH: 

```solidity
File: EntityForging.sol#forgeWithListed()
126:     require(msg.value >= forgingFee, 'Insufficient fee for forging');
...
146:     uint256 devFee = forgingFee / taxCut;
147:     uint256 forgerShare = forgingFee - devFee;
```

Excess ETH paid by buyers will remain locked in the contract. Over time, these locked funds can accumulate, leading to significant amounts of ETH being inaccessible.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
```diff
-    uint256 devFee = forgingFee / taxCut;
-    uint256 forgerShare = forgingFee - devFee;
+    uint256 devFee = msg.value / taxCut;
+    uint256 forgerShare = msg.value - devFee;
```
