# Vulnerability details

## Impact

The `Nuke` function allows users to burn their NFTs and receive up to 50% of the `NukeFund` contract balance. However, a malicious user can front-run large `Nuke` function calls by others, causing them to receive up to 50% less than expected.

## Proof of Concept

Consider a scenario with two users, Alice and Bob, both eligible to claim up to 50% of the `NukeFund` balance, which is 100 ETH (for simplicity):

1. Alice has been waiting for the balance to reach 100 ETH for long time! Once it does, she calls the `Nuke` function to burn her NFT and receive 50 ETH.
2. Bob is monitoring the situation and can also claim 50% of the balance but is not doing so as long as nobody else does, waiting for the balance to go up. He sees Alice's transaction in the mempool, calling `Nuke` to get 50 ETH.
3. Bob front-runs Alice's transaction, and since there's no slippage mechanism or delay between `Nuke` calls, both transactions proceed.
4. Bob nukes the 100 ETH balance and receives 50 ETH.
5. Alice nukes the remaining 50 ETH balance and receives 25 ETH, burning her NFT for 50% less than she intended.

## Tools Used

Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

Consider implementing a slippage mechanism where users specify a minimum amount of ETH they expect to receive from burning their NFT or minimum amount of ETH balance in the contract. Alternatively, introduce a delay between `Nuke` calls to prevent front-running.
