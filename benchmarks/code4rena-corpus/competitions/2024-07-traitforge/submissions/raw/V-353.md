# Vulnerability details

## Impact
Users could front-run others who nuke and claim most of their rewards, potentially making them burn their most valuable NFTs.

## Proof of Concept
[nuke](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-07-traitforge/blob/main/contracts/NukeFund/NukeFund.sol#L153-L182) is used to burn an NFT and claim a portion of the nuke fund. An NFT can claim up to 50% of the fund, where 25% comes from aging and 25% from entropy. High entropy and matured NFTs are highly valuable due to their claim potential.

However, users can spam nuke with a few low-value NFTs and claim 50% or more of the fund. This can be used to front-run other users who nuke high-value NFTs for a large reward, instead getting a small amount in return.

Example:
1. Alice has 1 merger and 1 forger NFT, both with a forging capacity of 9
2. Alice merges 9 times and generates 9 NFTs, each able to claim 15% of the fund with their entropy (the NFTs have `>= 600_000` entropy)
3. Bob has his god NFT with 999,999 entropy, fully matured, and able to claim 50% of the fund
4. Bob nukes his god NFT, aiming to claim 50% of a 10 ETH fund
5. Alice sees this transaction and front-runs Bob by nuking her 9 offspring NFTs
6. Alice claims 7.7 ETH (~77% of the fund)
7. Bob claims only 1.15 ETH (11.5% of the fund at the time of his transaction), wasting his NFT

This example illustrates how a user can sabotage another user by front-running and making their nuke attempt less effective. Note that forging is not required, but is used here to show how little it takes to claim most of the fund inside the honeypot.

The math used to calculate the claim rate for multiple nukes can be checked using [Desmos](https://www.desmos.com/calculator):
```markdown
y = 100 * (0.85)^x
100 * (0.85)^9 = 23.16 (that's what's left)
```

## Tools Used
Manual review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Add a slippage parameter to the nuke function, allowing users to revert their transaction if the generated funds from nuking are insufficient.
