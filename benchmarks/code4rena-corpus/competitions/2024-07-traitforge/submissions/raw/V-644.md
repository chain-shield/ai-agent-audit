# Vulnerability details

## Impact

The vulnerability allows excess ETH sent to the `forgeWithListed` function to become permanently stuck in the contract, leading to potential user fund loss. Since the function expects the sent-in ETH to be greater than or equal to the forging fee, any excess ETH will be lost as there is no refund or ETH recovery mechanism in place. This issue is particularly likely if the listed forging fee was modified right before the user's `forgeWithListed` call is executed.

## Proof of Concept

The root cause of the vulnerability is that the `forgeWithListed` function does not handle excess ETH correctly. 

- Found in contracts/EntityForging/EntityForging.sol at [Line 156-159](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-07-traitforge/blob/279b2887e3d38bc219a05d332cbcb0655b2dc644/contracts/EntityForging/EntityForging.sol#L156-L159)

Let us walk through the issue with the following scenario:
1. Alice lists her Forger token at a 1 ETH fee.
2. Bob sees a great Forger token which is a great combo for his Merger entropy and is willing to pay 1 ETH to forge with Alice's token. 
3. While Bob's transaction is pending, Alice impatiently reduces the forging fee to 0.5 ETH via `cancelListingForForging` and `listForForging` combo to attract faster forging.
4. Due to potential conditions such as a delay when catching the `CancelledListingForForging` event which makes the fee not up-to-date on the UI, a higher gas configuration of the Forger's fee update transaction could frontrun Bob's transaction or a block reorg which puts Alice's transaction in front of Bob's, Bob's `forgeWithListed` call would execute and pay 0.05 ETH to the nuke contract and 0.45 ETH to Alice as forging fee.
5. However, due to the non-refund issue, Bob's remaining 0.5 ETH got stuck in the `EntityForging` contract permanently.


## Tools Used

Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

Refund the remaining `msg.value - forgingFee` to `msg.sender` 

or 

strictly require `msg.value == forgingFee`.
