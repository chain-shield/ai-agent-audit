# Vulnerability details

## Impact
mintNewEntity() could be DOS'ed for an unknown amount of time. 
There is an edge case where mintNewEntity may be dos'ed because of the structure of the require and if statements in _mintInternal() and mintNewEntity().
In mintNewEntity the require:
```solidity
require(
      generationMintCounts[gen] < maxTokensPerGen,
      'Exceeds maxTokensPerGen'
    );
```
is at the start of the function.
What that means is that if we had a _mintInternal() call beforehand with 99999 generationMintCounts[gen],
here we would revert.

Why is that, shouldnt mintInternal() increment the generation if it has hit the max.
No because in mintInternal the incrementGeneration comes at the start, and then we increment the genCount.
Which leaves a window in which the forges are DOS'ed. 
Yes this fixes with a single mint of a token. But if there are no available whitelisted minters at the moment,
this could proceed for extended periods of time.

## Proof of Concept
Lets have whitelisted user Alice and merger Bob

1. Alice mints till 9999 genCount
2. Bob tries to merge (reverts)
3. If there is no one to mint this could extend to longer periods.

Impact - high (dos of functionality, it could be fixed with a single mint, although relies on whitelisted users) 
Likelihood - low
Overall - medium

## Tools Used
Manual review
## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Instead of require in mintNewEntity, use an if statement and increment generation. Or increment gen count in mintInternal before incrementGeneration check.
