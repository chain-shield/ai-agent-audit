# Vulnerability details

## Vulnerability details
The `EntityTrading` contract allows users to list NFTs for sale and takes a 10% tax on each sale to contribute to the `NukeFund`. However, if a user lists an NFT for a very low value (e.g., 19 wei or 59 wei), the calculation for the tax cut suffers from precision loss due to Solidity's integer division, resulting in the `NukeFund` receiving less than 10% or even zero contribution.


## Impact
Precision loss in the tax calculation can lead to the protocol not receiving the intended 10% contribution for low-value NFT sales. This reduces the funds available in the NukeFund and can be exploited by malicious users who list NFTs for very low prices to avoid paying the correct tax amount.

## Proof of Concept
EXAMPLES

Price = 19 wei, TaxCut = 10:

`NukeFund contribution: 19 / 10 = 1 wei` (should be 1.9 wei)
`NukeFund receives: 1 wei` (which is approximately 5.26%)
`User receives: 18 wei` (which is approximately 94.74%)

Price = 59 wei, TaxCut = 10:

`NukeFund contribution: 59 / 10 = 5 wei` (should be 5.9 wei)
`NukeFund receives: 5 wei` (which is approximately 8.47%)
`User receives: 54 wei` (which is approximately 91.53%)

## Tools Used

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
1. Implement a minimum listing price to ensure the tax cut calculation does not suffer from precision loss
2. Alternatively, round up the tax cut calculation to ensure the NukeFund receives at least the minimum expected amount.
3. Add a check to ensure the NukeFund receives the correct contribution, even for low-value sales. This can be done by ensuring the minimum tax amount is enforced
