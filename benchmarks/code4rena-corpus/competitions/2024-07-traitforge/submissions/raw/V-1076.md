# Vulnerability details

## Impact
Tokens with IDs that have zeros in the last two decimal places face major limitations: **Inability to Forge and Age**

Entropy plays a crucial role in the forging and nuking processes within the protocol. Specifically, `forgePotential` and `age` depend on the 5th and 6th decimal digits of entropy, respectively.

- The `forgePotential` variable determines forging effectiveness:
  ```javascript
  uint8 forgePotential = uint8((entropy / 10) % 10); // Extract the 5th digit from entropy
  ```
  If `forgePotential` is 0, the entity cannot forge. This means it cannot earn money with the `forger` role or forge new generation NFTs with the `merger` role. Essentially, the entity is unable to use the forging feature of the protocol at all.

- The `age` and `initialNukeFactor` variables affect the effectiveness of nuking:
  ```javascript
  uint256 performanceFactor = nftContract.getTokenEntropy(tokenId) % 10;
  ```
  If `age` is 0, the entity cannot age, which means it cannot increase the nuke amount it can receive. The entity will only receive a fixed percentage of funds from the NukeFund, depending entirely on its entropy. This severely limits their nuking potential, reducing their possible earnings from a maximum of 50% with aging to only 5% to 25% of the NukeFund based on entropy.

```javascript
    uint256 initialNukeFactor = entropy / 40; // calcualte initalNukeFactor based on entropy, 5 digits
```

Consequently, tokens with entropy ending in two zeros cannot be used effectively for forging or aging to maximize value. These tokens essentially represent a financial loss for users who have paid for them with the expectation of functional utility within the protocol. Instead, these tokens offer minimal real utility. Users may only be able to nuke with their `initialNukeFactor` when the NukeFund is sizable enough to recover their losses. Additionally, they may face challenges in nuking their tokens due to the low percentage earnings allowed by their entropy and market conditions.

As a result, players will be discouraged from purchasing tokens with poor entropy, and those who inadvertently mint such tokens will find it difficult to recover their losses.

The protocol anticipated very few instances of this issue. However, from the test we can conclude that tokens with entropies that end in two zeros (referred to as bad entropies) account for 450 to 500 positions out of every 10,000 per generation, representing 4.5% to 5% of the total tokens. Since entropies are fixed once generated, bad entropies will persist across all generations, even when considering different entropies produced through the forging process.

## Tools Used
Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
To address this issue, the entropy generation logic should be updated to ensure that `forgePotential` and `age` do not both have zeros simultaneously.
