# Vulnerability details

## Impact:
The EntityTrading contract allows users to list and purchase NFTs. However, the `BuyNft` function is susceptible to front-running attacks. Attackers can monitor pending transactions and use flash loans to preemptively buy listed NFTs before legitimate buyers' transactions are processed which could lead to the following:
1. Financial Losses for Users: Legitimate buyers may lose out on purchasing desired NFTs or end up paying inflated prices due to artificial market manipulation by attackers.
2. Market Manipulation: The exploit undermines fair market practices by allowing malicious actors to manipulate NFT listings and sales.
3. Erosion of Trust: Such vulnerabilities erode user trust in decentralized marketplaces, potentially reducing user engagement and participation.

## Proof of Concept:
Due to the sequential processing of transactions in a test environment, it is challenging to provide a coded Proof of Concept (PoC). Instead, i present a conceptual PoC outlining how this exploit can be executed:
Steps of Exploit:

1.Monitoring Pending Transactions: Attackers use off-chain bots/scripts to monitor the mempool for pending NFT purchase transactions.
2.Flash Loan Acquisition: Upon detecting a target transaction, attackers take out a flash loan from platforms like Aave.
3. Front-Running Purchase: Using higher gas fees, attackers ensure their transaction is mined before the legitimate buyer's transaction.
4.Immediate Resale: The purchased NFT is immediately resold at a higher price or relisted on the marketplace.
5.Repay Flash Loan: The attacker repays the flash loan within the same transaction block.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps:
1. Commit-Reveal Schemes:Implement commit-reveal mechanisms where buyers first commit their intent (hash) and reveal it later (actual bid), reducing front-running risk.
2. Time-Locked Transactions: Introduce time-locks between listing an NFT and allowing its purchase, preventing immediate exploitation by bots monitoring mempool activity.
