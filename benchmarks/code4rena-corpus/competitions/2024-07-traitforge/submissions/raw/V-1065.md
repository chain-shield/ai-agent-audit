# Vulnerability details

## Description 
The fallback `receive` function in the `NukeFund` contract is designed to receive ETH and update the `fund` balance. However, it does not restrict or validate the source of the funds being sent. This lack of restriction means that any user can send ETH directly to the contract, thereby altering the `fund` variable. This  variable is used in critical calculations, such as determining the `finalNukeFactor` and claim amounts during a nuke action. This functionality deviates from the intended behavior, which is to only accept funds from project-related activities such as minting, nuking, or forging. The ability for unauthorized users to send ETH directly could result in manipulation of the contract’s financial parameters, potentially leading to unfair advantages or exploitation.

## Impact
The ability for unauthorized users to send ETH directly to the `NukeFund` contract presents a high risk by:
- Compromising Financial Integrity: Altering the fund balance can distort calculations related to `finalNukeFactor` and claim amounts, leading to financial losses or exploitation.
- Undermining Fairness: It disrupts the intended financial mechanisms and fairness of the main process, allowing external manipulation of contract parameters.

## Proof of Concept
To illustrate this issue:
1. Deploy the `NukeFund` Contract: Set up the `NukeFund` contract on a test network.
2. Send ETH Directly: Use a wallet or an external contract to send ETH directly to the `NukeFund` contract.
3. Observe the Impact: Check how the direct ETH transfer affects the `fund` balance and observe any changes in the calculations related to `finalNukeFactor` and claim amounts.
This proof of concept demonstrates how unauthorized ETH transfers can alter the contract's financial parameters and disrupt its intended functionality.

Manual Review
## Recommended Mitigation Steps
1. Create a specific payable function in the `NukeFund` contract that is callable only by authorized project contracts for actions such as minting, nuking, or forging. Move the logic from the `receive()` function into this designated function.
2. Remove the `receive()` function to prevent unauthorized ETH transfers directly to the contract.
3. Ensure that the new payable function is protected by access control mechanisms to restrict who can call it.

By implementing these changes, the contract will only accept ETH through controlled and intended channels, ensuring accurate tracking of funds and maintaining the integrity of financial operations.
