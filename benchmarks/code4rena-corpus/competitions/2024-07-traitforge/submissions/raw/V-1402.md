# Vulnerability details

## Impact
Approximately 25% of tokens within a single generation have a `performanceFactor` of zero. This is significantly higher than the intended 10% as per the protocol’s intention. The excessive proportion of tokens with zero `performanceFactor` impacts the protocol in several ways:

Tokens with a zero `performanceFactor` cannot utilize the nuke function effectively. This means that a substantial portion of tokens cannot reach their maximum potential in terms of nuke rewards, contrary to the protocol's intended mechanics. Entropy plays a crucial role in the nuking processes within the protocol. Specifically, `performanceFactor` depend on the 6th decimal digits of entropy.

- The `performanceFactor` and `initialNukeFactor` variables affect the effectiveness of nuking:
  ```javascript
  uint256 performanceFactor = nftContract.getTokenEntropy(tokenId) % 10;
  ```
If `performanceFactor` is 0, the entity cannot age, which means it cannot increase the nuke amount it can receive. The entity will only receive a fixed percentage of funds from the NukeFund, depending entirely on its entropy. This severely limits their nuking potential, reducing their possible earnings from a maximum of 50% with aging to only 5% to 25% of the NukeFund based on entropy.

The presence of such a large percentage of tokens with zero `performanceFactor` skews the expected balance of token values. The protocol is designed to ensure that only a small fraction of tokens have zero `performanceFactor`, maintaining a fair distribution of rewards. The current situation results in a larger-than-expected portion of tokens being undervalued or ineffective.

The protocol anticipated 10% instances of this issue. However, tokens with zero `performanceFactor` account for 2400 to 2600 positions out of every 10,000 per generation, representing 25% of the total tokens. Since entropies are fixed once generated, entropies with zero `performanceFactor` will persist across all generations, even when considering different entropies produced through the forging process.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Seven percent of zero `performanceFactor` results from the calculation in the `getEntropy` function of the `EntropyGenerator` contract, which multiplies by 10 ** (6 - number of digits) because entropy slots typically have 77 or 76 digits. This means that 1 out of every 13 entropies will have 5 or 4 digits, which are multiplied by 100 or 10 to make them 6 digits long. Consequently, this results in the `performanceFactor` being zero for approximately 7% of all entropies.

Modifying this logic can significantly reduce the number of zero `performanceFactor` entropies.
