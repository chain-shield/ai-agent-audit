# Vulnerability details

## Impact
User funds can get stuck in the `EntityForging` contract

## Proof of Concept
Users can list their NFTs to allow others to merge his NFTs with theirs for a fee using `listForForging()`. Then, a different user can call `forgeWithListed()` to conduct the merging/forging. We have the following check there:
```solidity
require(msg.value >= forgingFee, 'Insufficient fee for forging');
```
Then, all of the operations occur, the NFTs get forged, etc. However, if the user has provided more `msg.value` than the required fee, it will get stuck in the contract as it doesn't refunded the excess as in many other contracts in this protocol.

Imagine the following scenario which can occur and will not be a user error:
1. Bob lists his NFT for forging for a fee of 0.1 ETH
2. A month passes but no one wants to forge with him
3. Bob assumes that the fee he has put up is too high and decides to lower it by cancelling the listing and setting up a new listing with a fee of 0.05 ETH
4. At the same time, Alice sees the listing and decides to forge her NFT with Bob's NFT, paying 0.1 ETH as the fee
5. Bob's transaction goes through, the fee changes and Alice accidentally pays 0.1 ETH when the fee was actually just updated to 0.05 ETH
6. The funds are stuck in the contract as the excess is not refunded, there is also no functionality to take out those funds manually

## Tools Used
Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Refund the excess amount of `msg.value` provided or use `==` instead of `>=`
