# Vulnerability details

## Summary
listForForging allows forger token owners to list their token and later marger token owner to forge with them and create a new token. When forger list his token, he sets a price that the merger should pay when forging with his token. A frontrunning bug allows a forger to change the price for forging and make the merger pay more than expected.

The forger owner can scam the merger who wants to forge with his token by frontrunning the merger transaction and updating his listing price.

## Proof of Concept
Owner of Forger token lists his token using EntityForging.sol::listForForging with a very low forgingFee , e.g 0.01ETH
Then he monitors the mempool to see who will call forgeWithListed with his token and how much ether is sent. Keep in mind that the merger most likely will sent more Ether, in order to cover the forgingFee, e.g 1 ETH.
The owner of  the Forger then frontruns the forgeWithListed  transaction and cancels the
listing by calling cancelListingForForging. Immediately after that, he lists his token again with listingFee of 1 ETH.
forgeWithListed is executed, but the Merger owner paid 1ETH instead of 0.01ETH.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
A possible solution is to implement a cooldown period after a listing is canceled in order to stop immediately listing the same token after it was canceled.
