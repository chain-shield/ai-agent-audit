# Vulnerability details

## Impact
User may loses ETH when executes `forgeWithListed`.

The cost of forge may decrease before `forgeWithListed` is executed, causing the user to pay a `msg.value` that exceeds the cost. `forgeWithListed` does not have a refund mechanism, resulting in the user losing the overpaid fees.
## Proof of Concept
In `forgeWithListed`, `forgingFee` will be obtained and `msg.value >= forgingFee` is required.

https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-07-traitforge/blob/main/contracts/EntityForging/EntityForging.sol#L125-L126
```solidity
    Listing memory _forgerListingInfo = listings[listedTokenIds[forgerTokenId]];

    uint256 forgingFee = _forgerListingInfo.fee;
    require(msg.value >= forgingFee, 'Insufficient fee for forging');
```

The `forgingFee` is not fixed, users can update the `forgingFee` by using the cancel+list.

The `forgingFee` is only related to `tokenId` and has nothing to do with `listId`, so cancel+list will not affect the acquisition of `forgingFee`.

Therefore, if the fee is reduced before `forgeWithListed` is executed, the `msg.value` paid by the user will exceed the fee. Here is a specific example:
1. Alice wants to execute `forgeWithListed(AToken,BToken)`, and the `forgingFee` for AToken is currently 1 ETH, so Alice pays 1 ETH to make the call.
2. Before Alice's `forgeWithListed` is executed, the owner of AToken reduces the `forgingFee` of AToken to 0.5 ETH through cancel+list.
3. When `forgeWithListed` is executed, it satisfies `msg.value >= forgingFee` and is successfully executed, but since there is no refund mechanism, the extra 0.5 ETH paid by Alice is not returned, resulting in a loss of 0.5 ETH for Alice.
