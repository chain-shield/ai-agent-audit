# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The pushing of `forgerShare` to the `forgerOwner` who is the owner of the entity that he has listed for forging. The ETH transferred is made via low-level `.call` that transfers ETH to the forgerOwner, which may invoke the `receive()` which can have a `revert()` to whole state changes made by the transaction.

This function itself is gas-intensive as it has many external calls to various functions for validation and state changes like `_resetForgingCountIfNeeded()`, and others which can cause some gas (lesser than ETH mainnet, as intended chain is BASE) to the merger.

Still by calling this function, they won't be able to forge with the listed entity despite willing to pay the fee for forging just because the merger has the control to make the call `revert`.

Similarly one more instance where the`seller` of the `entity` being listed for sale can always make the call `buyNFT()` revert when invoked.
The seller has the control to revert this `buyNFT()` call made by anyone, which should not be the case, as it being centralized to the seller to whom he wishes to sell his NFT. Although this shouldn't be the case, as any (user)address is free to buy the NFT if they are willing to pay the listed price.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement the `claimForgingFee(forgerTokenId)` that allows the `forgerOwner` to claim their fee. 
Also implement a mapping to keep track if the forger has claimed it, disallowing double-claims from the contract balance.

Following the [push-over-pull funds model](https://fravoll.github.io/solidity-patterns/pull_over_push.html).
