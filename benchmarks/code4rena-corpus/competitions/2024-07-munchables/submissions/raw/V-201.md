# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The code has a logic hole which can be exploited by the cohort of landlord that they only need to lock their fund at two key timepoints and then unlock it immediately after the `minDuration` has passed --- they do not have to keeping it locked but still can achieve the same effect on the yield as if their fund has been locked for the whole time being.

These two key timepoints are:
1. At the beginning timepoint when the renters stake their Munchables
2. At the timepoint of the renters having the action of farming the plots. The landlords can use bots to listen to their renter's pending trxns of `farmPlots` or `unstakeMunchable`, and front-run it with staking funds again.

In the POC section I'll explain why this cheating behavior can achieve the same effect as if the landlord's fund had been kept locked for the whole time being. 

Because this cheating scenario works generally, so every landlord can adopt it to unfairly get more yield then they should have been entitled to, making this impact one in the large scale. Hence it is an Medium severity.

## Proof of Concept
1. Assume that initially the landlord staked their fund normally, and at `t0` a renter came to stake a Munchable at the landlord's plotId of 0;
2. After the Munchable was staked, the landlord decided that instead keeping his fund locked, he'd unlock it immediately when it's allowed to(`minDuration` passed).  Assume that he unlocked it at `t1`;
3. Later at `t2`, the renter began to submit farming actions. The landlord's bot was listening to this and immediately front-run it with staking funds again. So, at `t2` when the farming action was executed, the `_getNumPlots(landlord)` went up again, leading to these results:
    * The renter's Munchable will NOT be marked as `dirty`;
    * The renter gets full yield for the whole time being as if the landlord's plot had always been active, therefore, the landlord will get his full tax yield as well. 
4. However, we can see that for the period between `t1` and `t2` the landlord actually had no locked fund, hence shouldn't have any yield for this period. They effectively cheated to get more yield than they should've been entitled to.
5. This cheating scenario can be easily amplified in the perspectives below:
    * ALL landlords can use this strategy, and everybody can cheat to get the `t1~t2` yield. In reality, depending on the cases of the renters' activity frequency, the `t1~t2` can potentially mean a significant amount of time.
    * My illustration above is using one plot as a simplified example, but in reality this can be amplified to multiple or all plots that a certain landlord may own, depending on the scale of fund they decided to cheat upon.

For the reasons above, the impact can be amplified to a quite large scale, which makes it no longer "_an edge case_" but rather a general case. The whole game's integrity in the design is ruined by this cheating strategy.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Move the relevant logic of labeling `dirty` and force to farm the yield of the impacted staked `tokenId`s into the logic of the landlord's `unstake` function, so that the related pieces get updated in real time and keep the game fair.
