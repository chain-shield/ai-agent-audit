# Vulnerability details

## Description
The LandManager contract sets a user's toiler state to dirty in case the landlord is not able to accommodate the plot id the user has been occupying.
This logic is determined by the amount of locked tokens held by the landlord. 
The issue with that is a plot can only become dirty in the context of the `_farmPlots` function. This function, however, is only accessible for one's own plot ids, meaning the message caller has to be a tokenId owner in order for its plot to get farmed.
The consequence of that is: in case a user detects his plotId is about to become dirty due to a landlord's unlocking of tokens, he/she can opt to avoid utilizing the LandManager contract until the landlord locks more tokens. Once this happens, the user can return to earn schnibbles - even the ones for the time period in which the landlord didn't have enough locked tokens to accommodate the plotId that was supposed to be dirty.
## Impact
The LandManager contract doesn't enforce plot's dirty status accurately and users are able to avoid losing rewards if they don't interact with the contract during the period in which their plots should be dirty.

The impact is further enhanced if we consider the landlord as a possible malicious actor: then he/she could coordinate multiple locks and unlocks to accrue rewards in a way that unfairly multiplies the amount of rewards earned.

A third impact is at the `_reconfigure` function: 
It updates the PRICE_PER_PLOT uint256 global state variable. Since this variable is utilized at the `_getNumPlots` function, it helps determine a toiler's dirty state. If a user doesn't call farmPlots often, then he/she is able to avoid the new configurations.
## Proof of Concept
**a.**
Alice is a landlord.
Bob stakes tokenId #111 to Alice's plot id #5.
Alice unlocks some tokens. Now Alice is only able to accomodate 3 plots.
Bob doesn't interact with the LandManager contract during this period.
Alice locks more tokens. Now Alice is able to accomodate 10 plots.
Bob farms its plot and earns for the whole period since its tokenId has been staked.

**b.**
Alice A. locks an amount that enables two plots to be open at her land.
Bob stakes tokenId #1 and #2. 
The unlock period passes. Alice A. unlocks this amount and sends to a sybil account of hers: Alice B..
Alice B. locks the same amount.
Bob stakes tokenId #3 and #4.
The unlock period passes. Alice B. unlocks this amount and sends to a sybil account of hers: Alice C..
Alice C. locks the same amount.
Bob stakes tokenId #5 and #6.
The unlock period passes. 40 weeks pass.
Alice C. transfers the amount to Alice A., who locks the tokens.
Bob now calls unstake for tokenId #1 and #2. He earns the rewards for the whole period in which the stake was going on.
The unlock period passes.
Alice A. transfers the amount to Alice B., who locks the tokens.
Bob now calls unstake for tokenId #3 and #4. He earns the rewards for the whole period in which the stake was going on.
The unlock period passes.
Alice B. transfers the amount to Alice C., who locks the tokens.
Bob now calls unstake for tokenId #5 and #6. He earns the rewards for the whole period in which the stake was going on.

Alice ended up having to lock 1/3 of the tokens she had given land plots to.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Consider introducing timestamp-based epochs/periods to determine a toiler state's dirty status. This mechanism is interesting because it creates precise time cuts when certain sets of users ought to stop receiving rewards.
This could be really interesting for the demonstrated edge-case as it has the ability of introducing a more granular approach to a landlord's capacity and the distribution of rewards.
