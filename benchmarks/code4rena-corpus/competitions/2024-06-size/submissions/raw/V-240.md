# Vulnerability details


## Proof of Concept

Take a look at https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-06-size/blob/8850e25fb088898e9cf86f9be1c401ad155bea86/src/oracle/PriceFeed.sol#L29-L33

```solidity
    AggregatorV3Interface public immutable base;
    AggregatorV3Interface public immutable quote;
    AggregatorV3Interface internal immutable sequencerUptimeFeed;
    uint256 public immutable baseStalePriceInterval;
    uint256 public immutable quoteStalePriceInterval;
```

These are all attributes attached to the functionality of querying the prices from: https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-06-size/blob/8850e25fb088898e9cf86f9be1c401ad155bea86/src/oracle/PriceFeed.sol#L63-L94, issue however is that both the addresses and the intervals have been marked as immutable with no functionality to update them.

Now, chainlink price feeds are not immutable as they can change, so is the `interval` for the heartbeats attached to them, the feed addresses can either be being taken down or being updated to a new address which is one of the reasons why [Chainlink advertises the use of their registry contract](https://blog.chain.link/introducing-the-chainlink-feed-registry/) for the feeds.
When this occurs or if the price feed gets buggy, changing the pricefeed will be impossible, breaking the protocol's functionality and will force redeployment of the contract which doesn't come cheap.

In our case, this then means that in the case where any of the feed address has been changed, the pricing functionality of Size would completely be broken as it would become inaccessible.

Naturally, we would expect the admins in this case to set the value of both `baseStalePriceInterval` & `quoteStalePriceInterval` to be around the current heartbeat of the feed, since these intervals are being used to ensure the price ingested is not stale here: https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-06-size/blob/8850e25fb088898e9cf86f9be1c401ad155bea86/src/oracle/PriceFeed.sol#L89-L91

```solidity
        if (block.timestamp - updatedAt > stalePriceInterval) {
            revert Errors.STALE_PRICE(address(aggregator), updatedAt);
        }
```

This then means that in the case the heartbeat of an integrated Chainlink feed is updated, this could lead to two issues:

- If new heartbeat < old heartbeat, protocol would be forced to ingest stale prices, since the accepted stale check would now be erroneously elongated.
- If new heartbeat > old heartbeat, protocol would be DOS'd from getting prices, i.e assume heartbeat goes from 1 hour to 2 hours, a price updated 80 minutes ago, whereas valid [would always revert in this line]() when the pricing functionality is queried within size, which means with the example above (1 hour -> 2 hours), we have ~ 50% DOS throughout the life time of protocol henceforth.

## Impact

As hinted under _Proof of Concept_ this could lead to multiple issues.

- Ingestion of stale if the heartbeat of the feed gets changed to a lower one (How stale the price would be depends on the change).
- Consistent partial DOS if the heartbeat of the feed gets changed to a higher one (Duration of DOS periodically depends on the change).
- And even worse a permanent DOS if any of the underlying feeds get changed.

Would be key to note that this DOS aren't just applicable to the pricing functionalities, but would also affect all other functionalities that directly query this, i.e liquidations et all, for liquidations, this would mean thats bad position would be able to accumulate more debt, considering attempts on their liquidations would always revert.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

Consider integrating a functionality that could update any of these addresses/interval values.
