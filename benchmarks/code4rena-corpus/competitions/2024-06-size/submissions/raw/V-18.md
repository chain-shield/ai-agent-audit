# [M1] Title: **Inconsistent Intermediate Asset Check in PriceFeed Constructor Leading to Incorrect Price Calculations**
***Description:***
The constructor of the PriceFeed contract does not check whether the _base and _quote price feeds use the same intermediate asset. This oversight can lead to the calculation of incorrect price ratios because the contract may compare prices denominated in different currencies.

**Impact:**
Incorrect Price Calculations: Without verifying that both oracles use the same intermediate asset, the contract may derive an incorrect price ratio. For instance, if the base oracle uses USD and the quote oracle uses EUR, the price ratio calculation will be meaningless, leading to flawed financial decisions and valuations.

Inconsistent Price Data: The prices fetched from the base and quote oracles may be inconsistent if they reference different intermediate assets. This inconsistency can undermine the reliability of the price feed data.

Increased Risk of Arbitrage and Manipulation: Discrepancies caused by differing intermediate assets can be exploited by arbitrageurs, potentially leading to significant financial losses for platforms relying on these price feeds.


**Proof of Concept:**
Consider the following scenario:

base oracle reports prices in USD.
quote oracle reports prices in EUR.
If the contract attempts to calculate a price ratio between these two oracles, the resulting ratio will not accurately reflect the true market value, as the prices are denominated in different currencies.

**Recommended Mitigation:**
Add a check in the constructor to verify that both the base and quote price feeds use the same intermediate asset. This can be done by fetching a known price from each oracle and ensuring that both prices are consistent with a known reference price. Here’s an example of how this check could be implemented:
