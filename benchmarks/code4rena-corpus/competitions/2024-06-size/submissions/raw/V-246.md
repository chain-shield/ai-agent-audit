# Vulnerability details

## Impact
An early repay by the borrower can be front ran, by buying the credit position from the lender, then letting the repay execute, and than withdraw right away.
This will be profitable for the attacker and will cause losses to the lender (which should be entitled to the full future value of the trade, since they were exposed to the risk of the debt up to the repay time).

Note: although there are fees applied on every credit swap, this fees are applied on the receiver of the tokens and not on the caller of the function. Meaning that, not only the fee won't represent a cost for the MEV, but they will instead appear as losses to the original lender, making things worst if anything. 

## Proof of Concept
The [buyCreditMarket](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-06-size/blob/8850e25fb088898e9cf86f9be1c401ad155bea86/src/Size.sol#L178C14-L178C29) function enables trades to buy an existing credit position from a lender, the apr of the credit being bought will be computed based on the curve specified by the lender selling the position, and the tenor will be de difference between the current time and the debt expiration date:

With `MaxCredit` being equal to `creditPosition.credit` (as can be observed in the above snippets of code), and with `ratePerTenor` being always positive (as it is a uint), this will imply that `cashAmountIn` is either smaller or equal to the `creditPosition.credit`. Or in simpler terms, this means that the amount the buyer has to pay is equal or smaller than the credit they are buying (Aka they won't lose money on the trade, and that very often it will be profitable to do so).

As a last piece, note that the fees are being subtracted to the amount that will be sent to the lender, meaning that they won't represent a cost to the buyer, but a cost to the passive seller:

```
        state.data.borrowAToken.transferFrom(msg.sender, borrower, cashAmountIn - fees);
        state.data.borrowAToken.transferFrom(msg.sender, state.feeConfig.feeRecipient, fees);
```
This means that if, for any reasons the borrowers decide to repay the debt early, a MEV bot can front run the transaction by buying the credit corresponding to it, getting a revenue corresponding to the difference between the debt future value which is being repaid (as they get the full credit) and the cash `amountIn` paid for the transaction. While providing no contribution or value to either the borrower or the lender (since the lender would have been paid the full amount anyway) 


Example (for simplicity, lets assume the Alice buys and sells the credit with the same curve):

Alice offers to buy credit (loan) with a linear growth of 1% per day
Bob sees the offers and borrows 100 tokens for 10 days:
(future value: 110)
(credit: 110)

After 5 days Bob wants to get back his collateral and decides to repay the debt

Eve sees this and front run the the repay by buying the full credit from Alice:
Since 5 days have passed the rate per tenor will be of 5%, with a full credit of 110, Eve will have to pay around 104.76 tokens to Alice (Ignoring fees, as they will be applied to Alice's side of the deal)

After Bob's repay get executed, the situation will be the following:
Alice gets: 104.76 - swap fees, token, instead of the 110 that are being deposited by bob
Eve gets: 5.24 tokens of revenue (110 - 104.76).

Since Bob was repaying his debt, the whole future value should have gone to Alice, and not just a portion of it. Implying that Eve stole profits from her.

Although this will result in a theft of the yield belonging to the lender, there are some limitations to it:
-The credit must be for sale
-The difference in time between the repay and the due date of the debt, must be grater than minTenor parameter of the protocol.
