# Vulnerability details

## Impact
Through the `Compensate.sol` library any borrower is capable of splitting their debt positions to a large number of smaller positions at no significant cost. As the fragmentation fee is wrongly not charged on every compensation, it may be extremely profitable for a borrower to split their debt position right before going under the liquidation threshold. This will not only delay their position from being liquidated but even worse it may even prevent its complete liquidation.

## Proof of Concept
Currently in the `Compensate.sol` library, the fragmentation fee can only be charged when another credit position, owned by the borrower, is used for the compensation. If `RESERVED_ID` is passed as `CompensateParams.creditPositionToCompensateId` than it is practically impossible for the fragmentation fee to be charged.
This assumption, however, is wrong and highly dangerous.

Why is the assumption wrong? Even though a separate credit position is not used to compensate for the loan, the `creditPositionWithDebtToRepay`, is also a credit position that is getting split into separate credit positions when partially repaid through `Compensate.sol`. According to the documentation, in particular, invariant `FEES_01: Fragmentation fees are applied whenever there is a credit fractionalization`. Therefore, when a credit position is partially repaid through `Compensate.sol` the fragmentation fee MUST be charged, regardless if a different credit position, owned by the borrower is used for the compensation.

Why is not charging the fragmentation fee dangerous? If borrowers are able to split their debt position freely to numerous smaller positions by passing `RESERVED_ID` in the `executeCompensate` function, they would be able to fractionalize their debt right before going under the liquidation threshold. This would delay the full liquidation significantly, increasing the possibility of their position becoming insolvent before it may be fully liquidated.

For example:

1/ a borrower has a debt position with a future value of 100,000$

2/ right before going under the liquidation threshold, they split their position through `Compensate` to 10,000 positions each worth 10$, as there is no fragmentation fee, all they need to pay for is gas, which is much less expensive on the Base chain

3/ in order for the 100,000$ to be fully liquidated 10,000, liquidate calls need to be made by liquidators, in order for the full amount to be liquidated. It is important to note that when more positions need to be liquidated more gas needs to be paid by liquidators and the liquidations would be less profitable

4/ thereby, the borrower is likely to delay and maybe even prevent the complete liquidation of their loan, further increasing the chances of their position becoming insolvent and bad debt accruing in the protocol

## Tools Used
Manual review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Charge the fragmentation fee on every compensation.
