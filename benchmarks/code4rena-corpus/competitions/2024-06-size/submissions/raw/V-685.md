# Vulnerability details


## Summary

Whales, or accounts with substantial cryptocurrency holdings, can exploit block stuffing to force positions into overdue status, triggering liquidation and benefiting at the expense of the borrower. This risk increased with the current low gas fees on the mainnet.

## Description

Whales can manipulate the `dueDate` mechanism by submitting a high volume of transactions, known as block stuffing. The `dueDate` is a timestamp that marks when a position becomes overdue and eligible for liquidation. Once overdue, the protocol allows liquidators to purchase the collateral at a discount. By block stuffing, whales can ensure positions become overdue, thereby triggering liquidation and allowing them to buy the collateral at a reduced price.

### Cost Analysis

- Current gas fee: 5 gwei
- Block time: 12 seconds
- Block gas limit: 30M gas
- Cost to stuff one block: 30M * 4 gwei = 120M gwei = 0.12 ETH = $409*

### Example Scenario

If the due date is in 10 minutes, approximately 50 blocks will be mined. The cost to block stuff for 10 minutes is:

- 50 blocks * $409/block = $20,450

A whale would benefit if the liquidation reward exceeds the block stuffing cost. For any borrow worth more than $409,000, the 5% liquidation reward ($20,450) makes the attack profitable.

### Worst Case Scenario

- Duration: 1 hour (300 blocks)
- Total cost: 300 blocks * $409/block = $122,700

For a borrow of $2.6 million USDC, the 5% liquidation reward ($130,000 USDC) exceeds the block stuffing cost, making the attack worthwhile.

Currently, such scenarios are less likely due to existing borrow caps, but they become more feasible if the borrow cap is increased.

## Impact

Short-term borrows are particularly vulnerable, as the cost to execute block stuffing is relatively low. This attack vector could become more prevalent as gas fees on the mainnet decrease.

## Recommendation

Given that the `dueDate` mechanism is integral to the protocol, an immediate technical fix is challenging. It is recommended to document this potential attack and ensure the development team is aware of this vulnerability. Enhanced monitoring and possibly adjusting protocol parameters to mitigate risk are advisable.
