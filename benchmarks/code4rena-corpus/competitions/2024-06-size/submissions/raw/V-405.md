# Vulnerability details

## Impact
There are a few ways in which lenders can capitalize and receive significantly more borrow token than they're entitled to after a loan was paid off:

- `claim()` wasn't called for a certain amount of time after it became available and allowed for a significant index change OR it wasn't sanitized frequently enough by bots.
- In a situation where the borrower and lender are the same person, a significant liquidity index change was observed, and the change was "sandwiched" in order to capitalize on it.

## Proof of Concept
Due to the way the claim ratio of the borrow token is calculated for lenders which are eligible to claim it, there are a few ways in which lenders can capitalize on it unfairly:

```
function executeClaim(State storage state, ClaimParams calldata params) external {
        CreditPosition storage creditPosition = state.getCreditPosition(params.creditPositionId);
        DebtPosition storage debtPosition = state.getDebtPositionByCreditPositionId(params.creditPositionId);

        uint256 claimAmount = Math.mulDivDown(
            creditPosition.credit, state.data.borrowAToken.liquidityIndex(), debtPosition.liquidityIndexAtRepayment
        );
        state.reduceCredit(params.creditPositionId, creditPosition.credit);
        state.data.borrowAToken.transferFrom(address(this), creditPosition.lender, claimAmount);

        emit Events.Claim(params.creditPositionId, creditPosition.debtPositionId);
    }
```

- A lender, purposefully can delay the claiming of their funds in order to capitalize on more borrow token that they're entitled to. For example, let's say that the `liquidityIndex()` when the loan was paid of was 1.05, the borrowToken amount wasn't claimed, and the function wasn't sanitized by bots, so we've observed a `liquidityIndex()` increase to 1.10.

- If the original amount that the borrower should've claimed was 50,000 USDC (according to whenever the loan was paid off), right now they'll be entitled to:
- 50,000 * 1.10 / 1.05 = 52,380 USD
- Its also important to note that when withdrawing their `borrowToken` they'll also capitalize once more on the increased liquidity index amount from the time that they've deposited as well.

There is another situation in which this can purposefully be "sandwiched" if there is a significant change in the liquidity index. If the borrower and the lender are the same person or are "in collusion", the borrower can repay the loan before the liquidity index change takes place and then claim the "increased" amount afterwards, which would make the `liquidityIndexAtRepayment` lower than the actual current liquidity index, capitalizing on this event.

This will allow the lenders to capitalize on an "outdated" liquidity index while at the same time capitalizing again if the index has increased from the time that they've deposited the borrow token to the time that they're withdrawing it after the claim.

The "double-capitalization" on the liquidity index changes:
- Bob deposits 10,000 USDC to the protocol which mints 10,000 aUSDC to the protocol and 10,000 szaUSDC to Bob. (Liquidity Index: 1.00)
- Bob has lent the money and after some time capitalized on the liquidity index change explained above, so instead of receiving 10,000 szaUSDC after the loan is paid off(interest won't be accounted, for a simpler example), Bob has capitalized upon a liquidity index change difference (between repay and claim) and received 10,750 szaUSDC.
- Since now the liquidity index is 1.075, when Bob withdraws his USDC, he will receive 11,556 USDC instead of 10,750.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Don't allow for differences between the liquidity indexes to occur when claiming.
