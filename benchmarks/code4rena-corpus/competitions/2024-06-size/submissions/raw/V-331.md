# Vulnerability details

## Impact
User funds could get stuck or unexpected reverts could occur

## Proof of Concept
Upon calling some functions like `buyCreditMarket()` and `sellCreditMarket()` (used for borrowing and lending), there is the following check:
```solidity
state.validateVariablePoolHasEnoughLiquidity(amount);
```
```solidity
function validateVariablePoolHasEnoughLiquidity(State storage state, uint256 amount) public view {
uint256 liquidity = state.data.underlyingBorrowToken.balanceOf(address(state.data.variablePool));
        if (liquidity < amount) {
            revert Errors.NOT_ENOUGH_BORROW_ATOKEN_LIQUIDITY(liquidity, amount);
        }
}
```
It checks the liquidity of an Aave pool and reverts if there is not enough liquidity in it as that means the user who receives that amount of money won't be able to withdraw at that time. The issue is that the tokens (cash) transferred to the borrower are not necessarily equal to the `amount` parameter. Here are the tokens transferred to the borrower and the fees transferred to the protocol in `sellCreditMarket()` (`cashAmountOut` is the returned `amount` parameter):
```solidity
state.data.borrowAToken.transferFrom(params.lender, msg.sender, cashAmountOut);
state.data.borrowAToken.transferFrom(params.lender, state.feeConfig.feeRecipient, fees);
```
And here are the tokens transferred to the borrower and the fees transferred to the protocol in `buyCreditMarket()` (`cashAmountIn` is the returned `amount` parameter):
```solidity
state.data.borrowAToken.transferFrom(msg.sender, borrower, cashAmountIn - fees);
state.data.borrowAToken.transferFrom(msg.sender, state.feeConfig.feeRecipient, fees);
```
As seen, in `sellCreditMarket()` the borrower receives an amount equal to the `amount` parameter while in `buyCreditMarket()`, the borrower receives an amount equal to `amount - fees`. Essentially, the returned `amount` parameter in `sellCreditMarket()` does not include the fees while in `buyCreditMarket()`, it includes them.

This creates a few possible issues:
1. There are 100 tokens as liquidity in Aave and a user borrows 100 tokens using `sellCreditMarket()` and the protocol gets 10 tokens as fees. The check passes, the protocol withdraws their tokens and now the user can't withdraw his tokens as now there are only 90 tokens in liquidity
2. There are 100 tokens as liquidity in Aave, a lender lends out 100 tokens to a borrower (`cashAmountIn` is 110 and fees are 10 so 110 - 10) and the protocol receives 10 tokens as fees. This check fails as the `amount` parameter (`cashAmountIn` which is 110) is larger than the liquidity in Aave (100)

I can't be a 100% certain which one of those is the intended design but one of those 2 explained scenarios is definitely an issue as they contradict each other. In the first scenario, the borrower gets 100 tokens, the liquidity is 100 and the fees are 10 and the check is successful while in the second scenario, the borrower again gets 100 tokens, the liquidity is 100 tokens again and the fees are 10 again but this time, the check fails.
## Tools Used
Manual Review
## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Decide which option is your intended design and stick to it. Either include the fees in the `amount` parameter in both cases or exclude them in both cases.
