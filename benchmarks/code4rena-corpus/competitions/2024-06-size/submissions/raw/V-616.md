# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The `Liquidate:executeLiquidateWithReplacement` mint new tokens(USDC) and transfer to a new borrower without a provided step for burning out the token of the previous user(borrower), this can cause incorrect accounting within the system and possibly stealing of funds by a malicious operator


## Proof of Concept
Assuming Malicious user (Max) becomes the new borrower of a defaulted loan without adjusting the total supply of the stablecoin (USDC), here's how Malicious Max might exploit this:

Identify Defaulted Loans: Malicious Max monitors the platform for defaulted loans where the borrower has not been replaced.
Act as the New Borrower: Upon identifying suitable defaulted loans, Malicious Max applies to become the new borrower. Given the protocol's oversight, Malicious Max is approved and becomes the new borrower without the protocol adjusting the total supply of USDC.
Manipulate Market: Knowing the total supply of USDC has increased due to the protocol's oversight, Malicious Max decides to manipulate the market. They could sell a significant portion of the newly minted USDC on a decentralized exchange (DEX) or directly to unsuspecting users, aiming to drive down the price of USDC.
Profit from Price Drop: As the price of USDC drops, Malicious Max buys back the USDC they previously sold at a much lower price, profiting from the price manipulation.
Repeat the Exploit: Malicious Max repeats this process with other defaulted loans, continuously profiting from the price instability caused by the unburned USDC tokens.
Potential Impacts
Financial Loss for Users: Users who lent USDC to the platform could suffer financial losses if the protocol's reserves deplete due to the exploit.
Market Volatility: The manipulation of USDC's price could lead to significant market volatility, affecting other DeFi protocols that interact with USDC.
Reputation Damage: Such an exploit could severely damage the reputation of the platform, leading to a loss of confidence among users.

## Tools Used
Manual Review
## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Automatically Burning USDC: When a borrower defaults and is replaced, the protocol should automatically burn an equivalent amount of USDC from the old borrower's balance to offset the minting of new tokens for the new borrower. A line of code carrying out this process should be added to the code before updating debt position for the new borrower
