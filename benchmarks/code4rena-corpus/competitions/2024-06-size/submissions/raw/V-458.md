# Vulnerability details

## Impact

insufficient borrower protection in terms of borrowed amount cap, meaning borrowers can end up in more debt than expected.


## Proof of Concept

LoanOffers are capped by the lender's borrowAtoken(which is basically a rebasing wrapper around usdc), BorrowOffers on the other hand are capped by the borrowers collateral ratio(ETH/USDC), as we'll see even though this value can be adjusted by the borrower, it is still not an adequate way to cap the borrowed amount due to its volatility.
Consider the following case: 
Alice has 1ETH (for simplicity 1ETH = 1500USDC) and would like to borrow 1000USDC. She places a sellCreditLimitOrder ,since her Cr is equal to the min. opening Cr(1.5) her borrowOffer is filled and she gets ~990USDC (after rate and fees). However if the price of ETH increases to say 1800USDC before alice is able to cancel the borrow offer or adjust her opening Cr, her Cr is now 1.8 and an addidional 200USDC in credit can be bought from her.
Even though real world price spikes may not be as sudden or as large as in this case, this is merely to prove a point.

```solidity
//test/local/actions/BuyCreditMarket.t.sol

    function test_cr_bad_cap() public {
        _setPrice(1500e18);//1500ETH/USDC

        //Alice deposits 1 ETH, her opening cr is 1.5(default). she's expecting a max 1000USDC in debt
        _deposit(alice, weth, 1e18);
        _sellCreditLimit(alice, 0.03e18, 365 days);

        //Bob fills the order
        _deposit(bob, usdc, 1000e6);
        _buyCreditMarket(bob, alice, 1000e6, 365 days, false);

        _setPrice(1800e18);//price changes

        //James can now buy extra 200USDC in credit increasing Alice's debt to 1200USDC
        _deposit(james, usdc, 200e6);
        _buyCreditMarket(james, alice, 200e6, 365 days, false);
    }
```
