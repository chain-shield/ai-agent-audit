# Vulnerability details

## Impact

Unwilling users can be forced to borrow unwithdrawable USDC, which defeats the whole purpose of borrowing.And made to pay interests on it plus exposing him to borrow risks like overDue or underwater.

Useless as in not convertible to USDC, for borrowers who wants USDC. 



## Proof of Concept
As mentioned in the [known issues](https://code4rena.com/audits/2024-06-size#top:~:text=If%20the%20Variable%20Pool%20(Aave%20v3)%20fails%20to%20supply%20or%20withdraw%20for%20any%20reason%2C%20such%20as%20supply%20caps%2C%20Size%27s%20deposit%20and%20withdraw%20may%20be%20prevented) and the [comments](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-06-size/blob/8850e25fb088898e9cf86f9be1c401ad155bea86/src/libraries/CapsLibrary.sol#L60C3-L64C58), ```szaUSDC``` cannot be withdrawn to ```USDC``` incase the liquidity pool is paused, and this is a know risk that should be taken by users who are willing to hold it. 

This means that borrowers who wants to borrow for the sake of withdrawing USDC has to clear their borrowOffer incase variable pool becomes inactive or paused.
But these users can be frontrunned by malicious borrowAToken holders to let them borrow unwithdrawable USDC.  


Example:
* Bob wants to borrow 1000 USDC, so he puts up a borrowOffer and collateral
* Bob's offer is not yet matched
* variable pool suddenly becomes unavailable(inactive, paused, frozen).
* Bob doesn't want to borrow anymore since he wont be able to withdraw USDC, so its useless to him.
* So Bob decides to clear his offer or withdraw his collateral.
* Alice has 1000 borrowATokens, since the token is currently worthless alice wants to get rid of it 
* Alice frontruns bob, by calling lending him 1000 borrowATokens
* Bob's collateral is now locked, Bob also cannot repay because 1000 is not enough to repay the futureValue which includes the interest. 
* Bob can only repay or withdraw when the pool is active.
* Alice sucessfully got rid of borrowATokens and by the time Bob repays the pool is available and borrowATokens becomes worth again.  

Since Alice had been holding the borrowATokens and earning interest way before the pool was paused, Alice should be the one taking the risk, not Bob who enters post unavailabilty of the pool. 
i.e. a user who has been in the protocol since the variable pool was active can transfer his risk to a new user who is just entering, post pool inactivity. 
 

And since borrowAToken becomes worthless, and when the pool will become available again is unknown, there is a very high chance for borrowATokens holders to perform this attack.




*There can also be another attack path where the attacker directly frontruns the aave's call to pause or freeze the pool, instead of the borrower's transaction. The same impact can be achieved this way.*

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
implement a boolean ```allowDuringPoolInactivity``` variable in the [```BorrowOffer```](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-06-size/blob/8850e25fb088898e9cf86f9be1c401ad155bea86/src/libraries/OfferLibrary.sol#L17C1-L21C2) so that a user can choose to accept borrowing even when pool is unavailable or choose to not accept the borrowATokens when pool is unavailable. 
And whenever a borrow market is to be executed during unavailable pool, check this variable and execute the borrow if true else revert. 


The second attack path can be completely avoided if aave decides to do update pool calls through flashbots, but still the first one exist.
And if aave doesn't use flash bots, then there is no immediate mitigation but size protocol could find a way to closely work with aave team so that they get notified off-chain atleast 5 minutes before pool gets paused/frozen. That way size can pause borrows through flashbots and unpause them after 5 minutes.
