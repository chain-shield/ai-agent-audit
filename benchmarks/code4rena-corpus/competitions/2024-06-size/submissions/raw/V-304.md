# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The current implementation of the fragmentation fee in the `executeCompensate` function could lead to unfair fee distribution and potential exploitation. Users could avoid paying fees or pay disproportionate fees.

## Proof of Concept
Let's look at the way the fragmentation fee is calculated and charged when a credit position is partially compensated: https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-06-size/blob/8850e25fb088898e9cf86f9be1c401ad155bea86/src/libraries/actions/Compensate.sol#L146-L155

```solidity
if (exiterCreditRemaining > 0) {
    // charge the fragmentation fee in collateral tokens, capped by the user balance
    uint256 fragmentationFeeInCollateral = Math.min(
        state.debtTokenAmountToCollateralTokenAmount(state.feeConfig.fragmentationFee),
        state.data.collateralToken.balanceOf(msg.sender)
    );
    state.data.collateralToken.transferFrom(
        msg.sender, state.feeConfig.feeRecipient, fragmentationFeeInCollateral
    );
}
```

This code block is executed when there's a remaining balance in the credit position after compensation (`exiterCreditRemaining > 0`). The protocol's intention here is to charge a fragmentation fee when a credit position is split. Albeit, there are several issues as a result of this:

- Fixed Fee Regardless of Fragmentation Amount:
   The fragmentation fee is a fixed amount (`state.feeConfig.fragmentationFee`) regardless of how much of the credit position is being fragmented. This could lead to disproportionate fees for small fragmentations.
 
- The fee is only charged when there's a remaining balance in the original credit position. If the entire credit position is used for compensation, no fee is charged, even though the credit position is effectively being transferred.

-  Users could potentially avoid the fragmentation fee by always compensating the exact amount of their credit positions, leaving no remaining balance.

- While capping the fee by the user's balance prevents the transaction from failing due to insufficient funds, it also means that users with low balances might pay reduced fees or no fees at all.

Let's consider 2 scenarios:

**Scenario 1:**
- User has a credit position of 1000 tokens
- User compensates 999 tokens
- Result: User pays the full fragmentation fee for splitting off 1 token which could be disproportionately high

**Scenario 2:**
- User has a credit position of 1000 tokens
- User compensates 1000 tokens
- Result: User pays no fragmentation fee at all, even though the entire position is transferred

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
A better way to go around this may be to:

1. Charge a proportional fee based on the amount being fragmented or transferred.
2. Apply the fee consistently, regardless of whether there's a remaining balance.
3. Implement a minimum fee to prevent micro-fragmentations.
4. Consider charging the fee in the same token as the credit position to maintain value consistency.
