# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The Size protocol's `multicall` functionality, designed to batch multiple transactions for user convenience, harbors a critical vulnerability that can be exploited to disrupt core protocol operations through front-running attacks. This vulnerability stems from the interaction between the multicall implementation and key protocol functions, particularly liquidations, repayments, and compensations. The vulnerability arises from the ability to include a call that returns large amounts of data, causing the entire multicall to revert due to an out-of-gas error.

An attacker can exploit this by crafting a malicious multicall transaction that:

Includes a call to a critical function (e.g., liquidate, repay, or compensate).
Follows it with a call that returns an excessive amount of data, ensuring the transaction reverts.

By front-running legitimate transactions with such malicious multicalls, an attacker can effectively block or delay the execution of critical protocol functions. This exploit takes advantage of:

The lack of gas limiting on individual calls within the multicall.
The ability to include critical functions in multicalls.
The protocol's vulnerability to transaction ordering exploitation (front-running).

The exploit can target multiple critical functions:

Liquidations (Liquidate.sol): Prevents timely liquidation of undercollateralized positions.
Repayments (Repay.sol): Hinders borrowers from repaying their loans on time.
Compensations (Compensate.sol): Interferes with users' ability to manage their debt and credit positions efficiently.

The impact of this vulnerability is severe:

Risk Accumulation:

Undercollateralized positions remain in the system, increasing the protocol's overall risk exposure.
In volatile market conditions, this could lead to rapid accumulation of bad debt and potentially causing insolvency. 
Undermines the protocol's risk management mechanisms.

Repayment Disruption:

Borrowers may be unable to repay loans on time, leading to unintended defaults.
Increased risk of loans becoming overdue, affecting the protocol's financial health.
Potential for cascading effects if large loans cannot be repaid during critical market events.

Compensation Interference:

Users may be prevented from efficiently managing their debt and credit positions, leading to unintended defaults.
Reduced flexibility in the protocol's credit system, potentially leading to suboptimal capital utilization.
Increased risk of positions becoming undercollateralized due to inability to adjust in volatile markets.

Economic Implications:

Potential for significant financial losses to the protocol and its users.
Distortion of the protocol's internal markets and interest rate mechanisms.
Creation of arbitrage opportunities that can be exploited at the expense of honest users.


Trust and Reliability:

Erosion of user confidence in the protocol's ability to function as intended.
Potential for reputational damage in the broader DeFi ecosystem.

An attacker can frontrun the liquidation, repay, or compensate function with a multicall and makes his own multicall transaction fail over and over again to stop liquidations/repays/compensations from happening indefinitely. The attack would look like this:

Bob monitors the mempool for liquidation transactions targeting Alice's position.
When such a transaction appears, Bob front-runs it with a multicall transaction that:
a. Includes a call to the liquidateliquidations/repays/compensate function for Alice's position.
b. Follows it with a call that returns large data, causing the multicall to revert due to out-of-gas.
Bob's transaction is processed first, reverting due to the out-of-gas error.

The original liquidations/repays/compensate transaction then processes, but fails because the nonce for the liquidations/repays/compensate call has increased.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement a two-step liquidation process:
Step 1: Mark a position as liquidatable (gas-efficient, hard to block)
Step 2: Execute the liquidation in a separate transaction

Add a cooldown period for addresses that cause frequent out-of-gas errors in multicalls.

Add gas limit checks in the multicall function to prevent execution of calls likely to cause out-of-gas errors.

Implement a circuit breaker that allows protocol administrators to force-liquidate positions in extreme cases.

Transaction Ordering Protection: Implement a commit-reveal scheme for critical operations.

Implement gas price auctions or priority fees for liquidation transactions to make frontrunning economically unfeasible.
