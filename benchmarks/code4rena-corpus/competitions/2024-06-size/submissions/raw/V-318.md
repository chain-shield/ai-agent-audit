# Vulnerability details

## Impact
A malicious borrower can split a credit position as much as he want and enforce a lender to claim many times, without paying the fragmentationFee.
In some cases (when the Gas is greater than loan amount), it is not affordable for lender to claim the loan and lender will lose the loan and the money he lent to that borrower.

## Proof of Concept
- Bob lends $100 to Candy (now Bob has 1 credit position with 100 credits).
- Bob borrows 150 dollars/USDC from Alice (now Bob has $150 debt).
- Bob calls `compensate` to pay $100 of his debt using his credits:
```solidity
        uint256 exiterCreditRemaining = creditPositionToCompensate.credit - amountToCompensate;
```
- `creditPositionToCompensate.credit = 100`, because Bob has 100 credits
- `amountToCompensate = 100`, because Bob wants to pay-back $100 of his $150 debt.
- So `exiterCreditRemaining = 100 - 100` = `0`

So because `exiterCreditRemaining` is not greater than 0, so Bob will not pay any fragmentationFee:
```solidity
if (exiterCreditRemaining > 0) {
            // charge the fragmentation fee in collateral tokens, capped by the user balance
            uint256 fragmentationFeeInCollateral = Math.min(
                state.debtTokenAmountToCollateralTokenAmount(state.feeConfig.fragmentationFee),
                state.data.collateralToken.balanceOf(msg.sender)
            );
            state.data.collateralToken.transferFrom(
                msg.sender, state.feeConfig.feeRecipient, fragmentationFeeInCollateral
            );
        } 
```
Now Alice should call `claim` 2 times (Alice should claim 2 credit positions to get back her $150), because there are created 2 credit positions and the Alice credit position (the loan Alice issued to Bob) is splitted into 2 credit positions:

- 1 time for claiming that $100.

- 1 time for the remaining $50.

Here a main invariant broke (because the credit is splitted but fee is not charged):
> FEES_01: Fragmentation fees are applied whenever there is a credit fractionalization

However, an attacker can leverage it and make a situation that there is no incentive (it is not affordable) for lender to claim their own loan and completely loses the money he lent:
- Assuming Attacker has a debt to Alice which is $100
- Attacker can lend 100 times and each time $1. (lending to another person or he can lend to himself)
- Now attacker has 100 credit positions and each one has 1 credit
- Attacker compensates these positions one by one without paying any fragmentationFee as shown above
- 100 positions will be created and Lender should claim all of these 100 positions to get back their money back (lender might pay $200 for Gas to claim that 100 dollars, so it is not affordable and lender will lose their money) 

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
```diff
- uint256 exiterCreditRemaining = creditPositionToCompensate.credit - amountToCompensate;

+ uint256 exiterCreditRemaining = creditPositionWithDebtToRepay.credit - amountToCompensate;
```
