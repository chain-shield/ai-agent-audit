# Vulnerability details



## Description
The borrowing mechanism within the contract has a vulnerability due to its failure to automatically clear limit orders once a borrower’s predefined borrowing conditions are fully met. This flaw can cause borrowers to unintentionally incur additional debt beyond their intended limit due to the persistence of active borrow offers. These leftover orders can be exploited through grief lending practices such as front-running, where opportunistic lenders fulfill outdated or unintended borrowing conditions before the borrower can update or cancel them. Although borrowers have the option to manually clear these borrow offers by sending empty parameters to the `sellCreditLimit` function, the system does not proactively manage this, leaving a significant risk open. It is advisable for the contract to automatically clear these limit orders once they are 100% satisfied to prevent unintended financial exposure and ensure the integrity of lending transactions.

## POC

#### Scenario 1: Unintentional Excessive Borrowing Due to Persistent Limit Orders
- **Initial Setup**: A user deposits 2 Ether (worth $4000 each), intending to borrow against a fraction of this collateral.
- **Borrower Actions**:
  - The user sets the `openingLimitBorrowCR` (Collateral Ratio) to 4, implying the desire to borrow up to 25% of the collateral's value.
  - A borrow offer is created with specific terms (1 month duration and 10% APR).
  - Lender lends the maximum possible amount to the borrower
- **Attack Vector**: 
  - After some time the borrower attempts to withdrawing some of the collateral and resetting their `openingLimitBorrowCR` to a more conservative ratio (1.5), an attacker sees the transaction in the mempool.
  - The attacker front-runs this transaction by quickly lending the maximum possible amount under the existing, more generous terms, effectively locking the borrower into a higher debt level than intended.

#### Scenario 2: Risk of Liquidation from Unintended Additional Borrowing
- **Initial Setup**: A borrower with an active loan backed by 1 Ether faces a drop in Ether's price, pushing the collateral ratio below safe thresholds.
- **Borrower Actions**:
  - To safeguard against liquidation, the borrower deposits an additional Ether to improve the collateral ratio.
- **Attack Vector**:
  - The persistent limit order in the borrower's profile, if not updated or cleared after the initial loan fulfillment, allows for additional borrowing against the newly deposited collateral before the borrower can adjust the terms.
  - This can lead to unintentional borrowing, instantly reducing the effectiveness of the additional deposit meant to secure the loan, pushing the collateral ratio back to minimum levels.

## Impact
Both scenarios expose borrowers to potential financial distress due to unintended debt exposure. The lack of automatic updates or clearance of limit orders after significant changes in a borrower's collateral or debt intentions creates vulnerabilities that can be exploited by other parties, potentially leading to financial losses or forced liquidations under adverse conditions.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Clear limit order as soon as Collateral ratio reaches `openingLimitBorrowCR` or user defined `openingLimitBorrowCR`
