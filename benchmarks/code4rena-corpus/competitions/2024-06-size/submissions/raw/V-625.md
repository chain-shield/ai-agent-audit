# Vulnerability details

## Summary

Chainlink aggregators have a built in circuit breaker if the price of an asset goes outside of a predetermined price band. If an asset experiences a huge crash, the price returned by the oracle will always be the minAnswer instead of the actual price which could be much lower. This means that the collateral value of a borrower will be much less than it actually is so he will be denied credit that he should be eligible for.

## Vulnerability Details

In Size we have 2 price feeds - ETH/USD and USDC/USD. Let's look at the USDC/USD Chainlink price feed - https://etherscan.io/address/0x8fFfFfd4AfB6115b954Bd326cbe7B4BA576818f6#readContract

If we then go to the underlying aggregator (https://etherscan.io/address/0x789190466E21a8b78b8027866CBBDc151542A26C#readContract) we can see that the `minAnswer` returned for that price feed is 1000000 with 8 decimals precision, this means 1 USDC token can be worth at minimum 0.01$.

However if USDC depegs and falls below 0.01$ then we cannot tell the actual price of USDC and the Size protocol will not function as expected.

## Proof of Concept

Let's say a user has 10WETH as collateral deposited. Assume 1WETH = 2000$ and USDC price is 0.005$.
The returned value of his collateral will be 10 * (2000$/0.01) = 2 000 000USDC and the actual value of his collateral is 10 * (2000$/0.005) = 4 000 000USDC

This means that the user will suffer liqudation on positions he isn't actually undercollateralized.

## Impact

In the event that USDC crashes the protocol will stop functioning correctly and its users will suffer losses.

## Tools Used

Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

The `PriceFeed` contract should introduce a minAnswer and maxAnswer circuit breaker check. The feed should revert if it hit minAnswer or maxAnswer.
