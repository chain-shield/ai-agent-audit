# Vulnerability details

## Impact
- Slippage protection is crucial in financial transactions to ensure that the execution price does not deviate significantly from the expected price. By setting slippage to `0`, you effectively allow no room for price fluctuations. This means that the transaction might fail if the price changes even slightly.
- However, a `0` slippage setting can be particularly risky in volatile markets. An attacker could exploit this by front-running the transaction. Front-running involves observing a pending transaction in the mempool and then placing a transaction with a higher gas fee to be executed first. 

## Proof of Concept
1. **Observation**: An attacker monitors the mempool and sees that a user has initiated a transaction using `modifyAutoHedgeAndClose`.
2. **Front-Running**: The attacker places their transaction with a higher gas fee, ensuring it gets executed before the user's transaction.
3. **Price Manipulation**: The attacker's transaction could manipulate the price or the state in such a way that the user's transaction, which follows, executes under unfavorable conditions or fails.
4. **Profit**: The attacker benefits from the manipulated price or from the user's transaction failure.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

1. **Slippage Tolerance**: Introduce a reasonable slippage tolerance. Instead of setting slippage to `0`, allow a small percentage of slippage to account for normal market volatility.
   
2. **Time-Weighted Average Price (TWAP)**: Use TWAP or similar mechanisms to mitigate the impact of sudden price changes and reduce the risk of price manipulation.
   
3. **Commit-Reveal Scheme**: Implement a commit-reveal scheme where users first commit to a transaction and then reveal the details in a later transaction. This makes it harder for an attacker to front-run as they cannot see the exact details initially.
   
4. **Gas Price Cap**: Set a cap on the gas price that can be used for transactions to discourage front-running based on gas price manipulation.
