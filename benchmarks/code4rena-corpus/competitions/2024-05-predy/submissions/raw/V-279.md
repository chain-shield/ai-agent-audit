# Vulnerability details

Currently, if a position is significantly unhealthy, the liquidator is in charge of paying back that bad debt. This will come at the expense of the liquidator and disincentivizes users from liquidating unhealthy positions. If these positions are not liquidated in a timely manner, then the positions can accrue more bad debt, hurting the protocol more and more.

Part of the risk of being a lender is that positions can take on such a loss that bad debt has accumulated. By putting this burden on the liquidator instead of the lenders, it actually hurts lenders even more. The reason being is that there will be fewer liquidators willing to take on a loss for the sake of the lenders, which leads to more bad debt accruing, which directly comes at the expense of the lenders.

Shielding the lenders from the risk of lending has the opposite of its intended effect and will result in the lenders experiencing more loss than if they just took on the initial loss when the bad debt first accrued.

## Impact
Greater bad debt will accrue, leading to loss for lenders of the pool.


## Proof of Concept
Alice opens a 10x position of 1 ETH for $4000
Price craters 11%
Alice is at a 110% loss, meaning any liquidator would need to make up for this loss
No liquidator volunteers to pay off the bad debt
Price continues to go down, increasing the amount of bad debt
Lenders' loss increases as no one has any incentive to liquidate the position.
## Tools Used
Manual analysis

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

Do not require that the liquidator has to subsidize the lenders. Instead, let the lenders take on the risk of lending so that liquidations are never disincentivized.
