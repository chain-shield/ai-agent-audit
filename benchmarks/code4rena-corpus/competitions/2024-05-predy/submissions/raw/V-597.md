# Vulnerability details

## Impact

The [callUniswapObserve()](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-05-predy/blob/a9246db5f874a91fb71c296aac6a66902289306a/src/libraries/UniHelper.sol#L35) function, as implemented, is indeed vulnerable to issues related to stale or outdated data, particularly in a Layer 2 (L2) environment like Arbitrum, where sequencer downtime can lead to significant delays in data updates. This vulnerability can be exacerbated by the nature of how Uniswap's Oracle works and how the function handles observations.

## Proof of Concept

**Stale Data Due to Sequencer Downtime**: In L2 solutions like Arbitrum, if the `sequencer goes down`, no new transactions are processed until it comes back online. This means that during downtime, no new observations are recorded in the Uniswap pool. The `callUniswapObserve()` function, when querying the pool's oracle, might only have access to the last recorded observation before the downtime.

**Extrapolation of Stale Prices**: When the sequencer resumes, and the `callUniswapObserve()` function is called, it might still rely on outdated price data until new observations are recorded. This can lead to the function returning a time-weighted average price that is not reflective of the current market conditions.

**Manipulation and Arbitrage Opportunities**: If an attacker is aware of the sequencer downtime and the subsequent reliance on outdated observations, they could exploit this by executing transactions that benefit from the stale prices. This is particularly concerning in financial protocols where accurate pricing is crucial for functions like liquidations, swaps, or collateral management.
