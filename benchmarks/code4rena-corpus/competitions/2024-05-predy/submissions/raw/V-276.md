# Vulnerability details

Because profit is not taken into consideration when determining what can be withdrawn, lenders have the opportunity to look ahead at the open positions to determine if they can take profit on their positions. If they see that there are positions that can take a lot of profit, then the lender can simply withdraw their funds before the trader trades. This does not need to be a front run; all they need to do is look on-chain and see profitable positions. At this point, they can decide that the risk of taking on that loss is not worth it and withdraw.

Not only do the lenders get to escape a loss, but this also prevents traders from potentially taking all their profit as they will only be able to withdraw a limited amount. And most importantly the burden of the loss is unfairly on the other lenders.

## Impact
Lenders can avoid losses at the expense of traders being able to close their positions, as well as other lenders taking on more of a loss.

## Proof of Concept
Alice supplies 1 ETH
Bob supplies 1 ETH
Charlie opens a 10x leverage position using 1 ETH
Charlie's position is 500% in profit. Alice sees Charlie is in large profit and decides to withdraw to not be responsible for paying out Charlie. Charlie closes the position taking out 0.5 ETH. Bob withdraws what is available (0.5 ETH).

Alice and Bob were both lenders, but Alice was able to withdraw her full 1 ETH. Bob had to take on 100% of the loss and only withdrew 0.5 ETH.

## Tools Used
Manual analysis

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
When calculating what is available, determine the total PNL of traders. If the PNL is positive (profit), then take that amount away from what can be withdrawn. If the PNL is negative (loss), do nothing.
