# Vulnerability details

## Description 
The liquidate function in the LiquidationLogic library is responsible for executing the liquidation process. It involves several critical steps, including updating interest growth, checking if the vault is in danger, executing trades, updating the vault's margin, and transferring any remaining margin.

However, the function does not account for the possibility of flash deposits, where a liquidator could temporarily deposit a large amount of assets to manipulate the vault's margin or value within a single transaction. This manipulation can lead to incorrect liquidation decisions, allowing the liquidator to earn extra yield unfairly.
 

## Impact
1. The manipulated values can lead to incorrect liquidation decisions, either preventing necessary liquidations or triggering unnecessary ones.

2. The liquidator can earn extra yield by exploiting the temporary inflated values.

## Proof of Concept
Scenario: 
- A liquidator deposits a large amount of assets into the vault within a single transaction.
- The temporary deposit inflates the vault's margin or value, making it appear safer or more liquidatable.
- The liquidator triggers the liquidation process based on the manipulated values.
- After the liquidation, the liquidator withdraws the deposited assets within the same transaction.

manual review 

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

1. Time-Weighted Averages:
- Implement time-weighted average values for margin and vault value calculations to mitigate the impact of temporary deposits.
- This approach ensures that the calculations are based on more stable and representative values rather than temporary spikes.

2. Deposit Lock Periods:
- Introduce a lock period for deposits to prevent immediate withdrawals within the same transaction.
- This strategy ensures that any deposited assets remain in the vault for a minimum duration, reducing the potential for flash deposit exploits.

3. Flash Loan Detection:
- Implement mechanisms to detect and prevent flash loan attacks by monitoring large, sudden deposits and withdrawals within a single transaction.
- This can involve setting thresholds for maximum allowed deposits or withdrawals within a single transaction and flagging suspicious activity.
