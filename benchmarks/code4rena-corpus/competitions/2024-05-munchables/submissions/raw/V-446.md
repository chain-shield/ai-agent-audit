# Vulnerability details

## Summary

On the Blast chain, USDB and WETH are implemented as rebasing tokens that adjust holders' balances automatically to reflect yield changes. When these tokens are locked in the protocol, users miss out on potential yields due to the tokens' rebasing nature. More critically, during a negative rebase, the protocol may not possess sufficient funds to fulfill all unlock requests, potentially leading to transaction reverts and locked funds.

## Vulnerability Detail

USDB and WETH adjust user balances directly to account for accrued yield or to reflect negative rebasing adjustments. This automatic balance adjustment poses significant risks when these tokens are locked in the lockManager.

The locking mechanism involves transferring tokens from the user to the contract without accounting for future rebasing events:

- _lock
```solidity=347
if (_tokenContract != address(0)) {
    IERC20 token = IERC20(_tokenContract);
    token.transferFrom(_tokenOwner, address(this), _quantity);
}
```

When users request to unlock their tokens, the amount originally locked is returned without any adjustments for accrued yield or loss due to rebasing:

- unlock
```solidity=422
IERC20 token = IERC20(_tokenContract);
token.transfer(msg.sender, _quantity);
```

This process does not compensate for any increase in token balance due to positive rebasing, effectively causing users to lose out on yield. Moreover, in the event of a negative rebase, the lockManager may find itself with fewer tokens than required to satisfy all unlock requests, leading to failed transactions.

## Impact

This vulnerability can result in two significant issues for users:

1. Loss of Yield: Users do not benefit from positive rebasing effects during the lock period, missing out on potential gains.
2. Inability to Unlock Funds: During a negative rebase, the total token supply in the lockManager may decrease below the amount needed to honor all unlock requests, causing user transactions to revert.
