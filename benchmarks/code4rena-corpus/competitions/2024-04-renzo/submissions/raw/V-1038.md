# Vulnerability details

The [`DepositQueue`](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Deposits/DepositQueue.sol) contract receives ETH rewards from various sources, such as execution layer rewards and MEV from native staking, likely via the [`OperatorDelegator`](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Delegation/OperatorDelegator.sol) contracts. These rewards are immediately sent to either the deposit queue or withdrawal queue by the [`receive()`](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Deposits/DepositQueue.sol#L163) function, after deducting a protocol fee if configured.

The core issue lies in the fact that the ETH rewards sent to the deposit or withdrawal queue are counted towards the protocol's TVL. Consequently, when a reward is received, it instantaneously increases the exchange rate between ETH and ezETH.

An attacker can exploit this vulnerability by sandwiching the reward transaction. The attack would involve first minting ezETH for a given amount of ETH at the current exchange rate. Subsequently, after the reward is received and the exchange rate increases, they could immediately withdraw their ezETH for ETH at the higher rate, thereby profiting from the difference.

The root cause of this vulnerability stems from the fact that external rewards directly affect the ETH/ezETH exchange rate before being distributed to ezETH holders.

## Impact
An attacker can extract value from the protocol by sandwiching reward transactions. This would be at the expense of other ezETH holders, as the attacker mints ezETH at a lower rate and withdraws at a higher rate. 

The attacker's profit would be proportional to the amount of the reward relative to the TVL. The higher the reward as a percentage of TVL, the more value they could extract.

## Proof of Concept
Here is a step-by-step illustration of how an attacker could exploit this vulnerability:

1. Assume the ETH/ezETH exchange rate is 1:1 and the TVL is 1000 ETH. 
   
2. Attacker mints 100 ezETH by depositing 100 ETH at the 1:1 rate.
   1. The function `calculateMintAmount()` will be called with the following arguments:
      1. `_currentValueInProtocol` -> `1000e18`
      2. `_newValueAdded` -> `100e18`
      3. `_existingEzETHSupply` -> `1000e18`
      4. It will return `100 ETH (100e18)`, which will be the amount of `ezETH` that the attacker will be minted.
   
3. The totalTVL will be increased by `100 ETH`. So it'll be `1100 ETH` now.

4. The protocol receives a `100 ETH` reward from EigenLayer. This increases the TVL to `1200 ETH`.

5. The new ETH/ezETH exchange rate is 1.2:1.1. For 1 ezETH you can now withdraw ~1.09 ETH.

6. Attacker immediately withdraws their 100 ezETH and receives 109 ETH due to the higher rate.
   1. The function `calculateRedeemAmount()` will be called with the following arguments:
      1. `_ezETHBeingBurned` -> `100e18`
      2. `_existingEzETHSupply` -> `1100e18` (1000 + the 100 ezETH which are transferred to the withdrawal queue to be burnt)
      3. `_currentValueInProtocol` -> `1200e18` (Total TVL)
      4. It will return `109.09 ETH`. So the attacker will be walking away with `9.09` more ETH than he is supposed to.

7. Attacker profits 9.09 ETH from the sandwich attack, while the protocol's TVL is reduced by 9.09 ETH.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
It would be advisable to accumulate external rewards in the `DepositQueue` contract and then distribute them pro-rata to ezETH holders over time. This approach would effectively decouple the rewards from the ETH/ezETH exchange rate, mitigating the risk of the identified vulnerability.
