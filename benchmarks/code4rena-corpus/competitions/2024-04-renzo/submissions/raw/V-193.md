# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The lack of a burning mechanism for `ezETH` tokens upon withdrawal presents a significant risk of inflation within the protocol, potentially leading to an imbalance between the circulating supply of `ezETH` tokens and the actual assets held within the protocol. This imbalance could distort the protocol's tokenomics, diminish the value proposition of `ezETH` tokens, and undermine the protocol's stability and sustainability over time.
## Proof of Concept
The vulnerability stems from the incomplete implementation of the withdrawal process within the `RestakeManager` contract. Specifically, the burning mechanism for `ezETH` tokens, which should be triggered upon withdrawal, is missing.
The intended behavior, as specified in the documentation, dictates that when users initiate withdrawals by calling the `completeQueuedWithdrawal` function in the `OperatorDelegator` contract, their `ezETH` tokens should be moved into the `RestakeManager` contract and subsequently burned. However, the `RestakeManager` contract does not contain the necessary logic to execute this burning process.

Despite the critical role of the `RestakeManager` contract in managing assets, there is no code segment within the contract dedicated to burning `ezETH` tokens. This oversight directly contradicts the protocol's intended behavior and poses a security risk by allowing `ezETH` tokens to remain in circulation after withdrawal.
Without the burning mechanism in place, the circulating supply of `ezETH` tokens may increase unchecked, leading to potential inflationary pressures and disrupting the protocol's economic model. This vulnerability compromises the protocol's stability and undermines its integrity.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement a burning mechanism within the `RestakeManager` contract to ensure that the appropriate amount of `ezETH` tokens is burned when users withdraw their assets, aligning the contract's behavior with the intended functionality outlined in the documentation.
