# Vulnerability details

## Impact

If Chainlink encounters difficulties initiating a new round and achieving consensus on the updated value for the oracle, various issues may arise. For instance, if Chainlink nodes abandon the oracle, congestion on the chain occurs, or vulnerabilities or attacks target the Chainlink system, consumers of the contract might persist in using outdated data. Consequently, this could result in stagnant prices and incorrect return values, or prices that are no longer current.

Such scenarios could cause functions relying on precise price feeds to malfunction, potentially leading to financial losses. The impacts of these occurrences can vary, contingent upon the specific circumstances at hand.

## Proof of Concept
The RenzoOracle contract's [lookupTokenValue](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/1c7cc4e632564349b204b4b5e5f494c9b0bc631d/contracts/Oracle/RenzoOracle.sol#L71) and [lookupTokenAmountFromValue](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/1c7cc4e632564349b204b4b5e5f494c9b0bc631d/contracts/Oracle/RenzoOracle.sol#L85) function calls `latestRoundData()` from a Chainlink oracle to obtain token prices. It verifies the timestamp but not the round's completeness.

[Chainlink's documentation](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/historical-data) indicates that `latestRoundData()` may return `zero` or `outdated data` if `no new round` has been completed. This reliance on an external oracle for price information introduces risks, such as delayed updates or maintenance failures, leading to the use of obsolete data in critical financial calculations. Past incidents in the DeFi space have shown that such dependencies can cripple systems, often due to simple issues like network congestion.
