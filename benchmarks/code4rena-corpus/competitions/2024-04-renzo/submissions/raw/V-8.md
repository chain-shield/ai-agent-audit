# Vulnerability details

Delegated staking protocols may be exposed to a [known vulnerability](https://ethresear.ch/t/deposit-contract-exploit/6528), where a malicious operator front-runs a staker’s deposit call to the Beacon chain deposit contract and provides a different withdrawal credentials. This issue impacts Reno as well.

## Impact
Even though operators are trusted by the DAO, there's a risk associated with incoming ETH deposits potentially reaching the ETH_DEPOSIT_SOFT_CAP of 3200 ETH. This sizable amount could serve as a strong incentive for an operator to turn malicious and execute an attack, with the cost of the attack being only 1 ETH per validator.

In practice, incoming deposits could surpass the soft cap, possibly in multiples of 3200 ETH. This scenario could unfold if several rebalances are initiated without delay, allowing for the depositing of all the ETH within a few blocks.

Should such an attack occur, all deposited funds would be lost.

This vulnerability also affected several LST/LRT protocols, see [Lido](https://research.lido.fi/t/mitigations-for-deposit-front-running-vulnerability/1239) and [EtherFi](https://246895607-files.gitbook.io/~/files/v0/b/gitbook-x-prod.appspot.com/o/spaces%2FG3Lk76lfvw9ecPIg0mK8%2Fuploads%2FFgdNivH2FNNe7JwkZXtd%2FNM0093-FINAL-ETHER-FI.pdf?alt=media&token=5aa1a2dc-33c7-430d-a2cb-59f56d2cfd2b) reports.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
The Lido discussion thoroughly explores potential solutions. My suggestions are:

To ensure that validator key entries submitted by a node operator are approved by the DAO, require the operator to deposit 1 ETH, along with the correct protocol-controlled WC, for each public key used in these deposit data entries. Upon approval of the validator keys, refund the operator the deposited 1 ETH.

Alternatively, emulate Lido's approach by establishing a committee of guardians tasked with monitoring the deposit contract and issuing a signed off-chain message to authorize deposits.

Rio should also consider measures to reduce incentives for malicious operator behavior, such as:
1) Limiting the maximum ETH deposit amount per transaction.
2) Implementing a brief delay between rebalances, even if the soft cap is reached, to prevent the chaining of rebalances for depositing a large amount of ETH.
