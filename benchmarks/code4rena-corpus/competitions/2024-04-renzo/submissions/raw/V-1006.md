# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The [`xRenzoBridge`](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Bridge/L1/xRenzoBridge.sol) contract on L1 is responsible for receiving ETH from the L2 chain, depositing it into the [`RestakeManager`](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/RestakeManager.sol) to mint `ezETH`, and then sending the minted `ezETH` to the `xezETHLockbox` to be wrapped into `xezETH`. However, the conversion rate from ETH to `ezETH` in this process is [determined](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/RestakeManager.sol#L605-L609) by the `RestakeManager.depositETH()` function, which may not always match the rate at which `xezETH` was minted on L2.

When a user deposits ETH on L2 through the [`xRenzoDeposit`](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Bridge/L2/xRenzoDeposit.sol) contract, `xezETH` is minted immediately at a price retrieved from an L1 price feed. This price may be different from the actual conversion rate used when the deposited ETH is swept to L1 and converted to `ezETH` via `RestakeManager.depositETH()`.

If the `RestakeManager` mints `ezETH` at a lower rate than the rate used for minting `xezETH` on L2, the lockbox on L1 (i.e., the `xezETHLockbox` contract) will not hold enough `ezETH` to back all the circulating `xezETH` supply. This can lead to insolvency, as there might not be enough `ezETH` to redeem all the `xezETH` tokens.

## Proof of Concept
Consider the following scenario:
1. The price feed on L2 indicates that 1 ETH is worth 1,000 `ezETH`.
2. Alice deposits 1 ETH into the `xRenzoDeposit` contract on L2 and receives 1,000 `xezETH`.
3. The deposited ETH is swept to L1 and received by the `xRenzoBridge` contract.
4. The `xRenzoBridge` contract deposits the ETH into the `RestakeManager`, which mints only 900 `ezETH` due to a different conversion rate.
5. The minted 900 `ezETH` is sent to the `xezETHLockbox`, leaving the lockbox undercollateralized, as it holds less `ezETH` than the amount of `xezETH` in circulation.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
To mitigate this risk, consider the following:
1. The safest mitigation would be to disallow instant minting of `xezETH` on L2. Instead, when a user deposits ETH on L2, send the collateral to L1 first. Then, have the `xRenzoBridge` contract reply to L2 with the actual mint rate at which `ezETH` was minted, and mint `xezETH` on L2 at the same rate. This ensures that the `xezETH` supply is always backed by an equal amount of `ezETH` in the lockboxes.
2. Modify the `RestakeManager` contract to provide a separate function that can only be called by the `xRenzoBridge` contract. This function should mint `ezETH` for ETH at a predetermined rate that matches the rate used when minting `xezETH` on L2.
3. Implement a mechanism to periodically reconcile the `xezETH` supply on L2 with the `ezETH` balance in the lockboxes on L1. If a discrepancy is detected, adjust the `xezETH` supply or the `ezETH` balance to maintain a 1:1 backing.
