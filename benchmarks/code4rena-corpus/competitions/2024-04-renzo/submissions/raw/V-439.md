# Vulnerability details

## Impact
Unverified calldata allows an attacker to steal ERC20 tokens from users who have approved the contract to spend their tokens.

## Vulnerability Details

The bridgeTo function combines both Lockbox deposit and Blast bridge's BridgeERC20To call. As seen below, the function makes a call to Blast L1StandardBridge with an unverified calldata. This calldata can be the 'transferFrom' of a previously approved user. This allows an attacker to steal tokens from users who have approved the contract to spend their tokens.

[LockboxAdapterBlast.sol#L93](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/519e518f2d8dec9acf6482b84a181e403070d22d/contracts/Bridge/Connext/integration/LockboxAdapterBlast.sol#L93)
```
        L1StandardBridge(blastStandardBridge).bridgeERC20To(
            xerc20,
            _remoteToken,
            _to,
            _amount,
            _minGasLimit,
@>          _extraData
        );
```

An example of an exploit caused by unverified calldata is the Paraswap [exploit](https://medium.com/neptune-mutual/analysis-of-the-paraswap-exploit-1f97c604b4fe) where the attacker used a malicious calldata input to steal the tokens of users who had previously approved the contract to spend their tokens.

## Tools Used
Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
[The Graph solved this by whitelisting users who can use calldata](https://etherscan.io/address/0xd41ca6a1d034d178c196dfa916f22f7d1a1b8222#code#F1#L294). Whitelist users who can use calldata or verify that the calldata provided by users isn't malicious.
