# Vulnerability details

## Impact

Withdrawers can be affected by the volatility of the LST assets when waiting for their withdrawal requests to mature.

## Proof of Concept

The current flow of the `Renzo` withdrawals consists of 2 steps:

- The first step is the initiation for withdrawal, which transfers the `ezETH` tokens to the `WithdrawQueue` and **calculates how many assets the user will receive after the waiting period expires.**

[WithdrawQueue.sol#L206-L263](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Withdraw/WithdrawQueue.sol#L206-L263)


- The second step is the actual burning of `ezETH` tokens and transferring the amount of `collateralToken` which has been calculated when the request was initiated, which can be at least 7 days, there `amountToRedeem` is not checked for acceptable slippage.

The problem is that the collateralTokens - stETH, wBETH, mETH are volatile and for sure will have slightly differences after the waiting period and the `amountToRedeem` calculated in the first step will greatly differ (both negatively and positively) at the time of withdrawal and users will be affected.

For example the current ratio of stETH/ETH is exactly 1:1, a user initiates withdrawal of 1 ezETH, and it will first calculate how much ETH is this in calculateRedeemAmount(), and then if the token is not native (like in our case - stETH), it will convert it to ETH in `RenzoOracle::lookupTokenAmountFromValue`. 

Consider this example:

1. Alice deposits 100 stETH, and 100 ezETH are minted for her as first depositor.
2. She request withdraw for 1 ezETH
3. For simplicity consider exchange rate of ezETH/ETH 1:1 (it can be any other rate, since it does not affect the core issue from this report), then `amountToRedeem` is 1 ETH, and `lookupTokenAmountFromValue` gives her 1stETH that will be claimable after the delay passes.
4. `amountToRedeem` = 1e18
5. Between the request and the actual claim, the `stETH/ETH` price changed, now 1 ETH worth 1.05 stETH.
6. When she call `claim`, it will sent her 1 stETH, despite that now it is now worth 1.05 ETH
7. Since there is no withdrawal request expiration users can wait until the price is favorable and they can extract as much value as possible.

And the reversed example:

5. Between the request and the actual claim, the `stETH/ETH` price changed, now 1 ETH worth 0.95 stETH.
6. When she call `claim`, it will sent her 1 stETH, despite that now is it is worth 0.95 ETH.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

A possible solution is to create a parameter for the maximum deviation of the collateral in `WithdrawQueue::withdraw` and recalculate the `amountToRedeem` applying the max deviation check when claiming it in `WithdrawQueue::claim`, which is acceptable for both users and the protocol.

The downside of this solution is if the price moves even more the deviation check will make the transactions revert, this can be solved with a function to cancel existing requests that are eligible to be claimed.
