# Vulnerability details

Chainlink aggregators come with a built-in circuit breaker that triggers when the price of an asset surpasses a pre-set range. Additionally, it's essential to note the `minPrice` and `maxPrice` parameters of the Chainlink oracle, ensuring these limits are not breached.

For comprehensive documentation on `minPrice` and `maxPrice`, please consult the Chainlink API reference. This matter has already been covered in: 

https://solodit.xyz/issues/missing-checks-for-chainlink-oracle-spearbit-connext-pdf

https://solodit.xyz/issues/m-02-missing-check-for-the-maxmin-price-in-the-chainlinkoraclesol-contract-code4rena-moonwell-moonwell-git

## Impact
In the event of a market crash, there's a risk of receiving incorrect pricing data, potentially enabling adversaries to borrow based on this inaccurate valuation. This could lead to accumulating harmful debt within the protocol.

## Proof of Concept
The function `lookupTokenValue` in `RenzoOracle.sol` checks only that `price` to be non-positive for reverting:

```
if (price <= 0) revert InvalidOraclePrice();
```

Nevertheless, it fails to verify minimum and maximum price thresholds. Let's examine a potential vulnerability:

Token X, configured with a minimum price of 10, undergoes a decline in value to 1. Despite this plunge, the aggregator persistently indicates 10, allowing users to borrow against Token X under the impression that it's valued at 10, when in reality, it's only worth a fraction of that.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

The `RenzoOracle.sol` must ensure that the returned answer falls within the designated `minPrice` and `maxPrice` boundaries.

```solidity
require(price < _maxPrice, "Upper price bound breached");
require(price > _minPrice, "Lower price bound breached");
```
