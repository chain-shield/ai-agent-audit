# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The `_ccipReceive` function in the `CCIPReceiver.sol` contract does not have any mechanism to validate the origin of the CCIP message, leaving it vulnerable to replay attacks across different chains.

The `_ccipReceive` function has two checks, which are:
`if (_ccipSender != xRenzoBridgeL1) revert InvalidSender(xRenzoBridgeL1, _ccipSender);` -  checks if the _ccipSender is the same as the xRenzoBridgeL1 address to verify the sender.

and `if (_ccipSourceChainSelector != ccipEthChainSelector)
revert InvalidSourceChain(ccipEthChainSelector, _ccipSourceChainSelector);` - checks if the _ccipSourceChainSelector matches the ccipEthChainSelector to verify the source chain.

These checks are a good start, but they are not sufficient to fully protect against replay attacks across different chains.

The key issue is that these checks only verify the origin of the message on the current chain. They do not provide any protection against a valid message being replayed on a different chain.

For example, an attacker could intercept a valid CCIP message on one chain, and then replay that same message on a different chain, where the `_ccipSender` and `_ccipSourceChainSelector` would still pass the checks, even though the origin is different.

An attacker could intercept a valid CCIP message on one chain and replay it on a different chain, resulting in unauthorized actions, such as updating the price feed for their benefit or triggering other sensitive operations. This could lead to the loss of user funds and compromise the integrity of the cross-chain functionality.

## Proof of Concept
Attacker, Bob, observes a valid CCIP message being sent from the `xRenzoBridge` contract on chain A.

Bob intercepts the CCIP message and stores the relevant parameters, such as the `sourceChainSelector`, `sender`, `price`, and `timestamp`.

Bob then replays the CCIP message on chain B, where the `CCIPReceiver` contract is also deployed.

Since the `_ccipReceive` function does not validate the origin of the message, it will execute the price update without any checks, allowing Bob to potentially manipulate the price feed.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement a mechanism to validate the origin of the CCIP message, such as using a nonce or a chain ID.

Update the `_ccipReceive` function to check the chain of origin and reject any replayed messages.

Consider using a cryptographic signature or other secure authentication method to ensure the CCIP message is from the expected source.
