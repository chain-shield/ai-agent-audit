# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The function `_refundGas` calculates the gas refund based on `tx.gasprice`, which can be manipulated by validators or in cooperation with a malicious user.

A validator or a user in collaboration with a validator could set an arbitrarily high `gasprice` for their transaction, leading to an inflated gas refund from the contract's balance. This could drain a decent amount out of the contract's funds if the balance is sufficient.


## Proof of Concept
#### Scenario:

- A validator or a group of validators identifies the `DepositQueue` contract with the refundable gas feature.
- They create a transaction that interacts with the contract in a way that triggers the `_refundGas` function.
- The validators set an artificially high `gasprice` for the transaction.
- Since they are the block proposers, they can ensure that this transaction is included in the block they are validating.
- The contract calculates the refund based on the inflated `gasprice` and refunds more `ETH` than the actual cost of gas used for the transaction.
- The validators receive the excess refund, extracting value from the contract.


## Tools Used
Manual review


## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement a gas price limit within the contract to cap the refundable amount.
OR
Use a gas price oracle to determine a reasonable gas price for refunds.
