## Impact

```
As per docs and sponsor confirmation staleness only allowed up to 1 day only.
```

Deposits are permitted using price data that is up to 2 days and 60 seconds old. This allowance for utilizing stale price information can lead to inaccuracies in deposit valuations, potentially resulting in overestimations or underestimations of user deposit amounts due to outdated price data.

### Explanation

Based on the current implementation, the oracle extends a freshness threshold of (timestamp < block.timestamp - MAX_TIME_WINDOW) ``1 day + 60 seconds``. Concurrently, the ``_deposit`` function performs its own check, verifying if the timestamp exceeds the last recorded price ``timestamp`` by more than a (block.timestamp > _lastPriceTimestamp + 1 days) ``1 day``. This dual-layer check functions correctly when using ``lastPrice`` and ``lastPriceTimestamp``. However, an issue arises when the price is directly fetched from the oracle (``oraclePrice`` and ``oracleTimestamp``). In this scenario, since the oracle already allows for a staleness of ``1 Day + 60 seconds``, and the ``_deposit`` function further allows an additional ``1 Day`` from the ``oracleTimestamp``, the system inadvertently accepts a price that could be up to ``2 days + 60 seconds old``, thus potentially compromising the accuracy and reliability of the price data used in transactions.

### Steps

This setup means that even though the Oracle first records a price as valid for 1 day and 60 seconds, the _deposit function extends this period by another day, thereby making the price potentially acceptable for up to 2 days + 60 seconds:

Original Oracle Timestamp: T
Price valid in Oracle until: T + 1 day + 60 seconds
Price potentially accepted in _deposit until: T + 1 day + 60 seconds + 1 day (T + 2 days + 60 seconds)

### POC 

Assume the current date and time is May 10, 2024, at 12:00 PM UTC. 

- Step 1: Oracle Timestamps the Price

Oracle Timestamp: May 8, 2024, at 11:59 AM UTC.
Oracle Price Valid Until: May 9, 2024, at 12:00 PM UTC + 60 seconds (i.e., May 9, 2024, at 12:01 PM UTC).

- Step 2: Deposit Function Checks for Staleness

The _deposit function retrieves the price at May 10, 2024, 12:00 PM UTC with the Oracle timestamp of May 8, 2024, at 11:59 AM UTC.

The function checks:
if (block.timestamp > _lastPriceTimestamp + 1 day)
In this case, if (May 10, 2024, 12:00 PM UTC > May 8, 2024, 11:59 AM UTC + 1 day)
This equates to if (May 10, 2024, 12:00 PM UTC > May 9, 2024, 11:59 AM UTC)
The price is still considered valid in the _deposit function up until May 9, 2024, 11:59 AM UTC + 1 day, which is May 10, 2024, 11:59 AM UTC.

Outcome:
The price is still acceptable for use in the _deposit function on May 10, 2024, at 12:00 PM, which is almost 2 days and 1 minute after the Oracle initially timestamped it. This scenario reveals that the price, although expired by the Oracle's definition, is still considered fresh by the _deposit function for an extra day.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Remove (timestamp < block.timestamp - MAX_TIME_WINDOW) ``1 day + 60 minutes`` check from ``RenzoOracleL2`` oracle contract. Only check in the _deposit() function. So that this will applicable for both ``oraclePrice``, ``oracleTimestamp`` and ``lastPrice``,``lastPriceTimestamp`` values.
