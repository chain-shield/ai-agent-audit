# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The `withdraw` function in the `withdrawQueue` contract allows users to request a withdrawal of their ezETH tokens. However, the function lacks any form of slippage protection, exposing users to potential sandwich attacks. An attacker can monitor the mempool for pending withdraw transactions, execute their own transactions before and after the victim's transaction, and manipulate the redemption amount in their favor.

The absence of slippage protection in the `withdraw` function can lead to users receiving less tokens than expected during the withdrawal process. Attackers can exploit this vulnerability to extract value from users' withdrawals by strategically placing their own transactions before and after the victim's transaction. This can result in financial losses for the users and undermine the trust in the Renzo protocol.

## Proof of Concept

Alice initiates a withdrawal request by calling the `withdraw` function, specifying the amount of ezETH she wants to redeem.

Bob detects Alice's pending transaction in the mempool and quickly crafts two transactions:

Transaction 1: Bob interacts with the protocol to manipulate the redemption amount in his favor (e.g., by making a large donation to inflate the TVL).
Transaction 2: Bob sets up a second transaction to reverse the effect of Transaction 1 after Alice's transaction is executed.

Bob submits Transaction 1 with a higher gas price, ensuring it is mined before Alice's transaction.

Alice's transaction is executed, and the redemption amount is calculated based on the manipulated state caused by Bob's Transaction 1.

Bob's Transaction 2 is mined immediately after Alice's transaction, reversing the manipulation and allowing Bob to profit from the difference in redemption amounts.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
To mitigate the risk of sandwich attacks and protect users from slippage, the following measures can be implemented:

Introduce a slippage tolerance parameter in the `withdraw` function, allowing users to specify the maximum acceptable deviation from the expected redemption amount.

Calculate the redemption amount based on the state of the protocol at the time of the user's transaction execution, rather than relying on the current state that can be manipulated by attackers.

Implement a time-weighted average price (TWAP) mechanism to calculate the redemption amount based on the average price over a specified time period, reducing the impact of sudden price fluctuations.

Consider using a commit-reveal scheme or a batch auction mechanism to aggregate withdraw requests and execute them at predetermined intervals, making it harder for attackers to target individual transactions.
