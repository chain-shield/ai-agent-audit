# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The [`RenzoOracle`](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Oracle/RenzoOracle.sol) contract uses Chainlink oracles to fetch the price of assets, which is then used to calculate the value of tokens deposited by users. The `lookupTokenValue()` function fetches the latest price from the oracle using `latestRoundData()`.

However, Chainlink oracles have a built-in circuit breaker that gets triggered if the price of an asset goes outside a predetermined price band, defined by a `minAnswer` and `maxAnswer`. If the actual price of the asset falls below `minAnswer` or goes above `maxAnswer`, the oracle will keep returning the `minAnswer` or `maxAnswer` instead of the real price.

This could lead to a situation where the oracle is returning an incorrect price that doesn't reflect the true value of the asset. As a result, the `RenzoOracle` contract would calculate the value of user deposits based on this incorrect price. 

In a scenario where an asset experiences a massive crash in value (like LUNA), the oracle price could get stuck at `minAnswer`, allowing users to continue borrowing the asset but at an inflated price compared to its real value. This is similar to what happened to Venus protocol on BSC when LUNA [crashed](https://rekt.news/venus-blizz-rekt/).

## Proof of Concept
1. Collateral asset X has a current price of $100, which falls within the `minAnswer` and `maxAnswer` range of the Chainlink oracle. 
2. The price of Asset X suddenly crashes and falls to $10, which is below the oracle's `minAnswer` of $50.
3. When `RenzoOracle.lookupTokenValue()` fetches the latest price for Asset X, the oracle returns `minAnswer` of $50 instead of the real price of $10.
4. `RenzoOracle` calculates the value of user deposits of Asset X based on the incorrect price of $50 returned by the oracle.
5. Users can now mint ezETH for X at an inflated price of $50 instead of its real value of $10.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Add a check in `lookupTokenValue()` to verify if the price returned by the oracle is equal to `minAnswer` or `maxAnswer`, and revert if that's the case:

```solidity
(, int256 price, , , ) = oracle.latestRoundData();
(uint80 roundID, int256 minAnswer, int256 maxAnswer, , , , ) = oracle.getRoundData(oracle.getRoundId());

if (price == minAnswer || price == maxAnswer) revert OraclePriceOutsideRange();
```

This will ensure that the protocol doesn't use prices from the oracle if they are outside the valid range and stuck at the min/max thresholds. The `minAnswer` and `maxAnswer` values can be obtained by querying the aggregator contract, as described [here](https://medium.com/cyfrin/chainlink-oracle-defi-attacks-93b6cb6541bf#00ac).
