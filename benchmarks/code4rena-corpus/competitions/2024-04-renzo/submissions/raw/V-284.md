# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The `sendPrice` function in the `xRenzoBridge.sol` contract is responsible for sending the current price of `ezETH` to the L2 contracts, such as the `CCIPReceiver.sol` and `ConnextReceiver.sol`. This function is marked as `onlyPriceFeedSender`, which means only authorized entities with the `onlyPriceFeedSender` role can call it.

However, despite this role-based access control, the function is still vulnerable to front-running attacks. The issue arises due to the nature of the Ethereum blockchain, where transactions are processed in the order of the highest gas price, rather than the order in which they were submitted.

The exploitation of this vulnerability can have severe consequences:

The attacker can manipulate the price feed, undermining the integrity of the entire cross-chain functionality.

Users who mint `xezETH` tokens based on the manipulated price feed can receive an incorrect, inflated amount of tokens, leading to financial losses.

Attackers can manipulate price feed with flashloans to deposit a smaller amount of assets and receive a larger amount of `xezETH` tokens, effectively stealing funds from other users.

## Proof of Concept
The authorized `onlyPriceFeedSender` entity, such as the `xRenzoBridge` contract, submits a transaction to call the `sendPrice` function and update the price feed.

An attacker, Bob, observes the pending transaction and quickly obtains a large amount of ETH through a flash loan.

Bob submits his own transaction to call the `sendPrice` function, with a higher gas price than the authorized transaction.

Bob's transaction, being processed first, can manipulate the price data before it is sent to the `CCIPReceiver.sol` and `ConnextReceiver.sol` contracts.

Bob can then use the manipulated price feed to deposit a smaller amount of assets and receive an inflated amount of `xezETH` tokens, effectively stealing funds from other users.

Bob can withdraw the xezETH tokens and repay the flash loan, keeping the profit.

Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement anti-front-running measures, such as a commit-reveal scheme or the use of a decentralized oracle network, to make it more difficult for attackers to front-run the authorized price feed updates.

Consider using a more decentralized or time-delayed price update mechanism to reduce the window of opportunity for front-running attacks.
