# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The funds of the Renzo protocol need to participate in the re-staking of the EigenLayer protocol to generate income. If an attacker interferes with and prevents the Renzo protocol from staking to the EigenLayer, it will prevent the Renzo protocol from generating income.

## Proof of Concept
The key to the attack is that all deposits made by Renzo will be prioritized to meet the withdrawal buffer of the WithdrawQueue contract. To put it in a metaphor, a malicious attacker is equivalent to digging a black hole in the WithdrawQueue contract, which will absorb all new deposits into the WithdrawQueue contract as much as possible, thereby preventing Renzo from depositing funds into EigenLayer.

Taking Native ETH as an example, the ETH deposited by users into the Renzo protocol will be first held in the DepositQueue contract until the ETH held in the DepositQueue contract reaches 32 ETH. Only then will the Renzo protocol deposit the 32 ETH into the EigenLayer protocol

Suppose the value of the withdrawalBufferTarget for Native ETH in the WithdrawQueue contract is equal to 100 ether, and the Renzo team has perfectly filled the withdrawalBufferTarget when the project was launched.

1. Ethereum mainnet: Because the Ethereum mainnet can observe the transaction records of the mempool, the attacker observes that Alice is about to deposit X ETH into the Renzo protocol. At this time, the attacker conducts a front-run to initiate a preemptive transaction, depositing X ETH into the Renzo protocol and immediately applying for withdrawal. At this time, the WithdrawQueue contract will generate X amount of buffer that needs to be filled. Alice's transaction is executed after the attacker, so her X ETH flows to the WithdrawQueue contract. In this way, the attacker prevents X ETH from being deposited into the EigenLayer protocol.

2. L2 network (Arbitrum, Base chain, etc.): Although L2 networks cannot preempt transactions, attackers can find the amount of ETH deposited into Renzo every T period (such as one hour) from historical transactions and calculate the average value R. Note that R < 100 (100 represents the value of withdrawalBufferTarget). Then the attacker executes an attack every T period, first depositing R amount of ETH in the same transaction and then immediately applying for withdrawal. The purpose of this is to ensure that as much as possible, the ETH deposited into the Renzo protocol will flow to the WithdrawQueue contract within T period. The higher the accuracy of T period, the more likely it is to prevent more ETH from being deposited into the EigenLayer protocol

Other attack methods that are not native to ETH follow the same logic. Next, let's analyze the cost of the attacker:

1. Because the attack is to apply for withdrawal immediately after depositing in a transaction, the attacker's funds will not be lost, and the only real loss is the gas fee, which is sufficiently low in the L2 network
2. The Renzo protocol requires a 7-day delay before a withdrawal request can be made, so an attacker with sufficient funds can prevent more ETH from being deposited into the EigenLayer protocol. This amount of funds is approximately equal to the total ETH deposits generated by the Renzo protocol over a 7-day period. I found sample data from the Renzo website, which shows that the amount of ETH deposited into Renzo on January 29, 2024 was 66.54k, and the amount of ETH deposited into Renzo 7 days later, on February 4, 2024, was 80.79k. The cumulative amount of ETH over these 7 days was 14.25k
3. Obviously, ordinary individuals cannot meet this funding scale, although the attack threshold requirements are high, it is not theoretically impossible to achieve. For example, some financial institutions that have competitive relationships with Renzo have the ability to launch attacks. Therefore, theoretically speaking, there is a risk that Renzo will be attacked. The most important thing is that the attacker does not have to achieve a 100% attack. An attacker that can affect 50% or 10% of Renzo's funds and prevent them from interacting with EigenLayer will also cause some degree of damage to Renzo.

In summary, it is recommended to consider this attack strategy as a medium-severity risk.

Manual audit

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
After limiting user deposits, a short period of time is required before applying for withdrawals to prevent simultaneous deposits and withdrawal requests in the same transaction
