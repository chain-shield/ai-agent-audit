# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The `lookupTokenValue` function in the `RenzoOracle` contract (line 71-81) does not have any specific measures to prevent front-running of oracle price updates. If an attacker can anticipate or manipulate the timing of price updates, they might be able to exploit this vulnerability, potentially manipulating prices to their advantage and putting funds at risk.

If an attacker successfully front-runs oracle price updates, they could manipulate prices to their advantage, leading to direct risk to funds a loss of trust in the platform by users.

## Proof of Concept

Bob, the attacker, studies the Chainlink oracle used by the RenzoOracle contract and identifies patterns in price update intervals and gas prices.

Bob sets up a bot to monitor the Ethereum mempool for pending transactions related to the Chainlink oracle.

When Bob's bot detects a pending price update transaction, it quickly calculates the expected price change based on the transaction data.

If the expected price change is favorable for Bob's intended manipulation, his bot sends a transaction with a higher gas price to front-run the oracle update.

Bob's transaction is mined before the oracle update, allowing him to exploit the outdated price in the `RenzoOracle` contract.

Bob profits from the manipulated price by executing trades or interacting with the protocol in a way that benefits from the price discrepancy.

Alice, an honest user who interacts with the protocol during the manipulated price period, suffers financial losses due to buying or selling assets at unfavorable rates.

Bob repeats this process multiple times, accumulating profits at the expense of other users and the protocol itself.

Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Ensure the protocol is not affected in the case where oracle price updates are front-run.

Renzo Protocol could implement a price feed delay mechanism to prevent front-running. Instead of using the latest price directly, consider using a time-weighted average price (TWAP) or a similar approach to smooth out price fluctuations and make it harder for attackers to front-run price updates.
