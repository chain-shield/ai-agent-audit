# Vulnerability details

## Impact
the `xReceive` function in the `ConnextReceiver.sol` contract does not have any mechanism to validate the origin of the Connext message, leaving it vulnerable to replay attacks across different chains.

An attacker could intercept a valid Connext message on one chain and replay it on a different chain, resulting in unauthorized actions, such as updating the price feed for the attackers benefit or triggering other sensitive operations. This could lead to the loss of user funds and compromise the integrity of the cross-chain functionality.

## Proof of Concept
Attacker, Bob, observes a valid Connext message being sent from the xRenzoBridge contract on chain A.

Bob intercepts the Connext message and stores the relevant parameters, such as the `sourceChainDomain`, `originSender`, `price`, and `timestamp`.

Bob then replays the Connext message on chain B, where the ConnextReceiver contract is also deployed.

Since the `xReceive` function does not validate the origin of the message, it will execute the price update without any checks, allowing Bob to potentially manipulate the price feed.

## Tools Used
Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement a mechanism to validate the origin of the Connext message, such as using a nonce or a chain ID.

Update the `xReceive` function to check the chain of origin and reject any replayed messages.

Consider using a cryptographic signature or other secure authentication method to ensure the Connext message is from the expected source.
