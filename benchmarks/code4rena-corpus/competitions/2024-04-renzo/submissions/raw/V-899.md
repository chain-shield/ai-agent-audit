# Vulnerability details

## Impact
Chainlink's price feeds are configured with predefined lower and upper price bounds; during market anomalies such as flash crashes, bridge failures, or asset depegging incidents, if an asset's market value drops beneath the feed's set floor price, the Chainlink feed will persist in reporting this floor price, even though it may not reflect the asset's true market price.

## Proof of Concept
If the RenzoOracle provides the price of [stETH/ETH](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/1c7cc4e632564349b204b4b5e5f494c9b0bc631d/contracts/Oracle/RenzoOracle.sol#L71) without implementing safeguards against incorrect price reporting from Chainlink during extreme market events:

The protocol could overvalue stETH when its market value diverges significantly from ETH, especially if stETH depegs.

Users could exploit this by depositing stETH and receiving more assets or benefits than the stETH is truly worth.

This could lead to a depletion of the protocol's reserves and affect its overall financial health.

[go to the Oracle Returns Incorrect Price During Flash Crashes section](https://medium.com/cyfrin/chainlink-oracle-defi-attacks-93b6cb6541bf#00ac).

[lookupTokenAmountFromValue also has the same issue](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/1c7cc4e632564349b204b4b5e5f494c9b0bc631d/contracts/Oracle/RenzoOracle.sol#L85C14-L85C40)

Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement on-chain checks for `minAnswer` and `maxAnswer` on both `lookupTokenAmountFromValue` & `lookupTokenValue` functions.
