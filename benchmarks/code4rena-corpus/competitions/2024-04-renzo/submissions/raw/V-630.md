# Vulnerability details

## Impact

Attackers can take a part of rewards by front-running the call to `DelayedWithdrawalRouter.claimDelayedWithdrawals()` of EigenLayer.

## Proof of Concept

Rewards that are generated in EigenLayer are sent to `DelayedWithdrawalRouter` contract of EigenLayer first. After a delay, the rewards can be claimed by calling the permissionless function `DelayedWithdrawalRouter.claimDelayedWithdrawals()` of EigenLayer. This call will instantly increase the TVL of the protocol.

An attacker can take advantage of this to steal a part of the rewards. Let's consider following scenario:
1. Initial sate:
    - Total supply of `ezETH`: `1,000e18`,
    - TVL: `1,000 ETH`,
    - Exchange rate: 1.
2. The protocol is about to call the `DelayedWithdrawalRouter.claimDelayedWithdrawals()` function of EigenLayer and receive `50 ETH` as the reward.
3. The attacker deposits `9,000 ETH` by front-running. Then the state becomes:
    - Total supply of `ezETH`: `10,000e18`,
    - TVL: `10,000 ETH`,
    - Exchange rate: 1,
    - The attacker's `ezETH` balance: `9,000e18`.
4. After the protocol's transaction, the state becoems:
    - Total supply of `ezETH`: `10,000e18`,
    - TVL: `10,500 ETH`,
    - Exchange rate: 1.05,
    - The attacker's `ezETH` balance: `9,000e18`.

Finally, the attacker's asset becomes `1.05 * 9,000 = 9450 ETH`, results in taking `45 ETH`, that is the `90%` of protocol's reward.

## Tools Used

Manual review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

Recommend not send `DepositQueue` contract all the reward at once. It would be better distribute the reward following linear function on time.
