# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The original depositor's transaction might be affected by the attacker's manipulation. They could lose tokens, experience unexpected behavior, or have their intended actions thwarted due to the altered state of the contracts.

## Proof of Concept
In Ethereum, pending transactions are stored in the mempool until they are included in a block by miners. Transactions interact with smart contracts, and their execution order can affect the final state of these contracts.
The `OperatorDelegator` contract's `deposit()` function can only be called by the `RestakeManager`, ensuring that deposits are managed securely. However, the `StrategyManager` contract's `deposit()` function is public, allowing anyone to invoke it. This public accessibility creates an opportunity for front-running.
An attacker monitors the mempool for pending transactions and identifies a deposit transaction targeting the `OperatorDelegator` contract. Since miners prioritize transactions with higher gas prices, the attacker quickly submits their own transaction to deposit tokens into the `StrategyManager` contract with a higher gas price. Consequently, the attacker's transaction is likely to be included in the next block before the original `OperatorDelegator` transaction.
By getting their transaction included before the original `OperatorDelegator` deposit, the attacker can manipulate the state of the contracts to their advantage. Since the `StrategyManager` contract approves the `depositQueue` and then the `OperatorDelegator` to spend tokens, the attacker could exploit this approval chain to transfer tokens to themselves or perform other unauthorized actions.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

One effective mitigation to address the front-running vulnerability in this context is to use a commit-reveal scheme for token deposits.

In a commit-reveal scheme:

Commit Phase: The depositor commits to their intended transaction by submitting a hash of the transaction details to the blockchain without revealing the actual details.
Reveal Phase: After a certain period, the depositor reveals the original transaction details, including the token amount and destination contract, which matches the previously committed hash.
By enforcing a commit-reveal scheme, the original depositor's transaction details remain undisclosed until the reveal phase, preventing attackers from front-running and manipulating the transaction order.
