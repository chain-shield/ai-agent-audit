# Vulnerability details

## Impact
This can be used to manipulate the `exchangeRate` of `ezETH` and a malicious user can benefit from the arbitrage opportunity since the price will be different on different chains

## Proof of Concept
When [`xRenzoBridge::sendPrice(...)`](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/519e518f2d8dec9acf6482b84a181e403070d22d/contracts/Bridge/L1/xRenzoBridge.sol#L210) is called to send the price of `ezETH` form L1, a permissionless external call is made to [`BalancerRateProvider::getRate(...)`](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/519e518f2d8dec9acf6482b84a181e403070d22d/contracts/Bridge/L1/xRenzoBridge.sol#L215) which in turn calls the [`RestakeManager.calculateTVLs()`](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/519e518f2d8dec9acf6482b84a181e403070d22d/contracts/RestakeManager.sol#L316-L321) function to evaluate the TVL of the protocol.

Because the balance of all `collateralTokens` including `ETH`  in the protocol’s `WithdrawalQueue` are included in the protocol's `totalTVL` calculation, the TVL can be easily manipulated by a well funded attacker

This is a design flaw because when deposit is made in the `RestakeManager.sol` contract, the protocol prioritises filling its withdrawalQueue up to its `withdrawalQueueBuffer` even when there are no queued withdrawals as against forwarding the deposit into the EigenLayer strategy and getting a strategy share in return, hence the protocol includes the balance of the `collateralTokens` in the `withdrawalQueue` of the protocol when calculating the `totalTVL`

## POC

A malicious user can frontrun the `BalancerRateProvider::getRate(...)` call  to donate any  `collateralTokens` to the withdrawal queue of the protocol to inflate TVL calculation and by extension `ezEth` pricing

- Assume `totalTVL` = 100_000e18, ezETH `totalSupply` = 100_000e18
- `getRate()` returns 10 ** 18

```solidity
return (10 ** 18 * 100000e18) / 100000e18; // 1e18
```

- `PriceFeedSender` calls `xRenzoBridge::sendPrice(...)`
- Bob notices the `BalancerRateProvider::getRate(...)` call and frontruns it to donate 10e18 of any  `collateralTokens` of choice to inflate the `totalTVL` without affecting the `totalSupply` of `ezETH` as there will be no `ezETH` minted to bob when he send funds to the protocols `withdrawalQueue` thus increasing the value returned by `BalancerRateProvider::getRate(...)`

```solidity
return (10 ** 18 * 100010e18) / 100000e18; // 1.0001e18
```

A well funded user can inflate the price way higher and benefit form arbitrage opportunity as the price will be different on different chains

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Consider

- depositing all tokens to the strategy instead of prioritising filling the withdrawal queue before filling strategies when deposits are first made.
- remove the code block below for `RestakeManager.sol`
