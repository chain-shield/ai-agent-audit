# Vulnerability details

Executing [WithdrawQueue::claim()](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Withdraw/WithdrawQueue.sol#L279) to complete the withdrawal process will almost always lead to instant changes in `ezETH` valuation.

Redeeming `ezETH` for underlying assets involves two steps:

1. User calls [WithdrawQueue::withdraw()](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Withdraw/WithdrawQueue.sol#L206), which:
    1. Transfers `ezETH` from the caller to the contract
    2. Caches the amount of assets to redeem based on the **current** `ezETH` valuation
2. After a delay, the user can call [WithdrawQueue::claim()](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Withdraw/WithdrawQueue.sol#L279), which:
    1. Burns the amount of `ezETH` previously transferred
    2. Transfers to the caller the amount of assets to redeem cached during step `1`

Because there is an uncapped delay in between step `1` and step `2`, when step `2` is executed the amount of `ezETH` burned might not be equivalent to the assets transferred out anymore, which will cause an instant change in the `ezETH` valuation.

## Impact

The valuation of `ezETH` should not increase (or decrease) instantly because this is not a situation where the protocol is receiving rewards (or penalties).

The instant increase of `ezETH` value can be abused by an attacker by depositing `ETH` in the protocol via [RestakeManager::depositETH()](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/RestakeManager.sol#L592) before a call to [WithdrawQueue::claim()](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Withdraw/WithdrawQueue.sol#L279) and withdrawing after the call.

Important to note that the delay between a call to `withdraw()` and `claim()` is uncapped and can be very long.

## Proof of Concept

Let's assume the protocol TVL is `2 ETH` and there are a total of `2 ezETH` in circulation, which implies `1 ezETH` is valued at `1 ETH`.

1. Alice calls [WithdrawQueue::withdraw()](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Withdraw/WithdrawQueue.sol#L206) to redeem `1 ezETH`, the protocol caches the current equivalent amount to be redeemed, `1 ETH`.
2. In between the calls the TVL of the protocol increases while the amount of `ezETH` in circulation stays constant, due to rewards. Let's assume the TVL doubled and is now `4 ETH` (unrealistic, but simple math).
3. Alice calls [WithdrawQueue::claim()](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Withdraw/WithdrawQueue.sol#L279), which burns `1 ezETH` and transfers out `1 ETH`.

Before step `3` was executed the protocol state was:

- `4 ETH` TVL
- `2 ezETH` in circulation
- `1 ezETH` = `2 ETH`

After step `3` is executed the protocol state is:

- `3 ETH` TVL
- `1 ezETH` in circulation
- `1 ezETH` = `3 ETH`

The valuation of `ezETH` instantly increased by `1ETH` after `claim()` was executed.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

When [WithdrawQueue::withdraw()](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Withdraw/WithdrawQueue.sol#L206) is executed the protocol should:

1. Burn the transferred `ezETH` tokens
2. Don't account for the amount of assets that will be redeemed as part of the TVL. They can be sent to an external contract that is responsible to keep the funds until they are claimed via [WithdrawQueue::claim()](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Withdraw/WithdrawQueue.sol#L279).

If this is implemented calls to [WithdrawQueue::claim()](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Withdraw/WithdrawQueue.sol#L279) will have no impact on the `ezETH` valuation, because both the amount of `ezETH` and the TVL won't change after the call.
