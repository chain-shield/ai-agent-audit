# Vulnerability details

## Bug description

Currently, users that want to withdraw assets from Renzo need to go through a queuing process that consists in two steps:

1. Calling `withdraw` in the `WithdrawQueue`: This will create a withdraw request for the user. In this step, the user’s `ezETH` will be transferred to the queue (**not burnt**), and the withdraw amount corresponding to the `ezETH` to redeem will be computed **using the current state of `ezETH's` supply and the current `totalTVL`.** This step includes two important concepts that need to be noted:
    - **`ezETH’s` supply is NOT reduced,** leaving the protocol state as is
    - The `amountToRedeem` is added in the `withdrawRequests` for the given user. This amount will be the actual assets that the user will receive when claiming
    
    ```solidity

            ...

            // calculate totalTVL
    ```

2. Claiming the queued withdrawal via `claim`: This second step will actually transfers the corresponding assets to the user. Note how **there is no calculation performed here to convert the `ezETH` to the amount to redeem by the user.** Instead, the values added in the `withdrawRequests` are used to determine the amount that the user should obtain for the `ezETH` amount he is burning.

Following this approach in order to perform withdrawals leads to an issue. As mentioned, no `ezETH` is burnt between step 1 and step 2. This means that `ezETH`'s `totalSupply` is not reduced during a queued withdrawal, so **all the exchange rate computations will consider that the amounts of `ezETH` queued during withdrawals should still be earning protocol rewards**. However, because the amount to be withdrawn to the user given the queued `ezETH` **is not recomputed when claiming** and instead the previously calculated `amountToRedeem` is withdrawn, the amount given to the user won’t consider the rewards earned during the queued withdrawal.

This leads to a situation where the time during a queued withdrawal makes users interacting with the protocol receive a wrong amount of shares, given that assets that should not be earning rewards are considered as if they were actually earning them, leading to a broken exchange rate.

## Impact

High. As shown in the proof of concept, the bug leads to two issues:

- Assets will be locked forever in the protocol due to the wrong exchange rate computations
- Yield earned during queued withdrawals will be wrongly distributed to users with queued withdrawals, making users without queued withdrawals earn a smaller amount of rewards than expected. The rewards corresponding to queued withdrawals can’t be withdrawn, so this leads to assets remain locked forever in the protocol.

## Proof of Concept

The following proof of concept shows the exchange rate issue during queued withdrawals.

- Prerequisites: Let’s consider a scenario where two users (`userOne` and `userTwo`) deposit 100 tokens each into the protocol. This will make `totalTVL` be 200, and `totalSupply` be 200 as well, and each of the users will receive 100 `ezETH` tokens each.
1. `userOne` decides to queue a withdrawal of his 100 `ezETH` tokens. Because `totalSupply` is 200 and `totalTVL` is also 200, `ezETH`'s price is 1, so `RenzoOracle`'s `calculateRedeemAmount` will compute `amountToRedeem` as 100. The following data will be queued in `userOne`'s `withdrawRequests`:
    1. `amountToRedeem` will be 100
    2. `ezETHLocked` will also be 100
2. After the queued withdrawal, the amount of assets held in the protocol increase by 100 additional tokens due to yield generated by validators/LST’s. This makes `totalTVL` increase from 200 to 300. However,`totalSupply` is still 200 **because no `ezETH` tokens were burnt when queuing the withdrawal in step 1.** It is important to note that the increase in the assets held in the protocol **should no longer be attributed to `userOne`,** given that he has already queued the withdrawal.
3. After some time (and before `userOne`'s cooldown period finishes), `userTwo` decides to queue a withdrawal for his 100 `ezETH`. Because `totalTVL` is 300 and `totalSupply` is still 200, `ezETH`'s price will be 1,5 (given by RenzoOracle’s `calculateRedeemAmount` computation, which is `totalTVL` / `ezETH’s` `totalSupply`). This will make the `amountToRedeem` computed for `userTwo` be 100 * 1,5 = 150. The following data will be queued in `userOne`'s `withdrawRequests`:
    1. `amountToRedeem` will be 150
    2. `ezETHLocked` will also be 100
    
    This is wrong, as the 100 additional tokens obtained in step 2 **should belong entirely to** `userTwo`, so the `amountToRedeem` for `userTwo` should actually be 200 instead of 150. 
    
4. Finally, the cooldown period passes for both `userOne` and `userTwo`. Both of them trigger the `claim` function:
    1. When `userOne` triggers `claim`, `ezETH`'s `totalSupply` will be reduced from 200 to 100 (because he queued `100 ezETH` to be withdrawn), and the `totalTVL` held in the protocol will be reduced from 300 to 200 (because the `amountToRedeem` computed for `userOne` was 100). `userOne` will effectively burn 100 `ezETH` in exchange for 100 underlying tokens.
    2. When `userTwo` triggers `claim`, `ezETH`'s `totalSupply` will be reduced from 100 to 0 (because he also queued `100 ezETH` to be withdrawn), however the `totalTVL` held in the protocol will be reduced from 200 to 50 (because the `amountToRedeem` computed for `userTwo` was 150, instead of 200). `userTwo` will burn 100 `ezETH` in exchange for 150 underlying tokens

As shown, this leaves the protocol in a state where the additional 100 underlying tokens earned during `userOne`'s queued withdrawal are wrongly computed as if they had to be distributed among `userOne` and `userTwo`, when in reality they should only correspond to `userTwo`. This leaves the protocol with 50 tokens locked forever, and `userTwo` earning less rewards than he should.

## Recommended Mitigation

When queuing withdrawals, burn the `ezETH` amount that is being queued directly. Additionally, substract `claimReserve` when computing the total TVL for the withdrawal queue:
