# Vulnerability details

## Impact

### deployOptimismMintableXERC20
The `deployOptimismMintableXERC20` function in the `OptimismMintableXERC20Factory` contract does not implement access control, allowing any address to deploy an OptimismMintableXERC20 contract. This lack of access control creates several potential issues and vulnerabilities.

Malicious actors may exploit this lack of access control to deploy fraudulent or malicious tokens, such as tokens with misleading names or symbols, tokens that mimic legitimate projects, or tokens with harmful functionality. These malicious tokens could deceive users and cause financial losses or reputational damage.

Unauthorized deployments of OptimismMintableXERC20 contracts without proper oversight or validation can lead to contract bloat, cluttering the blockchain with unnecessary or redundant contracts. This can increase gas costs for users and reduce the efficiency of the Ethereum network.

Also an attacker could programmatically invoke this function repeatedly, deploying a large number of OptimismMintableXERC20 contracts in a short period.  Each contract deployment transaction consumes gas, and deploying multiple contracts in quick succession can exhaust the gas limits of the Ethereum network. This could result in a backlog of transactions, delays in transaction processing, and increased transaction fees for legitimate users. 

A DoS attack targeting the OptimismMintableXERC20Factory contract could disrupt the operations of projects relying on this factory for deploying tokens. The increased transaction fees and delays caused by the attack may deter users from interacting with the affected projects, leading to economic losses and reputational damage.

### _deployOptimismMintableXERC20 

The `_deployOptimismMintableXERC20` function within the `OptimismMintableXERC20Factory` contract presents several issues. The function does not perform sufficient validation on its input parameters, such as `_minterLimits`, `_burnerLimits`, and `_bridges`. This lack of validation opens the door to potential errors or exploitation. For instance, if any of these arrays are of insufficient length or contain invalid addresses, the deployment process could fail or result in unexpected behavior. 

The salt used for contract deployment is generated using a combination of `_name`, `_symbol`, and `msg.sender`. While this approach may provide a degree of uniqueness to the salt, it's important to ensure that the combination of these parameters is sufficient to prevent collisions and predictable salt values. If an attacker can predict the salt value, they may be able to manipulate the contract deployment process or exploit other vulnerabilities.

Also in the `for` loop, it doesn't check whether `_bridges[_i]`, `_minterLimits[_i]`, & `_burnerLimits[_i]` are valid or not. They can also be zero and set to zero which is not the expected behaviour.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Add access control mechanisms to restrict the deployment of `OptimismMintableXERC20` contracts to authorized addresses only. Define roles and permissions for specific users or roles, such as administrators or trusted entities, who are allowed to deploy new contracts.

Also implement valiations on the params sent to `_deployOptimismMintableXERC20` function.
