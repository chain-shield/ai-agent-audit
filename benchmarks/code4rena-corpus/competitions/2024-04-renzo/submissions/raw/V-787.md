# Vulnerability details

## Impact
Under specific conditions, OperatorDelegators included in a further index may never receive deposits despite the set allocation. OperatorDelegators are discouraged to participate.

A small number of OperatorDelegators threatens the stability of the protocol and is inconsistent with the operational assumptions.

## Proof of Concept
During the deposit in `RestakeManager`, current TVLs are calculated [#L500-504](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/RestakeManager.sol#L500-L504). Based on them, it is determined to which `operatorDelegator` the funds should be transferred through the `chooseOperatorDelegatorForDeposit` [#L534](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/RestakeManager.sol#L534-L537) function.

The `chooseOperatorDelegatorForDeposit` [#L362-L393](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/RestakeManager.sol#L362-L393) function selects the FIRST `OperatorDelegator` with the TVL below the threshold OR returns the FIRST one in the list, as stated in the comment.

This approach under specific conditions will create unfair distribution.

Look at the example below:
1. We have 3 `OperatorDelegator`, each of them have the same allocation (1/3). The first deposit is 0,6 ETH. It automatically goes to the `operatorDelegator` located at the first index[0].

**Total TVL before deposit:** 0 ETH
**Before deposit:** Operator 1: 0/0 ETH         Operator 2: 0/0 ETH         Operator 3: 0/0 ETH
**After deposit:** Operator 1: 0,6/0,2 ETH         Operator 2: 0/0,2 ETH         Operator 3: 0/0,2 ETH
**Total TVL after deposit:** 0,6 ETH

2. As we can see, OperatorDelegator 1 exceeds their threshold, and the others are waiting for the next deposits. Now, someone deposits 3 ETH.

**Total TVL before deposit:** 0,6 ETH
**Before deposit:** Operator 1: 0,6/0,2 ETH         Operator 2: 0/0,2 ETH         Operator 3: 0/0,2 ETH

The OperatorDelegator 2 at index[1] is taken into account as the first one that does not have the threshold filled. So the situation changes to the following:

**After deposit:** Operator 1: 0,6/1,2 ETH         Operator 2: 3/1,2 ETH         Operator 3: 0/1,2 ETH
**Total TVL after deposit:** 3,6 ETH

3. The next deposit, regardless of its size, will again go to OperatorDelegator 1, index[0] as it will be below the threshold (0,6/1,2), which constantly increases with each deposit. Even though OperatorDelegator 3 has not received any funds yet.

If the deposit is large enough, it will increase the threshold of OperatorDelegator no. 2 index[1] and the funds can be handled by only 2 of the 3 OperatorDelegators.

**This scenario only illustrates this problem on a small scale. The more operators there are and the more diverse the deposits and withdrawals, the more significant the problem may be.**


## Tools Used
Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Choose OperatorDelegator for deposit proportionally based on the lowest allocation utilization to create incentives for new OperatorDelegators that rank further down the index[]. The funds should go to the OperatorDelegator who has the largest gap between the established allocation and the actually deposited capital.

Use a similar approach when selecting OperatorDelegator to withdraw, but in reverse. Pick OperatorDelegator with the highest allocation utilization rate.
