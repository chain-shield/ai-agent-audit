# Vulnerability details



## Impact
Standard inflation attack. First depositor can steal funds from subsequent depositors.

## Proof of Concept
In `RestakeManager.deposit()` the [amount of ezETH to mint](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/519e518f2d8dec9acf6482b84a181e403070d22d/contracts/RestakeManager.sol#L565-L569) is calculated by [`RenzoOracle.calculateMintAmount()`](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/519e518f2d8dec9acf6482b84a181e403070d22d/contracts/Oracle/RenzoOracle.sol#L123-L149). While written on a novel form this calculation simplifies to the standard `mintAmount = _existingEzETHSupply * _newValueAdded / _currentValueInProtocol` (albeit with the occasional extra wei rounding down; consider simplifying this). [`mintAmount == 0` reverts](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/519e518f2d8dec9acf6482b84a181e403070d22d/contracts/Oracle/RenzoOracle.sol#L146) so the best we can do for profit is to round down from `2` to `1`. That is, if we deposit one wei so `_existingEzETHSupply = 1`, and then inflate `_currentValueInProtocol` to half the next depositor's `_newValueAdded` (because of the extra rounding mentioned above we can inflate to exactly half rather than `1` above half). This can be done by [a direct transfer of tokens](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/519e518f2d8dec9acf6482b84a181e403070d22d/contracts/RestakeManager.sol#L319) or [ETH](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/519e518f2d8dec9acf6482b84a181e403070d22d/contracts/RestakeManager.sol#L352). Then `mintAmount` will be rounded down from a theoretical just below `2` to `1` and the next depositor thus receives only half the shares needed to represent his deposit. The attacker can then withdraw his share (out of a total two shares) of all deposited value (three times the donated amount), i.e. 1.5 times his investment. This is locked in on calling `WithdrawQueue.withdraw()` so the timeout period has no effect on the feasibility of this attack, except that the attacker will have to wait to receive the funds.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Consider making an initial deposit on deployment. Note that the inflation attack can also be used to DoS subsequent depositors and does not need to target a specific depositor, so the protective deposit must be atomic with or prior to deployment to preclude this.
