# Vulnerability details

## Impact
When users apply for a withdrawal, they deposit ezETH into the `WithdrawQueue`. In `RestakeManager.sol`, the function `renzoOracle.calculateMintAmount` calculates how much new ezETH to mint, taking into account the ezETH in the `WithdrawQueue`, and `calculateTVLs()` also accounts for the collateral in the WithdrawQueue.

https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/519e518f2d8dec9acf6482b84a181e403070d22d/contracts/RestakeManager.sol#L605-L609

https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/519e518f2d8dec9acf6482b84a181e403070d22d/contracts/RestakeManager.sol#L315-L321

The issue here is that the exchange ratio between ezETH and collateral is fixed at the time of withdrawal application. Any rewards earned by the protocol after this application do not affect this ratio. However, including these deposited ezETH in the TVL calculation can dilute subsequent users' staking rewards and can be manipulated to influence the price of ezETH.

Based on this vulnerability, an further attack scenario can be constructed, assuming the protocol's TVL is initially 0:
1. An attacker deposits ETH and immediately applies for a withdrawal without claiming it.
2. A victim deposits ETH, earning staking rewards for the protocol.
3. The attacker deposits more ETH and immediately executes the claim from step (1).

Through this attack process, the attacker can realize an instant profit in step three, derived from the staking rewards earned by the victim.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
In the `calculateTVLs` function of `RestakeManager.sol`, the calculation should exclude `claimReserve` from the `WithdrawQueue`, and the total supply of ezETH should also deduct the ezETH in the WithdrawQueue.
