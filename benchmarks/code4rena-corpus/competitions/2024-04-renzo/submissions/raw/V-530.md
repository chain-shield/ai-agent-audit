# Vulnerability details

## Cause
The [withdraw request ensures that sufficient funds are available](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Withdraw/WithdrawQueue.sol#L236) in the buffer, and the buffer [cannot be overfilled](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/main/contracts/Deposits/DepositQueue.sol#L299). At the same time, new deposits are withdrawable immediately at no cost. An attacker willing to DoS user withdrawals can frontrun all users' withdrawal transactions (of asset A) with their own transaction of deposit(asset B) + withdraw(asset A). Alternatively, the attacker can also backrun all buffer refills from deposits or EigenLayer withdrawals or rewards.

## Impact
A well-capitalized attacker can indefinitely DoS protocol withdrawals at no cost and exploit this to also achieve a depeg of ezETH relative to its underlying value, profiting from it. By preventing withdrawals, the attacker breaks the arbitrage cycle for ezETH's price. Due to the inability to withdraw funds, users willing to withdraw will have to trade out of ezETH instead via DEX or CEX. At the same time, arbitrageurs will not be able to complete withdrawals either, so they will not buy the ezETH sold by users. This will cause the ezETH price to depeg relative to its value.

The attack is highly likely because the attacker can directly profit from the depeg by preemptively shorting ezETH where available.

In addition to the depeg and DoS impacts, the protocol's indefinite withdrawal DoS will result in locking all users' funds for the duration of the attack, and will cause users to lose funds by having to trade out of ezETH at a reduced price.

Moreover, because only new deposits and EigenLayer withdrawals and rewards are used to replenish the buffers, increasing the buffer size by the admin and trying to fill the buffers at a higher pace via withdrawing additional funds from Eigenlayer will be delayed by at least 7 days (due to Eigenlayer's withdrawal delay). This allows the first 7 days of DoS to require only minimal capital that would need to, at most, match the organic pace of new deposits, which will also be greatly diminished by the attack.

After the same 7 days however, the previously queued withdrawals by the attacker become claimable, and available for recycling back into the attack.

## Proof of Concept
1. The attacker shorts ezETH (and REZ) - this is the profit motive.
2. A user submits a withdrawal request for asset A.
3. The attacker frontruns with a deposit of asset B and a withdrawal request for asset A. This "takes" the assets in the buffer.
4. The user's withdrawal request reverts due to insufficient assets available in the buffer.
5. After some time, ezETH depegs below its underlying value due to the inability to complete price arbitrage and increased selling pressure from users trading out instead of withdrawing.
6. Users' funds are locked for some users, and other users lose value by trading at a below-value price.
7. The attacker profits by closing the shorts.

Manual review.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Use the buffer for serving withdrawal claims, but allow withdrawal requests to be queued without the buffer requirement:

```diff
-    /// @dev mapping of claimReserve (already withdraw requested), indexed by token address
-    mapping(address => uint) public claimReserve;

    function withdraw(uint256 _amount, address _assetOut) external nonReentrant {
        ...
-        // revert if amount to redeem is greater than withdrawBufferTarget
-        if (amountToRedeem > getAvailableToWithdraw(_assetOut)) revert NotEnoughWithdrawBuffer();

    function claim(uint256 withdrawRequestIndex) external nonReentrant {
        ...
+        // revert if amount to redeem is greater than withdrawBufferTarget
+        if (_withdrawRequest.amountToRedeem > getAvailableToWithdraw(_withdrawRequest.collateralToken)) {
+            revert NotEnoughWithdrawBuffer();
+        }
```
