# Vulnerability details

## Impact
In `RestakeManager`, functions `deposit` and `depositETH` use `RenzoOracle#calculateMintAmount` to determine how much ezETH to mint to the sender. Although there's a [check](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/519e518f2d8dec9acf6482b84a181e403070d22d/contracts/Oracle/RenzoOracle.sol#L146) that reverts the transaction if 0 shares are going to be minted, it is still possible to execute the inflation attack. Ideally, the attacker would need to make 1 share worth `victimsDeposit / 2 + 1`, in order to maximize victim's loss due to rounding in price calculation.

## Proof of Concept

1. Bob is about to deposit 10e18 ETH into Renzo;
2. Alice frontruns Bob with 2 transactions: She deposits 10 wei, receives 10 wei of ezETH; Then she donates 5e18 * 10 = 50e18 ETH to DepositQueue (e.g. via "selfdestructing" a contract to avoid DepositQueue [fees](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-04-renzo/blob/519e518f2d8dec9acf6482b84a181e403070d22d/contracts/Deposits/DepositQueue.sol#L158-L183)*), so each share becomes worth (5e18 + 1) ETH;

3. Bob's deposit is executed and he receives `10e18 * 10 / (50e18 + 10) = 1` share;

4. Alice redeems back her shares and receives 54.545454545454545463e18 ETH, making 4.545e18 ETH in profit.

*`selfdestruct` reverts the state changes if it runs out of gas (but still updates the balance). As `DepositQueue#receive()` consumes too much gas to fit into selfdestruct's gas limit, the fee will not be applied.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

Consider implementing virtual shares: they would absorb the donation, making the attack unprofitable; or implement slippage protection for deposits.
