# Vulnerability details

## Impact
Block reorgs can disrupt cross-chain transfers, leading to potential double-spending, loss of funds, or incorrect token supply.[Read here](https://abarbatei.xyz/blockchain-reorgs-for-managers-and-auditors)

## Proof of Concept

Multiple users, including Alice, have deposited WETH into the xRenzoDeposit contract on Chain A (L2), which has been swapped for a total of 1000 `nextWETH`.


Initial State:

Multiple users, including Alice, have deposited WETH into the `xRenzoDeposit` contract on Chain A (L2), which has been swapped for a total of 1000 nextWETH.

Bridging Process:

A sweep function is called, which triggers `connext.xcall` to bridge the entire balance of 1000 nextWETH to Chain B (L1).

Chain B Processing:

The `xRenzoBridge` contract on Chain B receives the `nextWETH`, unwraps it to WETH, and proceeds with restaking operations.

Blockchain Reorg: A reorg occurs on Chain A after the `xcall` is made but before the transaction is finalized. The deposit transaction that initiated the bridge is now reverted.

Resulting State: On Chain B, the `xRenzoBridge` contract has already unwrapped nextWETH to WETH and possibly completed restaking operations. However, due to the reorg on Chain A, the original WETH deposit no longer exists on L2.

There is now `WETH` on `Chain B` that is unbacked by an equivalent deposit on `Chain A`, leading to a discrepancy between the two chains. Essentially, Chain B has extra `WETH` that wasn't actually locked or bridged from Chain A due to the reorg. This creates an imbalance where more `WETH` exists on `L1` than is backed by L2 deposits, potentially affecting the integrity of the token peg and the restaking process.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

Waiting for more confirmations: Chain B's contract could wait for a higher number of confirmations from Chain A before minting wrapped tokens, reducing the risk of acting on a transaction that might be dropped.

Reorg detection: Both chains implement mechanisms to detect reorgs and temporarily halt cross-chain operations until the network stabilizes.

State reconciliation: After detecting a reorg, Chain B's contract could verify the state of Chain A to ensure the lock transaction is still valid before minting wrapped tokens.

Fallback procedures: If a reorg invalidates a transfer, Chain B's contract could have a mechanism to burn the incorrectly minted wrapped tokens to correct the token supply.
