# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The `EzEthToken.sol` contract does not have proper handling for zero-amount token transfers when the contract is not paused. This could lead to potential issues in certain integrations and operations.

The lack of handling for zero-amount transfers in the EzEthToken contract could result in the following impacts:

Unexpected Behavior: Some integrations or applications may expect the token contract to revert on zero-amount transfers, as this is a common practice to ensure consistent behavior. The `EzEthToken` contract not reverting on these transfers could lead to unexpected behavior in these integrations.

Unnecessary Gas Consumption:
Allowing zero amount transfers could lead to unnecessary gas consumption, as the protocol would still need to process the transactions and update the internal state, even though no actual value is being transferred.
This could increase the overall gas costs for users and potentially degrade the protocol's performance and efficiency.

Potential Exploitation of Edge Cases:
The lack of restrictions on zero amount transfers could also lead to unexpected edge cases or unintended behavior in the protocol's logic.
Attackers might be able to exploit these edge cases to achieve undesirable outcomes, such as manipulating the protocol's internal state or triggering unexpected actions.

Usability Issues: Some operations, like complex swap algorithms in other protocols using this token, may rely on the token contract to revert on zero-amount transfers. The lack of this behavior could introduce usability issues or complexities in integrating the `EzEthToken` token.

Potential Denial-of-Service Attacks: Malicious actors could exploit the lack of zero-amount transfer handling by spamming the protocol with a large number of zero-amount transfers, causing a significant increase in gas consumption and potentially leading to a denial-of-service (DoS) attack on the protocol.

## Proof of Concept
Deploy a malicious contract that interacts with the `EzEthToken` contract.

In the malicious contract, implement a function that performs a zero-amount transfer of EzEthToken tokens to a specific address (e.g., the zero address).

Invoke the zero-amount transfer function from the malicious contract.

This proves the contract does not revert the transaction despite the zero-amount transfer.
