# Vulnerability details

## Vulnerability Details

The callContractWithToken and callContract functions do not perform any validation on the destinationAddress parameter. This allows a malicious user to provide an incorrect or malicious contract address, potentially leading to the irreversible loss of transferred tokens or other assets.

## Impact

Loss of Funds: If a user mistakenly or intentionally provides an incorrect destinationAddress, the cross-chain call will interact with an unintended contract. If this contract is malicious or has unexpected behavior, it could lead to the loss of the tokens transferred in the callContractWithToken function.

## Scenario

A user, either by mistake or with malicious intent, provides an incorrect destinationAddress when calling callContractWithToken. This address doesn't belong to the intended contract on the destination chain.

The Axelar network processes the cross-chain call, but instead of reaching the intended contract, it reaches the contract located at the incorrect destinationAddress.

If the contract at the incorrect address is malicious, it could be designed to automatically seize any tokens sent to it. The transferred tokens would be lost to the user.

The malicious contract could analyze the payload (the data intended for the original contract) and execute unintended actions. It might find ways to further drain funds or manipulate the state of the contract, potentially causing additional losses.

Even if the contract isn't explicitly malicious, it might have unexpected behavior. For instance, it could lock the transferred tokens in a way that makes them irretrievable, effectively resulting in a loss for the user.


## Why this is Problematic:

Cross-chain transactions are typically irreversible. Once the tokens are transferred to the incorrect contract, it might be impossible to retrieve them, especially if the contract is malicious or doesn't have a mechanism to return funds.
The user loses control over their tokens once they are sent to the incorrect contract. The behavior of that contract, whether malicious or simply unexpected, dictates the fate of the tokens.
The burden of providing the correct destinationAddress lies with the user. The contract itself, without proper input validation, cannot prevent interactions with unintended contracts.


## Recommendation

Implement strict validation on the destinationAddress parameter to ensure it points to a legitimate and intended contract on the destination chain. If the GatewayCaller is designed to interact with a limited set of known contracts on the destination chain, maintain a whitelist of valid destinationAddress values and enforce checks against this whitelist.
