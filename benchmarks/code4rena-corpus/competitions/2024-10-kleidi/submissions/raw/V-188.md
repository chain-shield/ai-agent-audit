# Vulnerability details

## Proof of Concept

Safe can schedule different operations so that after the required delay those operation can be executed by anyone.

These operations typically involve timelock functions, enabling Safe owners to update the state variables of the timelock.

However, this setup could lead to an undesirable scenario:

1. `Safe` owners may decide to use `removeCalldataChecks()` to remove all `callDataChecks` from a function of a `target contract`, followed by using `addCallDataChecks()` to add new `callDataChecks` in the same block. This could happen when they need to quickly recover funds from external contracts.

2. The `Safe` schedules these operations by calling `_schedule()`, providing the hashed IDs and necessary parameters for both functions

3. Once the delay has passed and both operations are ready for execution, an `attacker` could `frontrun` the execution by ensuring that `addCallDataChecks()` is executed first. They could then choose whether or not to execute `removeCallDataChecks()` afterward.

This would force the `Safe` owners to `reschedule` the `addCallDataChecks()`, potentially leading to significant losses as they may fail to secure funds from a protocol in time.

This vulnerability could have severe consequences for Safe owners, such as losing control over funds.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

Implement a mechanism to track the order of scheduled executions, especially when Safe owners are scheduling timelock functions, to prevent operations from being frontrun and executed out of order.
