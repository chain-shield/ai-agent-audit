# Vulnerability details

### Impact

Some of the functions that query the `swap_internal` (including itself) are missing the check for minimum amount out and for deadline check. As a result, transactions involving the function are vulnerable to sandwich attacks due to lac of slippage control.

### Proof of Concept

1. Context

Interactions with AMMs should provide their users with an option to provie the minimum amount of tokens they're willing to receive during swap. This is because, due to various on chain conditions, a user expecting to swap at a particular price, might end up swapping at a different, potentially much lower price. This issue is also excarcibated by flahsbots, mevbots and other malicious swappers which monitor various user transations to execute a sandwich attack on any unsuspecting victim. Also, a limit on the execution time of their pending swap is required as malicious miners/validators can hold the swap transaction and execute it at a later time when it would return a reduced token amount than if it had been executed immediately. Without these protections in place, users can unknowingly perform bad trades.

2. Bug Location

3. Final Effect
 
Users attempting through these functions can have their transactions frontrun and sandwiched, causing them to make swaps at a worse price than expected when they initially submitted the transaction. They have no control in how much they get and no time protection which means the trade can be delayed by miners or users congesting the network, as well as being sandwich attacked - ultimately leading to loss of user funds. Since the pool is also like uniswaps', slippage control is also important to ensure that the pool does not get fully empty. 

### Tools Used
Manual code review
 
### Recommended Mitigation Steps

Recommend introducing a `minAmtOut` and `deadline` parameter to help handle these scenarios.
