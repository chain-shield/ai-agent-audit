forwardRevenue does not have a check to ensure erc20s.length > 0,  the impact of length being 0 is that rTokens will be minted but not transferred to revenue traders
```solidity
        uint192 baskets = (basketsHeld.bottom.div(FIX_ONE + backingBuffer));

        for (uint256 i = 0; i < length; ++i) {
            IAsset asset = assetRegistry.toAsset(erc20s[i]);
```
So these fresh rTokens which represent yield stay in backing manager, which is problematic cause in the rebalance function if backingManager hold any non dust rTokens they will be [dissolved](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-07-reserve/blob/3f133997e186465f4904553b0f8e86ecb7bbacbf/contracts/p1/BackingManager.sol#L131).
Consider the scenario-
Assumptions- Entire system is healthy & over-collateralized
1. there is some 100k tvl, & let say 200 $ of unclaimed claimable yield (i.e revenue above backingBuffer)
2.  Now governance changes weights or tokens in primeBasket, but attacker calls **forwardRevenue([])** first & 200 $ worth of rToken are minted to backing manager, due to erc20s input array not containing anything these rTokens will stay in custody of backingManager
3. Now setPrimeBasket call comes in & so does the rebalance() call, but in rebalance
```solidity
if (balance >= MAX_DISTRIBUTION * MAX_DESTINATIONS) rToken.dissolve(balance);
```
leading to freezing of unclaimed yield
**These rTokens which were supposed to give revenue to stakers & rToken holders are now used up for rebalance**

 Always transfer rToken balance to revenueTraders irrespective of rToken in the array or not when forwardRevenue() is called, safer to keep it with revenueTraders instead of backingManager
