# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The current implementation of `seizeRSR` function allows for the seizure of `RSR` from users’ unpaid rewards, which contradicts the expectation that only staked `RSR` (and `draftRSR` - not completely withdrawn) should be at risk. This leads to a scenario where users’ earned rewards—intended as an incentive for staking their `RSR`—are unjustly taken during a seizure. The impact could be significant as it discourages staking by putting both the principal and the earned rewards at risk, leading to user dissatisfaction and reduced participation in `stRSR` staking.

## Proof of Concept
According to the contest details:
> Staked RSR can be seized in the case of a default, in a process that is entirely mechanistic based on on-chain price-feeds, and does not depend on governance votes or human judgment. 
> But markets do not over-collateralize holders for free. In order to incentivize RSR holders to stake in an RToken instance, each RToken instance can choose to offer an arbitrary portion of its revenue to be directed towards its RSR over-collateralization pool. This encourages staking in order to provision over-collateralization.

Consider a user who stakes their `RSR` for 30 days, during which rewards that are sent to the contract but have not yet been distributed (i.e. not yet added to `stakeRSR`). If a seizure occurs during this period, the `seizeRSR` function will not only seize part of the staked `RSR` but also a portion or all of the rewards. This means the user’s 30-day earnings, which should be theirs for taking the risk of staking, are unfairly taken alongside their stake.

```ruby
// Remove RSR from yet-unpaid rewards (implicitly)
seizedRSR += (rewards * rsrAmount + (rsrBalance - 1)) / rsrBalance;

// updates the last payout rewards (i.e. that will paid out next time _payoutRewards() is called)
rsrRewardsAtLastPayout = rsrRewards() - seizedRSR;
```
Using a simple example from the docs, to see how users lose their rewards for a period. Suppose (these numbers are just made up for simplicity, to make the arithmetic clear):  

* A fictional `RToken` generated $100 in revenue in a period
* 20% of revenue was designated for `RSR` stakers
* 1000 total `RSR` was staked
* You had staked 100 of the total 1000 `RSR` that is staked
* In this simple example, you would get `$100 * 20% * (100/1000) = $2` for that period.
* However, if some `RSR` were seized (assume 50% i.e. total `RSR` now = 500, and generated revenue now = 10% and your stake reduced to 50 `RSR`)
* You would now get `$100 * 10% * (50/500) = $1` for that period, which means $1 of revenue generated for that period is seized.
* Assume the generated revenue was untouched then you would now get `$100 * 20% * (50/500) = $2` for that period which properly and rightly incentivises the staked period despite a seizure.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
To address this issue, modify the `seizeRSR` function to exclude yet-unpaid rewards from the seizure process. Users' rewards should be protected and only the staked `RSR` (and `draftRSR` - not completely withdrawn) should be subject to seizure. However, if the yet-unpaid rewards must be seized, it should only be when `if (stakeRSR == 0 || stakeRate > MAX_STAKE_RATE)` is true (since rewards should be for users with available stakes).
