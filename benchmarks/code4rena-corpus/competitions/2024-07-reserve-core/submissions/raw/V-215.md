# Vulnerability details

Impact
A Denial of Service (DoS) attack on the rebalance function could prevent the contract from executing necessary trades to maintain collateralization. This could lead to undercollateralization, affecting the stability and trust in the RToken. If the rebalance function is consistently blocked, it could prevent the protocol from maintaining its intended asset backing, leading to potential financial loss and a decrease in the protocol's reliability.

Proof of Concept
Code Reference:
The vulnerable code is located in the rebalance function:

Potential DoS Scenario:
Future Timestamp Manipulation: An attacker could repeatedly call rebalance and manipulate the tradeEnd[kind] to always set it to a future timestamp. This would prevent subsequent rebalancing operations since the rebalance function would fail the check tradeEnd[kind] < block.timestamp.

Persistent Open Trades: If tradesOpen is never zero due to malicious or faulty trades, the rebalance function cannot proceed. This could be exploited to block necessary rebalancing indefinitely, leading to potential collateralization issues.

Recommended Mitigation Steps
Rate Limiting: Implement rate limiting on rebalance calls to prevent abuse. This could involve limiting how frequently rebalance can be called per user or adding a cooldown period between calls.

Trade Monitoring: Ensure that tradesOpen is accurately tracked and that trades are resolved promptly. This might involve additional monitoring and auditing mechanisms to detect and resolve stalled or malicious trades.

Fallback Mechanism: Introduce a fallback mechanism that can force the resolution of trades or allow a privileged contract or governance action to override certain conditions if the system is stuck in an undesirable state. This ensures that rebalancing can occur even in the face of certain blocking conditions.
