# Vulnerability details

## Impact
A  single failing asset can render the entire protocol unusable, locking user funds and breaking core functionalities. If a single collateral token in the basket causes functions in its corresponding asset contract to fail, it can break the entire ``RToken`` functionality. This is due to the tight coupling between these contracts and their dependency on the AssetRegistry's ``refresh()`` function, which calls ``refresh()`` on all registered assets.
The BackingManagerP1, RTokenP1, and BasketHandlerP1 contracts, assume that all registered asset contracts will behave correctly at all times. This assumption can be incorrect due to various reasons such as upgrades, hacks, or intentional changes in the asset contracts. If this vulnerability manifests users might be unable to redeem ```RTokens`` for any collateral, unable to issue RTokens, update the basket and Stakers unable to unstake.
For example, if a basket holds two collateral assets [AssetA, AssetB] where AssetA holds 1% of the RToken collateral and AssetB holds 99%, and AssetA gets compromised, the entire 100% of the funds (including the 99% in AssetB) will be permanently locked and the RToken will be unusable.

Assume AssetA (representing 1% of the basket) is registered in the AssetRegistry.
- AssetA's contract is compromised and self-destructs.
- Any call to AssetA's contract will now fail.
- When ``BackingManagerP1.rebalance()`` is called, it triggers ``assetRegistry.refresh()``.
- ``assetRegistry.refresh()`` attempts to call ``refresh()`` on all assets, including AssetA.
- The call to AssetA's ``refresh()`` fails, causing the entire ``assetRegistry.refresh()`` to fail.
- This failure cascades, breaking rebalance(), issue(), redeem(), and other critical functions.
- The protocol becomes unusable, with all funds effectively locked.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

Implement a try-catch mechanism in the AssetRegistry's ``refresh()`` function to handle failures of individual assets gracefully. Add a mechanism to allow governance to forcefully unregister a compromised asset, bypassing the normal checks that depend on the asset's functionality. Also consider implementing a circuit breaker pattern that can isolate failing assets without compromising the entire system
