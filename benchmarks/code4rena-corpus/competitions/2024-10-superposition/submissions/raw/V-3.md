# Vulnerability details

The contract allows users to create liquidity positions using the create_position function. However, there are no limits enforced on the number of positions a user can create or on the total number of positions in the pool. This opens the contract to a potential Denial of Service (DoS) attack, where an attacker could create an excessive number of positions, resulting in high gas costs, bloating the contract's storage, and rendering the pool unusable for legitimate users.
Risk Impact:
The absence of position limits can lead to two major consequences:

Denial of Service (DoS): An attacker could spam the contract with an extremely high number of positions, consuming excessive amounts of gas and making interactions with the pool (such as adding liquidity, swapping tokens, or withdrawing funds) prohibitively expensive for legitimate users. In extreme cases, this could cause the pool to become completely unusable.

Storage Bloat: Each position created takes up space in the contract's storage. By allowing users to create positions indefinitely, the contract's storage will grow uncontrollably. This would increase gas costs for future transactions that read or iterate through the positions, further exacerbating the DoS effect.

Exploitation Scenario:
An attacker with sufficient resources (gas fees) could exploit this vulnerability by creating a large number of positions in the pool using a simple loop.

POC:
for i in 0..1000000 {
    pool.create_position(U256::from(i), -i, i);
}

In this scenario, each call to create_position would add a new entry to the contract’s storage, consuming gas and expanding the storage size. As the number of positions increases, gas costs for interactions with the pool would rise, potentially preventing legitimate users from performing operations due to excessively high gas fees or gas limits being exceeded.

Skill Required: Moderate. Any attacker capable of interacting with the contract can perform this attack by writing a script to spam positions.
Motivation: High. An attacker could execute this attack to disrupt the operation of a pool, especially in decentralized finance (DeFi) contexts where disrupting liquidity pools can lead to financial losses or trading disruptions.
Impact:
High

Operational Impact: Complete or partial Denial of Service. The pool could become unusable for legitimate users due to skyrocketing gas fees or the inability to call certain functions.
Financial Impact: Potential loss of revenue for liquidity providers and traders due to the inability to interact with the pool.
