S-497
The `SwapAutomator::_convertToLink()` can return incorrect `linkAmount` due to the fed price decimal mismatch vulnerability
serial-coder
78
Sufficient
383d ago
Finding Description

This report describes a fed price decimal mismatch vulnerability in the SwapAutomator contract.

Meanwhile, the _getAssetPrice() (see @1) is called by the checkUpkeep() to obtain asset prices. The _getValidatedAssetPrice() (@2) is called by the checkUpkeep(), performUpkeep(), and swapWithPriceFeedValidation().

In reality, the prices obtained from Chainlink feeds can be represented using different reported decimals for various assets. For instance, the LINK price is represented using 8 decimals, whereas the PEPE price is represented using 18 decimals (see below for proof).

        //          whereas the PEPE price is represented using 18 decimals.

Note, per the contest's Automated Findings / Publicly Known Issues section:

Malicious token processing: all tokens will be vetted before onboarding them to the system. Malicious tokens are not a concern. Due to all tokens being reviewed, upgradability / pausability / blocklists / balance modifications / fee token transfers are out of scope.

The assets listed above are just examples, and they are not malicious tokens (also supported by Chainlink feeds). Furthermore, this report does not refer to the upgradability, pausability, blocklists, balance modifications, or fee token transfers feature. Moreover, nowhere in the contest's readme, publicly known issues, or source code mentioned that the sponsor acknowledged this vulnerability.

Referring to the snippet below, if the assetPrice (@3.1) and linkUSDPrice (@3.2) are represented in different reported decimals, for example, the PEPE price is fed using 18 decimals whereas the LINK price is fed using 8 decimals, the computation (see @4.1 and @4.2) in the _convertToLink() will be incorrect, resulting in returning an incorrect linkAmount.

The root cause is that the _convertToLink() wrongly assumes that the fed assetPrice and linkUSDPrice must always be represented using the exact 8 decimals.

Please refer to the Proof of Concept section for math proofs.

Finding Impact

The checkUpkeep() will always skip processing the affected assets (e.g., PEPE, SHIB, ULTI, BONK, SATS, etc.) and refuse to include them in the performData payload. Please consider the following for more details.

When the checkUpkeep() is executed:
The checkUpkeep() retrieves the linkUSDPrice from the _getValidatedAssetPrice(i_linkUsdFeed).
The checkUpkeep() obtains the assetPrice from the _getAssetPrice(swapParams.oracle).
The checkUpkeep() calculates the amountOutCLPriceFeedQuote by executing the _convertToLink(swapAmountIn, assetPrice, linkUSDPrice, IERC20Metadata(asset)).
Due to the fed price decimal mismatch issue, the resulting amountOutCLPriceFeedQuote will be significantly greater than the expected value, causing the amountOutUniswapQuote to always be below the amountOutCLPriceFeedQuote with the maxSlippage applied (i.e., does not pass the slippage check). Subsequently, the checkUpkeep() will always skip processing the affected assets and refuse to include them in the performData payload.

The following case is unlikely to happen since the checkUpkeep() will not include the affected assets in the performData payload. However, assume that the affected assets are forced to be included in the performData payload off-chain. The swapWithPriceFeedValidation() will always skip the affected assets from swapping. Please consider the following for more details.

When the performUpkeep() is executed:
The performUpkeep() obtains the linkPriceFromFeed from the _getValidatedAssetPrice(i_linkUsdFeed).
The performUpkeep() executes the sub-call transaction to the swapWithPriceFeedValidation(swapInputs[i], asset, linkPriceFromFeed) (in a try-catch statement) to swap each asset specified in the performData payload.
The swapWithPriceFeedValidation() retrieves the assetPriceFromPriceFeed from the _getValidatedAssetPrice(swapParams.oracle).
The swapWithPriceFeedValidation() calculates the linkAmountOutFromPriceFeed by invoking the _convertToLink(swapInput.amountIn, assetPriceFromPriceFeed, linkPriceFromFeed, IERC20Metadata(asset)).
Due to the fed price decimal mismatch issue, the resulting linkAmountOutFromPriceFeed will be significantly greater than the expected value, causing the amountOut to always be below the linkAmountOutFromPriceFeed with the maxPriceDeviation applied (i.e., does not pass the price deviation check). Consequently, the swapWithPriceFeedValidation() will always revert the sub-call transaction when processing the affected assets.
Since the performUpkeep() executes the swapWithPriceFeedValidation() in a try-catch statement. The transaction will not be reverted. In other words, the swapWithPriceFeedValidation() will always skip the affected assets from swapping.

Please refer to the Proof of Concept section for math proofs.

Proof of Concept

This section describes some math proofs.

The following are the on-chain data at the time of writing:

The current LINK price is 29.47754 USD (fed by the LINK/USD feed with 8 decimal precision)
The current PEPE price is 0.000024255457 USD (fed by the PEPE/USD feed with 18 decimal precision)
On Optimism, the LINK/USD feed reports the price using 8 decimals (ref #7)
On Optimism, the PEPE/USD feed reports the price using 18 decimals (ref #4)
The LINK token decimals are 18 (i.e., the LINK_DECIMALS variable below)
The PEPE token decimals are 18 (i.e., the tokenDecimals variable below)

The remaining of this section can be categorized into three sub-sections:

Proof #1 -- Simple Calculation
Proof #2 -- Incorrect Calculation in the _convertToLink()
Proof #3 -- Correct Calculation (Fixed Version)
Proof #1 -- Simple Calculation

With the above pre-setting, we can simply calculate the amount of LINK we will receive if we swap 1 PEPE (without accounting for both tokens' decimals as they have identical token decimals), as in the snippet below.

linkAmount = (pepeAmount * pepePrice) / linkPrice
           = (1 * 0.000024255457) / 29.47754
           ~= 0.00000082284536 LINK
Proof #2 -- Incorrect Calculation in the _convertToLink()

This proof explains the vulnerability resided in the _convertToLink().

The root cause is that the _convertToLink() wrongly assumes that the fed assetPrice and linkUSDPrice must always be represented using the exact 8 decimals.

With the above pre-setting, please thoroughly consider the calculation.

assetAmount = 1 * 1e18 // 1 PEPE

assetPrice = 24255457000000 (fed by the PEPE/USD feed with 18 decimal precision)
           = 0.000024255457 USD

linkUSDPrice = 2947754000 (fed by the LINK/USD feed with 8 decimal precision)
             = 29.47754 * 1e8
             = 29.47754 USD

tokenDecimals = 18 // PEPE
LINK_DECIMALS = 18 // LINK

// Since the LINK and PEPE tokens have the exact token decimals (18), 
// the formula in the "else" case of the _convertToLink() is taken.
linkAmount = (assetAmount * assetPrice) / linkUSDPrice / 10 ** (tokenDecimals - LINK_DECIMALS)
           = (1 * 1e18) * (24255457000000) / (2947754000) / 10 ** (18 - 18)
           = 8.22845359552 * 1e21
           = 8228.45359552 * 1e18
           = 8228.45359552 LINK

As you can see, if we swap 1 PEPE, we will receive 8228.45359552 LINK. Compared to Proof #1's result, 0.00000082284536 LINK, the _convertToLink() returns a 10,000,000,000 (i.e., 1e10) times more enormous value (due to the 10 decimal differences between the fed assetPrice and linkUSDPrice).

Proof #3 -- Correct Calculation (Fixed Version)

This proof explains how to fix the vulnerability.

We can fix the vulnerability by adjusting the decimal precisions of the fed assetPrice and linkUSDPrice. In this example, we scale the linkUSDPrice up by 1e10 (i.e., adjustedLinkUSDPrice in the snippet below) to match the assetPrice's decimal precision.

// Adjust the fed LINK price's decimal precision (1e8) to match 
// the fed PEPE price's decimal precision (1e18) by multiplying by 1e10
adjustedLinkUSDPrice = 2947754000 * 1e10

linkAmount = (assetAmount * assetPrice) / adjustedLinkUSDPrice / 10 ** (tokenDecimals - LINK_DECIMALS)
           = (1 * 1e18) * (24255457000000) / (2947754000 * 1e10) / 10 ** (18 - 18)
           = 822845359552
           = 0.000000822845359552 * 1e18
           = 0.000000822845359552 LINK (approximately equal to the result from Proof #1, but with a higher precision)

As a result, if we swap 1 PEPE, we will receive 0.000000822845359552 LINK. Compared to Proof #1's result, 0.00000082284536 LINK, this result is approximately equal to Proof #1 (but with a higher precision).

Recommended Mitigation Steps

Improve the _convertToLink() by adjusting the decimal precisions of the fed assetPrice and linkUSDPrice to be equal before calculating the linkAmount. Proof #3 in the Proof of Concept section can be an improvement idea.

Low Downgrade Verdict

0xsomeone

After taking a closer look at this submission in light of each warden's comments, I believe that a QA (L) severity rating is more appropriate.

I failed to focus on the maximum impact of the submission; swaps simply failing to execute for a particular asset that can be rescued. This impact directly reduces the submission's severity as non-8 decimal price feeds are rarely configured for assets, and those assets would still be usable in such a circumstance.

As a result, I have downgraded this submission's severity to QA (L) and consider it finalized.
