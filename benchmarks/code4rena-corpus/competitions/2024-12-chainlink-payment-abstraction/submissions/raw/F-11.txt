S-288
`SwapAutomator` assumes heartbeat of 24 hours for all price feeds
zanderbyte
100
Sufficient
383d ago
Finding description and impact

The SwapAutomator contract uses a hardcoded staleness threshold of 24 hours for all price feeds:

  /// @dev The threshold is set to 1 day to match the Chainlink feeds heartbeat requirement
  uint256 private constant STALENESS_THRESHOLD = 1 days;

This does not account for the fact that different price feeds have varying heartbeat intervals. For example:

The ETH/USD price feed on Ethereum Mainnet has a heartbeat of 1 hour, meaning prices should not be considered valid after this interval.

When a price feed becomes stale, but remains within the hardcoded STALENESS_THRESHOLD, the system incorrectly assumes the data is valid. This can lead to operations being executed based on outdated and potentially inaccurate price. This issue becomes even more problematic in scenarios involving volatile assets, where accurate pricing is critical.

Proof of Concept
  function _getValidatedAssetPrice(
    AggregatorV3Interface oracle
  ) private view returns (uint256 assetPrice) {
    (uint256 answer, uint256 updatedAt) = _getAssetPrice(oracle);

    if (answer == 0) revert Errors.ZeroOracleData();
    if (updatedAt < block.timestamp - STALENESS_THRESHOLD) {
      revert Errors.StaleOracleData();
    }

Consider the following scenario:

The ETH/USD price feed has a heartbeat of 1 hour.
The price data becomes stale after 3 hours due to an oracle issue.
Since the STALENESS_THRESHOLD is hardcoded to 24 hours, the _getValidatedAssetPrice function will not detect the staleness and will allow the operation to proceed using outdated price data.
Recommended mitigation steps

Replace the hardcoded STALENESS_THRESHOLD with a dynamic mechanism that uses real staleness thresholds for each price feed. Introducing a mapping to store these thresholds is an appropriate and effective mitigation.

serial-coder

Appeal. This is a valid medium issue.

Case 1 -- When price feed's heartbeat < 24 hours (e.g., 1 hour)

The vulnerability allows attackers to perform MEV and pool manipulation attacks, leading to the loss of funds to the protocol.

Please consider the following scenario:

Assume that while invoking the checkUpkeep(), the performData is generated with a valid asset price of $10.
The asset price moves up to $15, for instance, but Chainlink's network fails to update the asset price on-chain. Thus, the reported asset price becomes stale.
The performUpkeep() is executed.

The Chainlink feed reports a stale asset price of $10 (the actual price is $15).

The swapWithPriceFeedValidation() consumes the stale price.

Subsequently, the function fails to enforce the price deviation/slippage check. Instead of reverting a transaction, the function allows the swap to perform. Since the actual asset price is now $15 but the reported price is only $10, an attacker profits from the MEV attack.

In other words, the vulnerability allows attackers to perform MEV and pool manipulation attacks, leading to the loss of funds to the protocol.

Case 2 -- When price feed's heartbeat > 24 hours (e.g. 48 hours)

Some price feed can have a heartbeat greater than 24 hours. For example, on Ethereum, the AMPL / USD feed has a configured heartbeat of 172800s (i.e., 48 hours).

Consequently, the checkUpkeep() will skip processing the asset and refuse to include it in the performData payload.

The following case is unlikely to happen since the checkUpkeep() will not include the asset in the performData payload. However, assume that the asset is forced to be included in the performData payload off-chain. The swapWithPriceFeedValidation() will skip the asset from swapping (due to transaction revert).

For this reason, setting a proper heartbeat for each price feed is necessary. Hence, this issue is a valid medium.

0xsomeone

The submission and its duplicates outline that fixed data staleness for oracle queries might be insecure for certain asset classes that have an update frequency that is more aggressive than 24 hours.

I do not consider the severity of this item to be medium given its impact. Specifically, the price movement in the delta between the 24 hours threshold and the update frequency of the oracle would be permitted (i.e. in the most extreme cases a delta of 23 hours).

A negative price movement in this time window would cause the swap to be skipped, resulting in no impact on the system's operation.

A positive price movement in this time window would result in the Uniswap AMM price measurement to be higher than the oracle one thus causing it to be used as the swap output's control variable. We need to keep in mind that the checkUpkeep function is invoked externally and thus cannot be manipulated in a cost-efficient manner.

I do not believe that there has been an instance of an oracle misbehaving for positive price movements and even in those cases, the impact would be negligible. A price update can presently occur between the invocation of the checkUpkeep and performUpkeep functions, and this deviation is acknowledged by the Chainlink team.

Combining the above thoughts, I believe this issue is better suited as a valid albeit QA (L) recommendation that the Chainlink team should consider but may wish to acknowledge.
