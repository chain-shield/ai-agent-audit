# Vulnerability details

## Impact

A fixed liquidation reward system can lead to larger debts going unliquidated. For instance, no one would liquidate a 20 ETH debt for a 0.2 ETH reward when they can liquidate a 4 ETH debt for the same reward. Also most liquidators are bots which calculate profit percentages and will avoid liquidating a 20 ETH debt for a 0.2 ETH reward, which yields only a 1% profit.

## Proof of Concept

Scenario 1:
1. Alice opens a position with 4 ETH (valued at $13,219.36 at the current price).
2. Alice's position become liquidatable.
3. Bob notices this and liquidates Alice's position by paying 4 ETH and receives 0.2 ETH as a liquidator reward.

This scenario is typical and frequently occurs.

Scenario 2:
1. Alice opens a position with 20 ETH (valued at $66,078.60 at the current price).
2. Alice's position become liquidatable.
3. Bob notices this but needs 20 ETH ($66,078.60) to liquidate, and he will receive only 0.2 ETH ($660.79).

In Scenario 2, Bob decides not to liquidate Alice's position because the 0.2 ETH reward represents only a 1% profit, which is not attractive enough.

## Tools Used

- Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

Implement a dynamic liquidation reward system that adjusts the reward based on the size of the debt. This approach would incentivize liquidators to manage larger debts and prevent significant positions from going underwater.
