# Vulnerability details

## Proof of Concept
The core of the issue lies in the calculation performed within the [_requireAtLeastMinNetStEthBalance](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-06-badger/blob/9173558ee1ac8a78a7ae0a39b97b50ff0dd9e0f8/ebtc-zap-router/src/EbtcLeverageZapRouter.sol#L217) function. The function aims to ensure that the net `stETH` balance of a CDP, calculated as `_stEthDepositAmount - LIQUIDATOR_REWARD`, does not fall below a predefined minimum threshold [(MIN_NET_STETH_BALANCE)](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-06-badger/blob/9173558ee1ac8a78a7ae0a39b97b50ff0dd9e0f8/ebtc-zap-router/src/ZapRouterBase.sol#L144C30-L144C52). This calculation is intended to account for the liquidator reward, which is a mechanism to incentivize liquidators to act when a CDP falls below a certain health factor.

However, the subtraction of `LIQUIDATOR_REWARD` from `_stEthDepositAmount` assumes that the liquidator reward is not already included in `_stEthDepositAmount`. This assumption may not always hold true, especially in scenarios where the `_stEthDepositAmount` encompasses the liquidator reward as part of the total `stETH` collateral amount. In such cases, subtracting the liquidator reward again would result in a negative net `stETH` balance, which contradicts the function's purpose and could lead to erroneous rejections of valid transactions.
Let's explore two hypothetical scenarios that illustrate the potential vulnerability in the `_requireAtLeastMinNetStEthBalance` function.
### Scenario 1: Incorrect Liquidator Reward Deduction
Background: Alice wants to open a CDP (Collateralized Debt Position) using the `openCdp` function. She deposits 5 stETH as collateral and intends to borrow 2 ETH through a flash loan. The liquidator reward is set to 0.2 stETH.

Current Implementation:

- Alice's `_stEthDepositAmount` is 5 stETH.
- The liquidator reward is 0.2 stETH.
- The function calculates the net stETH balance as $5 stETH - 0.2 stETH = 4.8 stETH$.
Issue: The function checks if the net `stETH` balance is greater than or equal to `MIN_NET_STETH_BALANCE` (which is 2 stETH). Since 4.8 stETH is greater than 2 stETH, the transaction proceeds. However, because the liquidator reward is not accounted for in the calculation, Alice could potentially benefit from a liquidation event without having the required minimum net stETH balance.

### Scenario 2: Double Deduction of Liquidator Reward
Background: Bob decides to adjust his CDP using the `adjustCdp` function. He increases his collateral by 1 stETH, bringing his total to 6 stETH. The liquidator reward remains at 0.2 stETH.

Current Implementation:

- Bob's `_stEthDepositAmount` is increased to 6 stETH.
- The function calculates the net stETH balance as $6 stETH - 0.2 stETH = 5.8 stETH$.
Issue: Similar to the first scenario, the function checks if the net stETH balance is greater than or equal to `MIN_NET_STETH_BALANCE`. Since 5.8 stETH is greater than 2 stETH, the transaction proceeds. However, if the liquidator reward were somehow deducted twice (either due to a mistake in the code or misunderstanding of the logic), the net stETH balance could incorrectly appear to meet the minimum requirement.
## Impact
Alice could potentially benefit from a liquidation event without having the required minimum net stETH balance.

## Tools Used
Manual
## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Instead of subtracting the liquidator reward from the `_stEthDepositAmount`, the function should verify that the `_stEthDepositAmount` plus the liquidator reward meets or exceeds the `MIN_NET_STETH_BALANCE`.
