# Vulnerability details

## Proof of Concept
The `_executeWithdrawal` function is designed to process withdrawals based on a user's share of the payout. It calculates the amount that can be withdrawn by the account (`normalizedAmountWithdrawn`) based on the total paid (`batch.normalizedAmountPaid`) and the user's share (`status.scaledAmount`). While the function prevents zero-value withdrawals by reverting when `normalizedAmountWithdrawn` is zero, it does not explicitly check whether the account has already withdrawn the full amount owed.

Here is the relevant section of the function:
```solidity
uint128 newTotalWithdrawn = uint128(
    MathUtils.mulDiv(batch.normalizedAmountPaid, status.scaledAmount, batch.scaledTotalAmount)
);

uint128 normalizedAmountWithdrawn = newTotalWithdrawn - status.normalizedAmountWithdrawn;

if (normalizedAmountWithdrawn == 0) revert_NullWithdrawalAmount();
```
The bug arises from the fact that the condition only checks whether `normalizedAmountWithdrawn` is zero at the time of withdrawal, without explicitly verifying if the user has already withdrawn the full amount they are entitled to (`newTotalWithdrawn`).

This issue is caused by the absence of a direct comparison between the user's previously withdrawn amount (`status.normalizedAmountWithdrawn`) and the total amount they are owed (`newTotalWithdrawn`). As a result, the code relies on the difference calculation to implicitly determine if a withdrawal should proceed, but this could lead to situations where a user might attempt to withdraw without an explicit check for full withdrawal.
## Impact
The lack of an explicit check could result in users repeatedly calling the `_executeWithdrawal` function, even after withdrawing the full amount they are entitled to. While they wouldn't receive additional funds due to the calculation of `normalizedAmountWithdrawn`, it could waste gas and increase unnecessary contract execution costs.
## Tools Used
Manual
## Recommended Mitigation Steps
An explicit condition should be added to check whether the user has already withdrawn the full amount they are owed, preventing redundant withdrawal attempts.
