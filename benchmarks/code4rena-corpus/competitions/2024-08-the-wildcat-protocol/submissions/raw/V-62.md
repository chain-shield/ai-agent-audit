# Vulnerability details

## Description
The Wildcat protocol allows borrowers to adjust interest rates at will, subject to certain checks. This feature, combined with the delayed withdrawal mechanism, creates a potential for short-term arbitrage. A borrower could theoretically manipulate interest rates to benefit specific lenders who time their withdrawal requests strategically. For example, a borrower could significantly increase the interest rate just before a withdrawal cycle ends, allow favored lenders to queue withdrawals at this higher rate, and then decrease the rate immediately after the withdrawal cycle begins.

The root causes of this vulnerability are:

1. Unrestricted interest rate changes:
The primary root cause is the ability for the borrower to change interest rates without significant restrictions or delays. This is evident in the `setAnnualInterestAndReserveRatioBips()` function in `WildcatMarketConfig.sol`. This function allows the borrower to change interest rates at will, subject only to basic checks.

2. Batch processing of withdrawals:
The second root cause is the batch processing of withdrawals, which creates a window for exploitation. This is implemented in the `processExpiredWithdrawalBatch()` function in `WildcatMarketBase.sol`.This batch processing creates a time window between when withdrawals are queued and when they are processed, during which interest rates can be manipulated.

The vulnerability arises from the interaction between these two components, allowing a malicious borrower to change interest rates just before withdrawal batch processing, unfairly distributing accrued interest to specific lenders.


## Impact
This vulnerability could lead to:

1. Unfair distribution of interest, favoring lenders who can coordinate with the borrower or time their actions precisely.

2. Potential for increased interest costs to the borrower or reduced returns for other lenders.

3. Erosion of trust in the fairness and integrity of the protocol.

## Proof of Concept

Initial Setup:
- Market interest rate: 5% APR
- Withdrawal batch duration: 3 days
- Multiple lenders with deposits in the market
- Total market size: 1,000,000 tokens

Exploit Scenario:

Day 1:
1. Normal lenders queue withdrawals totaling 100,000 tokens
2. Market state: 100,000 tokens pending withdrawal at 5% APR
Day 3 (just before batch expiry):
3. Borrower increases interest rate to 20% APR
4. Borrower notifies colluding lender of the rate change
5. Colluding lender quickly queues a withdrawal of 50,000 tokens
6. Market state: 150,000 tokens pending withdrawal at 20% APR
Day 3 (batch expiry):
7. Withdrawal batch expires
8. Interest is accrued at 20% APR for 3 days on all 150,000 tokens
9. Accrued interest: ~2,466 tokens (20% 3/365 150,000)
10. Borrower immediately lowers interest rate back to 5% APR
Day 4:
11. Lenders execute their withdrawals
12. Normal lenders receive: 101,644 tokens (100,000 + 2/3 of accrued interest)
13. Colluding lender receives: 50,822 tokens (50,000 + 1/3 of accrued interest)

Result:
- Colluding lender earns 20% APR for 3 days on their withdrawal
- Normal lenders earn 20% APR for 3 days, but only on a portion of their funds
- Subsequent withdrawals earn the normal 5% APR

Impact:
- Unfair distribution of interest favoring the colluding lender
- Increased interest costs for the borrower
- Potential loss of trust in the protocol if detected

This proof of concept demonstrates how a borrower could manipulate interest rates to benefit specific lenders who can time their withdrawals precisely. They can then split the profit if they want to. While the impact is limited to interest distribution rather than direct theft of principle funds, it still represents a significant fairness issue for the protocol and it can be considered a form of indirect theft or unfair allocation of funds. 

The exploit manipulates the distribution of interest, allowing certain lenders to receive a disproportionate share of the accrued interest at the expense of other lenders. This unfair allocation effectively takes interest that would have been distributed to other lenders under normal circumstances.

The colluding lender benefits disproportionately because they receive 1/3 of the total accrued interest despite only contributing 1/3 of the queued withdrawal amount (50k out of 150k). In a fair scenario, they should receive less interest as they held their tokens for a shorter period.

This exploit allows the colluding lender to earn a higher effective interest rate on their tokens compared to other lenders, essentially "stealing" a portion of the interest that would have been allocated to long-term holders under normal circumstances.

The impact of this vulnerability is significant as it undermines the fairness of the interest distribution mechanism, potentially eroding trust in the protocol and disadvantaging lenders who are not able to time their withdrawals with interest rate changes.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement Rate Change Limits in setAnnualInterestAndReserveRatioBips:
Modify the `setAnnualInterestAndReserveRatioBips` function in `WildcatMarketConfig.sol` to include a maximum allowed rate change within a specified time period.

Introduce a time delay for rate changes:
Add a mandatory waiting period (e.g., 48 hours) between rate change announcement and implementation.

Implement a Moving Average Interest Rate for Withdrawal Calculations:
Modify the withdrawal process to use a moving average of the interest rate over the withdrawal cycle period, rather than the current rate.
