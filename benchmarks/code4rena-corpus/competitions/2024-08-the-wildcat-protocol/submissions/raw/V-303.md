# Vulnerability details

## Summary

The Wildcat protocol facilitates the creation of lending markets with varying parameters, like interest rates and withdrawal terms. These markets employ a mechanism to update their state, incorporating accrued interest, fees, and handling withdrawals. A crucial function in this process is _writeState, which updates the stored market state and ensures consistency between the actual and recorded states.

A critical oversight exists in the _writeState function related to market closure. When a market is closed, the protocol aims to halt further interest accrual and potentially redistribute remaining assets. However, the _writeState function doesn't prevent state updates even after closure, potentially leading to inconsistencies and unexpected behavior.

## Details

The Wildcat protocol uses the MarketState struct to track the essential parameters and variables of a market. This struct is updated periodically to reflect accrued interest, fees, and other relevant changes. The _writeState function is responsible for writing the updated MarketState to storage.

The closeMarket function allows the borrower to shut down a market, setting the annual interest rate to 0% and marking the market as closed in the MarketState. After closure, ideally, no further changes should occur to the market state except for processing pending withdrawals.

The _writeState function, responsible for updating the market state, doesn't have any mechanism to prevent updates if the market is already closed. This means that even after closure, functions that call _writeState can still modify the market state, leading to potential inconsistencies.

## Impact

The lack of restriction on state updates after market closure can lead to:

Inaccurate Accounting: If interest accrual or fee calculations continue after closure due to state updates, the recorded values for total debts, accrued fees, and other parameters might become inaccurate.

Unexpected Asset Transfers: Suppose the protocol logic relies on the market state to determine asset transfers during closure. In that case, incorrect updates after closure could cause unintended transfers.

Loss of Funds: In extreme cases, if state updates manipulate values significantly after closure, it might create loopholes for asset drainage or manipulation, leading to a loss of funds.

## Scenario

- A borrower initiates the closure of a market using the closeMarket function. The market state is updated, marking it as closed, and the annual interest rate is set to 0%.
- Due to a bug or a malicious action, another function that calls _writeState is executed after the closure. This function might inadvertently accrue additional interest, update fees, or modify other state variables.
- Since _writeState doesn't prevent updates for closed markets, the changes from step 2 are written to the market state, despite the market being closed.
- This creates an inconsistency between the intended state of the closed market (no further interest accrual or changes) and the actual recorded state. This can lead to inaccurate accounting, unexpected behavior, and potential vulnerabilities.

## Fix

Implement a simple check within the _writeState function to prevent state updates for closed markets:

Modify _writeState Function: At the beginning of the _writeState function, add a check that verifies if the market is closed by examining the isClosed flag in the MarketState struct.

Conditional Update: Allow the state update process to continue only if the market is not closed. If the market is closed, revert the transaction to prevent any state modification.
