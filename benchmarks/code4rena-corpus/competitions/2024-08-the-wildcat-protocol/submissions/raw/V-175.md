# Vulnerability details

The WildcatMarket contract allows borrowers to manipulate the reserve ratio in a way that can lead to excessive borrowing, potentially compromising the market's stability. By combining a liquidity injection with a lowering of the reserve ratio, borrowers can extract more liquidity than initially intended.


## Proof of Concept
The current implementation includes a safety check intended to prevent lowering the reserve ratio when there are insufficient reserves:
```solidity
function setAnnualInterestAndReserveRatioBips(
  uint16 _annualInterestBips,
  uint16 _reserveRatioBips
) external onlyBorrower nonReentrant sphereXGuardExternal {
....
  if (_reserveRatioBips < initialReserveRatioBips) {
    if (state.liquidityRequired() > totalAssets()) {
      revert_InsufficientReservesForOldLiquidityRatio();
    }
  }
}
```
However, this check can be easily bypassed by the borrower through the following steps:

1. The borrower makes a small repayment to the market, increasing totalAssets().
2. This repayment ensures that state.liquidityRequired() <= totalAssets(), allowing the check to pass.
3. The borrower then lowers the reserve ratio.
4. Finally, the borrower can borrow an amount larger than their initial repayment.

This sequence of actions effectively nullifies the intended safety measure, allowing the borrower to lower the reserve ratio even when it might compromise the market's stability. The ease with which this check can be circumvented exacerbates the vulnerability, as it provides a false sense of security while still allowing potentially harmful parameter changes.

Here is simple illustration, please note this is simplified version but similar problem should exist even with pending/ unpaid withdrawals


## Step a: Initial State

- Total Assets: 1,000
- Reserve Ratio: 10,000 bips (100%)
- Scaled Total Supply (Normalized): 1,200
- liquidityRequired: 1,200
- borrowableAssets: 0

In this initial state, the market has 1,000 units of total assets and a scaled total supply of 1,200. The reserve ratio is set at 100%, meaning all assets must be held as reserves. As a result, the liquidity required is equal to the scaled total supply (1,200), and there are no borrowable assets.

## Step b: Repayment of 500 units

- Total Assets: 1,500
- Reserve Ratio: 10,000 bips (100%)
- Scaled Total Supply (Normalized): 1,200
- liquidityRequired: 1,200
- borrowableAssets: 300

The borrower repay 500 units into the market, increasing the total assets to 1,500. The reserve ratio and scaled total supply remain unchanged. The liquidity required is still 1,200, but now there are 300 units of borrowable assets (1,500 - 1,200).

## Step c: Lower Reserve Ratio to 5,000 bips (50%)

- Total Assets: 1,500
- Reserve Ratio: 5,000 bips (50%)
- Scaled Total Supply (Normalized): 1,200
- liquidityRequired: 600
- borrowableAssets: 900

The borrower lowers the reserve ratio from 100% to 50%. This dramatically reduces the liquidity required from 1,200 to 600 (50% of the scaled total supply). As a result, the borrowable assets increase to 900 (1,500 - 600).

## Step d: Borrow 900 units

- Total Assets: 600
- Reserve Ratio: 5,000 bips (50%)
- Scaled Total Supply (Normalized): 1,200
- liquidityRequired: 600
- borrowableAssets: 0

The borrower takes advantage of the increased borrowable assets and borrows 900 units. This reduces the total assets to 600 (1,500 - 900). The liquidity required remains at 600, which is exactly equal to the remaining total assets. There are now 0 borrowable assets left.

## Summary

This scenario demonstrates how a borrower can exploit the ability to change the reserve ratio:

1. They initially inject 500 units into the market.
2. By lowering the reserve ratio from 100% to 50%, they dramatically increase the amount of borrowable assets.
3. They then borrow 900 units, which is 400 units more than they initially added.

The key to this exploitation is the ability to lower the reserve ratio, which allows the borrower to access a larger portion of the market's assets. This manipulation effectively drains liquidity from the market and renders the current validation ineffective.

This vulnerability is applicable when reserve ratio changes are allowed (e.g. when MarketConstraintHooks are not used)
## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement a time delay between reserve ratio changes and borrowing.
