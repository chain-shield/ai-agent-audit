# Vulnerability details

## Description
The `closeMarket` function in the `WildcatMarket` contract performs multiple critical operations in a single transaction, including settling debts, processing withdrawal batches, and closing the market. While the function includes safeguards against partial execution, it remains vulnerable to interruption due to gas limitations or external call failures. This could lead to temporary fund lockups and increased operational complexity.

The function could be interrupted due to a variety of reasons:

- Gas limit exceeded: If a market has accumulated a very large number of unpaid withdrawal batches (e.g., 1000+), processing all of them in a single transaction could exceed the block gas limit, causing the transaction to fail.

- External call failures:
For example:
 - Blacklist: If the underlying asset has a blacklist function and the user is blacklisted.
 - Token pausing: If the underlying asset is an ERC20 token with a pause feature (common in some stablecoins and other ERC20s), and it gets paused during the transaction, any transfers would fail, interrupting the closure process.
 - Approval issues: If the _repay function requires a separate approval step for transferring tokens from the borrower, and this approval wasn't properly set or has expired, the transfer could fail.

## Impacts
Temporary fund lockup: If the function fails due to gas limitations or any other reason, withdrawal processing could be interrupted, potentially leaving some user funds temporarily inaccessible.

Increased Gas Costs: Failed closure attempts result in wasted gas fees for the borrower. In cases with many withdrawal batches or persistent external issues, these costs could be significant due to repeated attempts.

Delayed Market Closure: The primary impact is the inability to close the market as intended. If the `closeMarket` function fails due to gas limitations or external call failures, the market remains open. This could interfere with protocol upgrades or changes that depend on market closure.



## Proof of Concept
Scenario #1: Closing a Large, Active Market
1. Deploy a `WildcatMarket` contract with a popular ERC20 as the underlying asset.

2. Over time, allow the market to accumulate a large number of lenders and withdrawal requests.

3. Simulate market activity:
Have multiple lenders deposit into the market.
Generate a high volume of withdrawal requests over several days or weeks.
Ensure that many of these withdrawal requests remain unprocessed, creating a backlog of unpaid withdrawal batches.


4. Initiate market closure:
As the borrower, call the `closeMarket` function when the number of unpaid withdrawal batches is very high (e.g., 1000+ batches).


5. Observe transaction failure:
The `closeMarket` transaction fails due to exceeding the block gas limit.
The market remains open, and the closure process is incomplete.


6. Attempt repeated closure:
The borrower makes multiple attempts to close the market, each time failing due to gas limitations.


7. Examine the aftermath:
The market remains open despite the borrower's intention to close it.
Lenders with pending withdrawals are unable to access their funds, as the closure process hasn't completed.
The borrower faces increased costs from failed transactions and the need for repeated attempts.

Outcome:
The market cannot be closed in a single transaction due to the high number of unpaid withdrawal batches.
Lenders experience a temporary fund lockup as their withdrawals can't be processed during the failed closure attempts.
The borrower faces increased operational complexity and costs trying to close the market.

Scenario #2: Token Transfer Failure Due to Asset Contract Pause

1. Setup:
Deploy a `WildcatMarket` contract using a popular ERC20 (with a blacklist functionality, token pausing, approval issues, etc) as the underlying asset.
Simulate normal market operations with multiple lenders and borrowing activities.
Ensure the market has some outstanding debt and some excess assets.

2. Initiate Market Closure:
As the borrower, call the `closeMarket` function when the market has both outstanding debt and excess assets.
This setup ensures that the function will attempt both types of transfers: debt repayment and excess asset return.

3. Simulate Asset Contract Pause:
Just as the `closeMarket` function begins executing, simulate a pause, blacklist, or a techinal reason/issue in the ERC20 contract.

4. Observe First Transfer Failure:
The `closeMarket` function attempts to transfer tokens to settle the outstanding debt.
This transfer fails due to the ERC20 contract.

5. Examine Partial Execution State:
The market closure process is interrupted mid-execution.

Outcome:
- The market closure process fails to complete due to the external call failure.
- The market remains open when the borrower intended to close it.
- Any gas fees associated with the failed transaction are lost.
- The borrower needs to resolve any external issues (e.g., token pause, blacklist, approval issues, etc) before successfully closing the market.

Impact:
Temporary fund lockup for all market participants.
Potential loss of trust in the protocol due to the inability to close markets reliably.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement a Batched Closure Process:
- Modify the `closeMarket` function to process withdrawal batches in smaller, fixed-size groups.
- Introduce a state variable to track closure progress, allowing the process to be resumed if interrupted.


Introduce a Closure Preparation State:
- Add a new market state "preparingForClosure" that can be set before initiating the closure process.
- During this state, restrict new deposits and non-essential operations to reduce complexity during closure.

Enhance Error Handling and Reporting:
- Implement more granular error messages for different failure modes in the `closeMarket` function.
- Add events to log closure attempts, progress, and failures for better off-chain monitoring.

Implement Gas Estimation:
- Before processing each batch or making external calls, estimate the gas cost.
- If the estimated cost is too high, break the operation into smaller steps.
