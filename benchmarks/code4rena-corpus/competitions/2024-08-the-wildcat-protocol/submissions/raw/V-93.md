# Vulnerability details

## Description
The Wildcat protocol's withdrawal mechanism in the `_processExpiredWithdrawalBatch` function adds unpaid withdrawal batches to an array (`_withdrawalData.unpaidBatches`) that can potentially grow without bounds. When a withdrawal batch expires without sufficient assets to cover all withdrawals, it is added to this array. However, there is no mechanism to remove old, unpaid batches from the array, even after they are processed.

The protocol includes a mitigating factor:

The `withdrawalBatchDuration` limits how quickly new batches can be created.

While this factors make it difficult to rapidly bloat the array, it does not address the core issue of unbounded growth over time. The `repayAndProcessUnpaidWithdrawalBatches` function allows processing a limited number of batches per transaction, but it does not remove processed batches from the array.

## Impacts
- Increasing gas costs: As the unpaid batches array grows, functions that iterate over this array (like `repayAndProcessUnpaidWithdrawalBatches`) will consume more gas, making certain protocol operations more and more expensive over time. Due to high gas costs, this can lead to a economic denial of service, as the prohibitive cost acts as the barrier.

- Long-term denial of service (DOS): In extreme scenarios where the array grows very large, it could exceed block gas limits, preventing the execution of functions that process withdrawals. 

- Reduced protocol efficiency: The accumulation of many small, unpaid batches could lead to decreased overall efficiency in withdrawal processing and increased operational complexity for managing markets with a large number of unpaid batches.

- Long-term sustainability risk: If left unaddressed, this issue could compound over time, potentially affecting the protocol's long-term viability and scalability.

## Proof of Concept

The unbounded growth of the unpaid withdrawal batches array occurs when withdrawal batches are created and expire without being fully paid. This is primarily a semi-long-term concern that could manifest under sustained market conditions where liquidity is consistently insufficient to meet withdrawal demands.

Below is the unbounded growth of the unpaid withdrawal batches array demonstrated through a long-term scenario involving a Wildcat market with consistent activity and periodic liquidity shortages. This proof of concept outlines how the vulnerability could manifest over time:

1. Initial Setup:
- A Wildcat market with typical parameters (e.g., withdrawal batch duration, maximum deposit, interest rates).
- Simulate regular deposit and withdrawal activities from multiple lenders.

2. Triggering Unpaid Batches:
- Over several months, introduce periodic liquidity shortages in the market.
- These shortages should be significant enough to prevent full payment of some withdrawal batches but not so severe as to completely halt market operations.

3. Accumulation of Unpaid Batches:
- Monitor the `_withdrawalData.unpaidBatches` array as withdrawal batches expire during liquidity shortages.
- Observe that each time the `_processExpiredWithdrawalBatch` function is called for an underfunded batch, a new entry is added to the unpaid batches array.

4. Partial Repayments:
- Simulate periodic repayments using the `repayAndProcessUnpaidWithdrawalBatches` function.
- Note that while this processes some unpaid batches, it does not remove entries from the array, leading to accumulation.

5. Long-term Observation:
- Continue this cycle of withdrawals, liquidity shortages, and partial repayments over an extended period (e.g., 1-2 years of simulated market activity).
- Track the growth of the `_withdrawalData.unpaidBatches` array and the gas costs associated with functions that interact with it.

6. Impact Analysis:
- Gas costs for operations like `repayAndProcessUnpaidWithdrawalBatches` as the array grows increase.
- In extreme scenarios, an attempt to process a large number of unpaid batches can lead to potential issues with block gas limits.

7. Efficiency Comparison:
- The performance and gas costs of withdrawal-related functions at the beginning of the simulation are very different to those at the end.
- Growing array size affects the overall efficiency of the protocol.

This proof of concept demonstrates that under realistic market conditions with periodic liquidity issues, the unpaid withdrawal batches array can grow indefinitely over time. While not an immediate threat, this growth can lead to increasing gas costs, reduced efficiency in withdrawal processing, and Denial of Service scenarios, impacting the long-term sustainability and scalability of the Wildcat protocol.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement a mechanism to remove or consolidate processed batches from the `_withdrawalData.unpaidBatches` array. This could be done by adding a cleanup step in the `repayAndProcessUnpaidWithdrawalBatches` function to remove fully processed batches.

Add a configurable cap on the number of unpaid batches that can be queued. This could be a protocol-wide parameter or a per-market setting adjustable by governance or the borrower.

Create a batch compression mechanism that combines multiple small unpaid batches into larger ones, reducing the overall number of array entries while preserving withdrawal order and amounts.

Further optimize the `repayAndProcessUnpaidWithdrawalBatches` function to ensure it can always execute within gas limits even if the array grows large, possibly by implementing a dynamic batch processing size based on current gas prices and array length.
