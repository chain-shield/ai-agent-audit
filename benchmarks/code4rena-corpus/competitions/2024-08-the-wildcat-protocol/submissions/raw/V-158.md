# Vulnerability details

## Description
The `FixedTermLoanHooks` contract contains a vulnerability in the `setFixedTermEndTime` function. This function allows the borrower to arbitrarily reduce the `fixedTermEndTime` of a market, including setting it to 0 or a timestamp in the past. While there is a check to prevent increasing the fixed term, there is no lower bound check on the new end time.

## Impacts
Premature withdrawal allowance: Setting `fixedTermEndTime` to 0 or a past timestamp would immediately allow withdrawals, before the intended loan duration has passed.

Premature Liquidity Pressure: Allowing withdrawals earlier than expected could create sudden liquidity pressure on the market. As more withdrawals are queued, the amount of liquidity required to be held in the market increases, even though the reserve ratio remains constant. This is because queued withdrawals are added to the required liquidity calculation on top of the reserve ratio requirement. The borrower might face challenges in maintaining this increased required liquidity if many lenders queue withdrawals simultaneously.

Potential for Increased Delinquency: If many lenders queue withdrawals earlier than the borrower anticipated (due to the removal of the fixed term restriction), the borrower might struggle to maintain the required liquidity. This could potentially lead to the market becoming delinquent more easily, which would trigger penalty interest rates.

Reduced Returns for Lenders: Lenders who choose to withdraw early will receive less return on their investment than initially expected, as they wouldn't benefit from the full term of interest accrual.

Vulnerability Chaining: This Vulnerability can be chained with other vulnerabilities to create more severe exploits and have more control over the exploits (see the `Delinquency Fee Manipulation in Withdrawal Process` finding that I have submitted).


## Proof of Concept

1. A borrower creates a Wildcat market with a fixed term end time set one year in the future. The market has a reserve ratio of 20%, a grace period of 3 days, and a delinquency fee of 5% on top of the base interest rate.

2. Several lenders deposit a total of 1,000,000 USDC into the market, expecting their funds to be locked for the full year.

3. The borrower borrows 750,000 USDC, leaving 250,000 USDC in the market (25% reserve, above the required 20%).


4. The borrower calls `setFixedTermEndTime`, setting the new end time to the current timestamp either by accident, or on purpose.

5. This change immediately allows all lenders to queue withdrawals. Some lenders, noticing this unexpected change, quickly queue withdrawals totaling 400,000 USDC.

6. The market now requires 400,000 USDC in liquidity to process these withdrawals, but only has 250,000 USDC available.

7. This situation causes the market to become delinquent, as the required liquidity (400,000 USDC) exceeds the total assets in the market (250,000 USDC).

8. If the borrower fails to repay enough funds to cure the delinquency within the 3-day grace period, the delinquency fee of 5% is triggered.

9. The penalty interest rate is now applied on top of the base interest rate, increasing the borrower's debt burden and the potential returns for lenders who haven't withdrawn.

10. The borrower now faces increased pressure to repay funds quickly to stop the accrual of the penalty interest and cure the market's delinquency status.

This proof of concept demonstrates how the ability to arbitrarily change the `fixedTermEndTime` can lead to a cascade of events resulting in market delinquency and the triggering of penalty interest rates. The vulnerability creates conditions that can rapidly destabilize the market's liquidity and trigger punitive measures designed to protect lenders.

** Note: This vulnerability can be chained with other vulnerabilities. For example, I have submitted another vulnerability called "Delinquency Fee Manipulation in Withdrawal Process"(https://code4rena.com/audits/2024-08-the-wildcat-protocol/submit?issue=I_kwDOMqdWds6WcQcM), this will allow this vulnerability to become even more severe. This vulnerability can not only be chained with "Delinquency Fee Manipulation in Withdrawal Process" to benefit the borrower, but also can be chained with other vulnerabilities that may be in the protocol. It is crucial to implement a lower bound check in `setFixedTermEndTime` to mitigate the success of any vulnerability exploiting and vulnerability chaining. **


## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement a lower bound check: Modify the `setFixedTermEndTime` function to include a check that prevents setting the new end time to 0 or a past timestamp. 

Implement a minimum fixed term duration: Consider adding a minimum duration for the fixed term that cannot be violated when updating the end time.
