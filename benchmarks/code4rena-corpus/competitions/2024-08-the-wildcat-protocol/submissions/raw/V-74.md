# Vulnerability details

## Description
The Wildcat protocol, designed to be deployed across multiple chains (Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, and Polygon), lacks a standardized mechanism for chain-specific credential validation. The `AccessControlHooks` contract, responsible for validating lender credentials, does not inherently verify the origin chain of a credential. This oversight could potentially allow a credential obtained on one chain to be reused on another chain with different regulatory or security requirements.

The vulnerability stems from the flexible nature of the protocol's hooks system and role providers. While this flexibility allows for customized implementations, it also places the responsibility of implementing chain-specific checks on the borrowers and role provider developers. Without proper guidance or a standardized approach, there's a risk that these crucial checks might be overlooked or implemented inconsistently across different markets and chains.

The `_tryValidateAccess` function in the `AccessControlHooks` contract serves as the main entry point for credential validation but does not include built-in chain verification. 

## Impacts
Regulatory Violations: Markets on chains with stricter regulations could inadvertently allow access to unqualified lenders.

Security Compromise: Markets intended to have higher security standards could be accessed using credentials from less secure environments.

Economic Exploitation: 
- Malicious actors could take advantage of economic differences between chains, potentially destabilizing markets or gaining unfair advantages.
- Malicious actors could potentially exploit economic differences between chains by obtaining credentials on one chain and using them on another.

Market Instability: Unauthorized access due to cross-chain credential replay could lead to unexpected behavior in affected markets, potentially causing financial instability and/or losses.

Inconsistent Security Standards: Different implementations of chain-specific checks could lead to inconsistent security standards across markets and chains.



## Proof of Concept
This proof of concept demonstrates how an attacker could exploit the Cross-Chain Credential Replay vulnerability in the Wildcat protocol:

1. Initial Setup:
- The Wildcat protocol is deployed on two chains: Chain A (with looser regulations) and Chain B (with stricter regulations).
- A borrower deploys similar markets on both chains using the `HooksFactory` contract.
- The borrower uses the same `AccessControlHooks` instance for both markets, configured with identical role providers.


2. Credential Acquisition on Chain A:
- The attacker completes a simpler verification process on Chain A to obtain credentials.
- The role provider on Chain A calls `grantRole` in the `AccessControlHooks` contract, storing the credential.


3. Extracting Credential Data:
- The attacker monitors the transaction on Chain A to extract the credential data, including any signatures or proofs used.


4. Preparing for Replay on Chain B:
- The attacker constructs the `extraData` parameter for a market function call on Chain B, including the extracted credential data.


5. Executing the Replay Attack:
- The attacker calls a restricted function (e.g., deposit) on the market contract on Chain B.
- The market contract calls `_tryValidateAccess` on the `AccessControlHooks` contract.
- `_tryValidateAccess` processes the `extraData`, calling `validateCredential` on the role provider.


6. Successful Unauthorized Access:
- The `validateCredential` function, lacking chain-specific checks, validates the credential from Chain A.
- `_tryValidateAccess` returns true, allowing the attacker to access the restricted function on Chain B's market.


7. Exploit Outcome:
- The attacker gains unauthorized access to a market on Chain B, bypassing its stricter regulatory requirements.
- This access could be used for further exploits, such as economic manipulation or regulatory violations.

This proof of concept highlights how the lack of chain-specific credential validation in the `AccessControlHooks` contract and role providers allows for credential replay across different chains. It exploits the flexibility of the Wildcat protocol's credential system, demonstrating the need for standardized chain verification in the credential validation process.

## Root Causes and Affected Code

- In `AccessControlHooks.sol`, the `_tryValidateAccess` function lacks chain-specific checks.

- In the `RoleProvider.sol` file, the `RoleProvider` type and associated functions don't include any chain-specific information, fundamentally limiting the system's ability to differentiate between chains in credential validation.

- In the market contracts (e.g., `WildcatMarketBase.sol`), the calls to hook functions don't include chain ID information and allows for potential cross-chain credential use.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement Chain ID Validation: Modify the `AccessControlHooks` contract to include a built-in chain ID check. This could involve storing the deployment chain ID in the contract's constructor and verifying it during credential validation.

Update Credential Structure: Modify the credential structure to include the chain ID as part of the credential data. This makes the chain information an integral part of the credential itself.

Standardize Role Provider Interface: Create a standardized interface for role providers that includes a method for chain-specific validation. This ensures consistency across different implementations.

Automated Verification Tool: Use/Develop a tool that can automatically verify whether a market's hooks and role providers include proper chain-specific checks. This can be part of the market deployment process.
