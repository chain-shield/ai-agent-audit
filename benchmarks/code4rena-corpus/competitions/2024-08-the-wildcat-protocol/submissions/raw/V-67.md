# Vulnerability details

## Description
The WildcatMarket protocol is vulnerable to a timing attack where the borrower can manipulate the market state just before a withdrawal batch is processed. By intentionally causing the market to become delinquent, the borrower can trigger a significant increase in the `scaleFactor` due to delinquency fees. This inflated `scaleFactor` is then used to process the withdrawal batch, resulting in users receiving fewer underlying assets for their market tokens than they should.

The vulnerability stems from the following factors:
1. The borrower has control over key market parameters, including the reserve ratio.
2. There is no cooldown period between state changes and withdrawal processing.
3. Delinquency fees are applied to the entire outstanding debt, causing a substantial scaleFactor increase.
4. Withdrawal batches are processed at predictable times, allowing for precise timing of the attack.

## Impact
- Users receive fewer assets than expected when withdrawing, especially during large withdrawal batches.

- The borrower can unfairly benefit from inflated delinquency fees.

- Trust in the protocol may be undermined, potentially leading to a "bank run" scenario if discovered.


## Proof of Concept
1. Initial Setup:
- A `WildcatMarket` is deployed with a significant total supply and multiple lenders.
- The market has a pending withdrawal batch set to expire soon.

2. Monitoring:
- The malicious borrower monitors the market state, waiting for a large withdrawal batch to be queued.
- They calculate the optimal timing to execute their attack, just before the withdrawal batch is processed.

3. Pre-Attack State:
- Record the current market parameters: reserve ratio, annual interest rate, and delinquency status.
- Note the size of the pending withdrawal batch and the current `scaleFactor`.

4. Attack Execution:
- Just before the withdrawal batch is set to be processed, the borrower calls `setAnnualInterestAndReserveRatioBips`:
a. They significantly lower the reserve ratio, pushing the market into delinquency.
b. This triggers the application of delinquency fees in the next state update.

5. Withdrawal Processing:
- The `getUpdatedState` function is called as part of processing the withdrawal batch.
- This function applies the new, lower reserve ratio and calculates delinquency fees.
- The `scaleFactor` is increased due to the sudden application of delinquency fees.

6. Impact:
- The `_processExpiredWithdrawalBatch` function uses this new, inflated `scaleFactor` to process withdrawals.
- As a result, users in the withdrawal batch receive fewer underlying assets than they would have under the previous market conditions.

7. Post-Attack:
- Immediately after the withdrawal is processed, the borrower calls `setAnnualInterestAndReserveRatioBips` again:
a. They restore the reserve ratio to its previous level or slightly higher.
b. This brings the market out of delinquency.

8. Profit Realization:
- The borrower has effectively reduced their debt to the lenders who withdrew.
- They retain more of the underlying assets in the market than they should have.

9. Verification:
The malicious borrower through this manipulation gained in profit the difference of the amount of assets withdrawn by users to what they would have received under the original market conditions.

This proof of concept demonstrates how a malicious borrower can exploit the lack of protection for queued withdrawals against rapid state changes, potentially leading to significant losses for withdrawing lenders.

Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement a cooldown period: Introduce a time delay between significant state changes (e.g., reserve ratio adjustments) and the processing of withdrawal batches. This would prevent last-minute manipulations.

Withdrawal batch protection: Add a mechanism to snapshot the market state at the time a withdrawal is queued, and use this state for processing the withdrawal when the batch expires. This would protect users from last-minute state manipulations.

Gradual fee application: Instead of applying delinquency fees to the entire outstanding debt at once, implement a gradual increase over time. This would reduce the impact of short-term delinquencies on the `scaleFactor`.
