# Vulnerability details

## Description
The `WildcatMarketWithdrawals` contract allows borrowers to control the timing and extent of withdrawal batch processing through the `repayAndProcessUnpaidWithdrawalBatches` function. While the function processes batches in a First-In-First-Out (FIFO) order, there is no mechanism to ensure that all unpaid batches are processed within a specific timeframe. A malicious or negligent borrower could exploit this by consistently repaying small amounts or not repaying at all, leading to significant delays in processing withdrawal requests.

While the protocol implements penalty mechanisms (delinquency fee and penalty APR) to discourage such behavior, these may not be sufficient to prevent a determined actor from causing significant withdrawal delays.


## Impact
- Lenders may experience unpredictable and potentially lengthy delays in withdrawal processing, extending beyond the intended withdrawal cycle duration.

- The reliability of the withdrawal process could be compromised, potentially eroding user trust in the protocol.

- In extreme cases, a malicious borrower could effectively create a "soft lock" on funds by continuously delaying withdrawal processing, though this would likely result in penalties.


## Proof of Concept

1. Initial Setup:
- A Wildcat market is deployed with a 20% reserve ratio and a 7-day withdrawal cycle.
- The market has 1,000,000 USDC in total deposits from various lenders.

2. Withdrawal Requests:
- Day 1: Lender A requests to withdraw 100,000 USDC.
- Day 3: Lender B requests to withdraw 150,000 USDC.
- Day 5: Lender C requests to withdraw 200,000 USDC.

3. Borrower's Actions:
- The borrower, noticing large withdrawal requests, decides to delay processing to maintain control over the liquidity.
- At the end of the 7-day cycle, instead of repaying the full 450,000 USDC requested, the borrower only repays 50,000 USDC.
- The borrower calls `repayAndProcessUnpaidWithdrawalBatches` with maxBatches set to 1, processing only the oldest batch (Lender A's request).

4. Impact on Withdrawal Processing:
- Lender A receives only a partial withdrawal (50,000 USDC out of 100,000 USDC requested).
- Lender B and C's withdrawal requests remain unprocessed and move to the unpaid batches queue.

5. Continued Delay:
- In subsequent cycles, the borrower continues to repay small amounts (e.g., 20,000-30,000 USDC) and processes only one batch at a time.
- This results in a growing backlog of unpaid withdrawal requests.

6. Consequences:

- Lenders experience significant delays in receiving their funds. For example, Lender C might wait several cycles beyond the initial 7-day period to receive their full withdrawal.
- The borrower incurs penalty fees and an increased APR due to maintaining a delinquent state.
The market's liquidity becomes unpredictable, potentially deterring new lenders.

This PoC demonstrates that while the borrower can't indefinitely avoid processing withdrawals without penalties, they can cause significant delays and uncertainty in the withdrawal process. 

A borrower could delay withdrawals for a period ranging from a few weeks to potentially a couple of months, depending on market conditions and their willingness to incur penalties. The exact duration would be influenced by:

- The withdrawal cycle duration 
- The delinquency grace period 
- The penalty APR 
- The market's reserve ratio 
- The base interest rate 

After the grace period, the borrower would incur penalties. Theoretically, a borrower could continue this behavior indefinitely by maintaining the minimum reserve ratio and processing batches very slowly, however in the long long term, the accumulating costs would make this unsustainable.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement a maximum time limit for processing withdrawal batches. After this limit is reached, the protocol should automatically process outstanding batches or incentivize the borrower to do so through increased penalties.

Add a mechanism for lenders to force-process withdrawal batches after a certain period has elapsed since the batch expiry. This could involve a collective action threshold to prevent abuse.

Perhaps even consider adding a "health factor" for markets based on the age and volume of unpaid withdrawals, which could be used to trigger automatic interventions or increased penalties.
