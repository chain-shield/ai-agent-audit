# Vulnerability details

## Description
The Wildcat protocol allows borrowers to adjust the annual interest rate and reserve ratio of their markets through the `setAnnualInterestAndReserveRatioBips()` function in `WildcatMarketConfig.sol`. While this feature is designed to provide flexibility for borrowers to respond to changing market conditions, it can be exploited due to the lack of additional safeguards.

This vulnerability affects both delinquent and non-delinquent markets, though the risks may be higher in delinquent markets where borrowers have less downside risk and more incentive to manipulate rates for potential gain. A malicious borrower can potentially:
1. Significantly increase the annual interest rate (up to 10000 basis points or 100% as per the BIP constant)
2. Manipulate the reserve ratio (also up to 10000 basis points or 100%)
3. Cause extreme short-term interest rate spikes by combining these actions with the existing delinquency fee mechanism

The vulnerability is exacerbated by the absence of rate change limits or cooldown periods between adjustments. This allows for rapid and frequent changes to these critical parameters.
The issue is further compounded by the frequent state updates in the `_getUpdatedState` function in `WildcatMarketBase.sol`. This function updates the market state, including interest accrual, every time it's called. The combination of flexible parameter adjustments and frequent state updates creates an opportunity for a malicious borrower to manipulate interest rates and reserve ratios to their advantage.

While the protocol includes a hooks mechanism that could potentially add constraints, it does not guarantee protection against this type of manipulation.

## Impacts
1. Unfair Distribution of Interest: Sudden spikes in interest rates could lead to an unfair distribution of interest among lenders. Those who happen to have funds in the market during high-interest periods would receive disproportionate returns, while others might suffer reduced gains. This impact could be more severe for markets with longer-term deposits or withdrawal restrictions, as short-term lenders might be able to exit more quickly.

2. Indirect Financial Losses: While not direct theft, lenders could face unexpected losses or reduced gains due to manipulated rates. These losses could occur through being forced to withdraw at unfavorable times due to sudden parameter changes.

3. Market Instability and Manipulation: The ability to rapidly change interest rates and reserve ratios could lead to extreme market volatility. A malicious actor could create artificial market conditions that benefit them at the expense of other participants. For example, they could temporarily create scarcity or abundance of loanable funds, potentially leading to market inefficiencies or exploitable arbitrage opportunities.

4. Increased Difficulty in Risk Assessment: The rapid and unpredictable changes in key market parameters would make it challenging for lenders to accurately assess the risks of participating in a market. This could lead to misinformed investment decisions and potentially deter risk-averse participants from the platform.

5. Borrower Impact: While the vulnerability primarily affects lenders, borrowers could also face challenges due to unpredictable borrowing costs. Rapid changes in interest rates could make it difficult for borrowers to plan and manage their debt effectively.

6. Potential for Cascading Effects: If multiple markets within the Wildcat protocol are affected, it could lead to broader instability in the entire ecosystem. Lenders and borrowers might lose confidence in all markets, not just the manipulated ones, leading to a potential "bank run" scenario across the platform.

7. Erosion of Trust: Erratic market behavior resulting from this vulnerability could significantly damage trust in both individual markets and the entire Wildcat protocol. This loss of confidence could lead to reduced participation, potentially threatening the long-term viability of the protocol.

8. Regulatory Risks: Extreme market volatility and potential for manipulation could attract unwanted regulatory attention. This might not only affect the specific markets or the Wildcat protocol but could have broader implications for the DeFi space, potentially leading to stricter regulations or scrutiny of similar platforms.

The combination of these impacts could severely undermine the stability, fairness, and attractiveness of the Wildcat protocol, potentially leading to a significant loss of users and liquidity if not addressed promptly.


## Proof of Concept
This proof of concept demonstrates how a malicious actor could exploit the flexibility in interest rate and reserve ratio adjustments in the Wildcat protocol to create market instability and potentially unfair distribution of interest.

Vulnerability:

The `setAnnualInterestAndReserveRatioBips` function in `WildcatMarketConfig.sol` allows borrowers to adjust interest rates and reserve ratios with minimal restrictions. While this flexibility is an intended feature, it can be exploited to create rapid and significant changes in market conditions, potentially leading to unfair advantages or market destabilization.

Preconditions:
1. The attacker has borrower privileges on a Wildcat market (either by deploying a new market or taking over an existing one).

2. The market has active lenders with significant funds deposited.

3. The protocol does not implement rate change limits or cooldown periods between changes.

Exploitation Steps:
1. Market Preparation:
- The attacker, as the borrower, ensures the market has a substantial amount of deposits from lenders.
- They may optionally borrow funds to bring the market closer to its reserve ratio limit, increasing the impact of subsequent actions.

2. Interest Rate Manipulation:
- The attacker uses `setAnnualInterestAndReserveRatioBips` to rapidly alternate between very high and very low interest rates.
- They set the interest rate to the maximum allowed (slightly below 10000 bips, or 100% APR, as limited by the BIP constant) for short periods, then quickly reduce it to a very low value (e.g., 100 bips or 1% APR).

3. Reserve Ratio Manipulation:
- Simultaneously or alternating with interest rate changes, the attacker adjusts the reserve ratio.
- They may lower the reserve ratio to increase borrowing capacity or raise it to potentially trigger delinquency.

4. Timing the Attack:
- The attacker times these changes to coincide with the protocol's interest accrual updates (handled by `updateScaleFactorAndFees` called within `_getUpdatedState`).
- They execute multiple transactions in quick succession to maximize the effect, working within the constraints of block times and gas costs.

5. Exploiting Market State Updates:
- The attacker takes advantage of the frequent state updates (`_getUpdatedState` and `_writeState`) to create brief windows of extreme conditions.
- They might time their actions to occur just before these updates to maximize the impact of their parameter changes.

6. Repeat and Amplify:
- The process is repeated multiple times, creating a pattern of volatility and unpredictability in the market.

Outcome:
1. Lenders in the market during high-interest periods would see artificially inflated returns, while those entering or exiting during low-interest periods would be disadvantaged.

2. The rapid changes in reserve ratios could cause the market to oscillate between normal and delinquent states, affecting borrowing capacity and triggering penalty fees.

3. The overall stability and predictability of the market would be severely compromised, likely leading to a loss of trust among participants.

If exploited across multiple markets, this could undermine confidence in the entire Wildcat protocol.

This proof of concept illustrates how the current implementation of interest rate and reserve ratio adjustments can be exploited to create significant market instability and potentially unfair distribution of interest. It highlights the need for implementing protective measures such as rate change limits and cooldown periods to prevent such manipulations.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement Rate Change Limits: Introduce maximum limits on how much the interest rate and reserve ratio can be changed in a single transaction or within a specific time frame. This could be a percentage of the current rate or an absolute value, whichever is lower.

Add Cooldown Periods: Implement a cooldown period between rate changes, particularly for delinquent borrowers. This prevents rapid successive manipulations.

Cap Effective Interest Rates: Implement a maximum cap on the effective interest rate (base rate + delinquency fee) to prevent extreme spikes.

Gradual Rate Change Mechanism: Implement a mechanism that applies rate changes gradually over time or has a mandatory waiting period (e.g., 48 hours) between rate change announcement and implementation, rather than instantly.
