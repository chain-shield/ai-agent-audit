# Vulnerability details

## Description
The Wildcat protocol's withdrawal mechanism is vulnerable to manipulation primarily due to its proportional distribution of liquidity within withdrawal batches. This vulnerability arises from the interaction between the `queueWithdrawal`, and `executeWithdrawal`, in the `WildcatMarketWithdrawals` contract and the `_processExpiredWithdrawalBatch` function in the `WildcatMarketBase` contract, but is fundamentally enabled by the `_applyWithdrawalBatchPayment` function in the `WildcatMarketBase` contract.

The `_applyWithdrawalBatchPayment` function distributes available liquidity proportionally among all withdrawal requests in a batch, regardless of their size or order. This mechanism, combined with the batch-based processing and FIFO queue for unpaid batches, creates an opportunity for exploitation.
An attacker could potentially exploit this system through the following process:

1. Monitor the market for low liquidity periods
2. Create a small withdrawal request, initiating a new batch
3. Front-run large legitimate withdrawals, pushing them into the same batch
4. If the batch becomes unpaid due to insufficient liquidity, create multiple small withdrawal requests, each in a new batch
5. Exploit the FIFO nature of the unpaid batches queue to prioritize their withdrawals
6. Front-run large deposits or repayments, ensuring their small withdrawals are processed first

While the protocol includes mechanisms to discourage low liquidity conditions (such as `delinquencyFeeBips` and `delinquencyGracePeriod` in `WildcatMarketBase`), and borrowers have some control over market parameters through `WildcatMarketConfig`, the current implementation of proportional liquidity distribution doesn't fully prevent this type of manipulation.


## Impact
1. Unfair prioritization of withdrawals, favoring manipulators over legitimate users

2. Potential delays or blocks on large withdrawals during low liquidity periods

3. Reduced efficiency and usability of the withdrawal system for legitimate users

4. Possible loss of user trust in the protocol
Increased difficulty for borrowers in managing their markets effectively

5. Potential disruption of normal market operations, especially during periods of low liquidity

## Proof of Concept
Initial Setup:
- Wildcat market with 1,000,000 tokens total supply
- Reserve ratio: 20% (reserveRatioBips = 2000)
- Withdrawal batch duration: 24 hours
- Delinquency fee: 5% (delinquencyFeeBips = 500)
- Delinquency grace period: 24 hours
- Current liquidity: 300,000 tokens (30% of total supply)

Step 1: Market State Analysis
The market's reserve requirement is 200,000 tokens (20% of 1,000,000). With 300,000 tokens in liquidity, there are 100,000 tokens available for withdrawals without causing delinquency.

Step 2: Attacker Preparation
The attacker prepares multiple accounts, each funded with 1,000 market tokens.

Step 3: Large Withdrawal Request
A legitimate user submits a withdrawal request for 250,000 tokens. This request initiates a new withdrawal batch.

Step 4: Attacker Execution
Immediately after the large request, the attacker uses their prepared accounts to submit multiple small withdrawal requests, totaling 50,000 tokens (50 requests of 1,000 tokens each).

Step 5: Batch Processing Mechanics
All withdrawal requests in the current batch (250,000 + 50,000 = 300,000 tokens) are recorded in the WithdrawalBatch struct. The scaledTotalAmount is set to 300,000, and scaledAmountBurned is initially 0.

Step 6: Borrower Action (Optional)
The borrower, noticing the large withdrawal requests, repays 50,000 tokens to the market. This increases the liquidity to 350,000 tokens, leaving 150,000 tokens available for withdrawals.

Step 7: Withdrawal Batch Expiry
After 24 hours, the withdrawal batch expires. The _processExpiredWithdrawalBatch function is called.

Step 8: Expired Batch Processing
The `_processExpiredWithdrawalBatch` function is called, which in turn calls `_applyWithdrawalBatchPayment` to process withdrawals:
- Available liquidity: 150,000 tokens
- Total requested in batch: 300,000 tokens
- The `_applyWithdrawalBatchPayment` function distributes liquidity proportionally:
scaledAmountBurned = (150,000 * 300,000) / 300,000 = 150,000
- The batch is marked as unpaid and added to the unpaid batches queue

Step 9: Withdrawal Execution
Users can now execute their withdrawals:
- Each withdrawal request receives: (requested amount / total batch amount) * available liquidity
- Large withdrawal: (250,000 / 300,000) * 150,000 = 125,000 tokens
- Each small withdrawal: (1,000 / 300,000) * 150,000 = 500 tokens
- The attacker's 50 small withdrawals collectively receive: 50 * 500 = 25,000 tokens

Step 10: Impact Analysis
- The large withdrawal request is only 50% fulfilled (125,000 out of 250,000)
- The attacker's requests are also 50% fulfilled, but spread across multiple small withdrawals
- The remaining unfulfilled amounts stay in the unpaid batch queue

Step 11: Subsequent Batches
The attacker can repeat this process in subsequent batches, continually diluting large withdrawal requests.

Step 12: Delinquency Consideration
If the borrower fails to maintain the required liquidity (200,000 tokens in this case), the market enters a delinquency state:
- After the 24-hour grace period, a 5% delinquency fee is applied
- This increases the cost for the borrower, potentially leading to further liquidity issues

Impact Demonstration:
1. Delayed large withdrawals: The legitimate user's 250,000 token withdrawal is significantly delayed, with only 50% processed in the first batch.

2. Fragmented liquidity: Available liquidity is distributed across many small withdrawals, making it harder for large withdrawals to be fulfilled quickly.

3. Potential for market instability: Continued manipulation could lead to a backlog of unfulfilled large withdrawals, potentially causing loss of trust in the protocol.

4. Borrower challenges: The borrower faces increased pressure to maintain liquidity or risk incurring delinquency fees, which could exacerbate liquidity issues.

This PoC demonstrates how an attacker can manipulate the withdrawal queue to disadvantage large withdrawal requests, potentially causing significant delays and eroding user trust in the protocol. This presents a significant risk to the fair operation of the Wildcat protocol.

## Tools Used
Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Enhance the withdrawal mechanism:
- Implement a minimum withdrawal amount to prevent the creation of many small batches
- Add a cooldown period between withdrawal requests from the same address
- Introduce a weighted queue system that considers both time and withdrawal size
- Implement a maximum number of active withdrawal requests per address
