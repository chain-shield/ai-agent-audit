# Vulnerability details

## Description
The `FixedTermLoanHooks` contract, which inherits from `MarketConstraintHooks`, allows borrowers to adjust interest rates on loans even during fixed-term periods when lenders are unable to withdraw their funds. This creates a vulnerability where borrowers could potentially attract lenders with high initial rates and then lower them once the funds are locked in the fixed term.

The interest rate setting mechanism, implemented in the `MarketConstraintHooks` contract, lacks specific checks for the fixed-term period status. While there are some protections in place, such as a temporary increase in the reserve ratio when rates are lowered and minimum rate limits, these are insufficient to fully protect lenders in fixed-term loans.

The `fixedTermEndTime` parameter, defined in the `FixedTermLoanHooks` contract, is not utilized in the interest rate adjustment logic, allowing rate changes at any time during the loan term. This oversight undermines the expected stability of fixed-term loans and exposes lenders to potential financial losses.

## Impacts
Financial Losses: Lenders may receive significantly lower returns than initially agreed upon, potentially resulting in substantial financial losses.

Exploitation Potential: Malicious borrowers could exploit this vulnerability by offering high initial rates to attract lenders, then lowering rates once funds are locked in the fixed term.

Undermining Core Functionality: The protocol's design goal of providing stable, predictable fixed-term loans is compromised, potentially affecting its overall utility and appeal to users.

## Proof of Concept

1. Borrower Creation:
 - A borrower creates a new fixed-term loan market using the `FixedTermLoanHooks` contract.
 - The borrower sets an attractive initial annual interest rate (e.g., 10%) and a fixed term of 1 year.

2. Lender Participation:
 - Multiple lenders, attracted by the high interest rate, deposit a total of 1,000,000 tokens into the market.
 - These funds are now locked for the 1-year fixed term, with lenders expecting to earn 100,000 tokens in interest.

3. Interest Rate Manipulation:
 - After a short period (e.g., 1 month), the borrower calls the `setAnnualInterestAndReserveRatioBips` function on the `WildcatMarketConfig` contract.
 - The borrower significantly lowers the annual interest rate from 10% to 2%.

4. Exploitation Result:
 - The `onSetAnnualInterestAndReserveRatioBips` function in `FixedTermLoanHooks` executes without any checks related to the fixed-term status.
 - The interest rate is successfully changed to 2%.
 - Lenders are unable to withdraw their funds due to the fixed-term lock.

5. Impact Demonstration:
 - Originally, lenders expected to earn 100,000 tokens in interest over the year.
 - With the new 2% rate, lenders will only earn approximately 20,000 tokens in interest.
 - This results in a loss of 80,000 tokens of expected interest for the lenders.

6. Mitigation Attempt:
 - The `MarketConstraintHooks` contract implements a temporary reserve ratio increase when the interest rate is lowered.
 - This increase is proportional to the magnitude of the interest rate reduction and lasts for 2 weeks.
 - While this doesn't prevent the interest rate change, it makes it more costly for the borrower to significantly lower the rate, as they must maintain a higher reserve during this period.

However, this mitigation is insufficient for fixed-term loans because:

 - It doesn't allow lenders to withdraw their funds if they're unsatisfied with the new rate.
 - The increased reserve ratio is temporary (2 weeks), while the fixed term may be much longer.
 - After 2 weeks, the reserve ratio returns to normal, but the lower interest rate remains in effect for the duration of the fixed term.

7. Repeated Exploitation:
 - The borrower could potentially repeat this process multiple times during the fixed term, further reducing the interest rate to the minimum allowed limit.

## Tools Used
Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement Fixed-Term Checks: Modify the `FixedTermLoanHooks` contract to include checks that prevent or significantly limit interest rate reductions during the fixed-term period. This could involve using the `fixedTermEndTime` parameter in the rate adjustment logic.

Time-Lock Mechanism: Introduce a time-lock mechanism for interest rate changes during fixed terms. This would require borrowers to signal rate changes in advance, giving lenders time to react before the change takes effect.
