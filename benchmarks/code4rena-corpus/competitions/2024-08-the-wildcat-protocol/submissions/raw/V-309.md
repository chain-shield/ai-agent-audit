# Vulnerability details

## Summary

The Wildcat protocol prioritizes security and regulatory compliance by incorporating a sanctioning mechanism. This mechanism utilizes a Sanctions Sentinel to identify sanctioned addresses, preventing them from engaging in transactions. While the protocol correctly handles the transfer of funds to an escrow contract when a sanctioned account attempts a withdrawal, a critical flaw exists in the executeWithdrawal function within the WildcatMarketWithdrawals contract that can lead to an inconsistent and incomplete transfer of funds.

## Details

When a user initiates a withdrawal via executeWithdrawal, the protocol checks if the user is sanctioned. If sanctioned, it creates an escrow contract specific to that user and attempts to transfer the user's entitled withdrawal amount to this escrow. This mechanism is designed to safeguard funds while adhering to sanctions.

The executeWithdrawal function, responsible for executing the withdrawal process, first calculates the amount to be withdrawn without considering the user's sanction status. This calculated amount, potentially representing the user's entire entitled withdrawal, is then used in subsequent steps, even if the user is sanctioned.

Due to the flawed logic, when a sanctioned user's withdrawal is processed, the safeTransfer function might still attempt to transfer the entire calculated amount to the escrow, even though this amount should have been deducted from the market's state and earmarked for the escrow in prior steps.

## Impact

This inconsistent handling of funds for sanctioned accounts can have several adverse consequences:

Incomplete Escrow Transfer: The safeTransfer function, when attempting to move more funds than available, will revert, resulting in the sanctioned user's funds remaining stuck in the market contract. This directly contradicts the protocol's objective of transferring such funds to an escrow.

Market State Discrepancy: As the transfer to the escrow might fail, the market's internal accounting could become inaccurate. The normalizedUnclaimedWithdrawals value, reflecting the total amount owed to users for withdrawals, might not be correctly adjusted, leading to discrepancies in the market's financial state.

Denial of Service: Reverted withdrawals due to this error can create a denial-of-service vulnerability. Sanctioned users, though intended to be blocked, could repeatedly attempt withdrawals, causing unnecessary transaction failures and potentially disrupting the market for other users.

## Scenario

- A user marked as sanctioned by the Sanctions Sentinel requests a withdrawal.
- The executeWithdrawal function is called, and it calculates the user's total withdrawable amount without considering the sanction status.
- The protocol identifies the user as sanctioned and creates an escrow contract.
- The safeTransfer function attempts to transfer the entire previously calculated amount to the escrow, which might exceed the actual amount available after deducting the sanctioned user's balance, leading to a revert.

## Fix

To rectify the inconsistent escrow transfer for sanctioned accounts:

Within the _executeWithdrawal function, after the sanction check, update the normalizedAmountWithdrawn variable to be zero if the account is sanctioned.
