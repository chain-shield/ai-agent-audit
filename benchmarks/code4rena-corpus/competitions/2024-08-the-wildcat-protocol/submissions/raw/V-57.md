# Vulnerability details

## Description
The `Wildcat` protocol contains a vulnerability related to the handling of unbounded `hooksData`, primarily during the market deployment process but with potential implications for ongoing market operations. The core issue lies in the `HooksFactory` contract, specifically in the `deployMarket` and `deployMarketAndHooks` functions, which accept arbitrary `hooksData` without size limits.

Key points:
1. Market Deployment: The `deployMarket` and `deployMarketAndHooks` functions in `HooksFactory` accept unbounded `hooksData`, potentially leading to excessive gas consumption or deployment failures.

2. Hook Implementation: While the protocol itself doesn't store large `hooksData`, custom hook implementations could potentially use this data in ways that affect ongoing market operations.

3. Indirect Effects: Functions in `WildcatMarketConfig` (e.g., `setProtocolFeeBips`, `nukeFromOrbit` and more) that interact with hooks could be indirectly affected if hook implementations process large stored data.

4. Access Control: The `AccessControlHooks` contract's `_tryValidateCredential` function, while internal, could potentially be influenced by large `hooksData` if used in custom hook implementations.

This vulnerability extends beyond a single contract, potentially affecting the entire lifecycle of markets within the Wildcat protocol.

## Impacts
The unbounded `hooksData` vulnerability in the Wildcat protocol presents several potential impacts:

1. Deployment Issues:
- Excessive gas consumption during market deployment, potentially causing transactions to fail or become prohibitively expensive.
- Possible denial of service if an attacker repeatedly deploys markets with large `hooksData`, congesting the network.

2. Operational Inefficiencies:
- Slightly increased gas costs for operations that interact with hooks (e.g., transfers, interest rate updates) if custom hook implementations use large stored data.
- Potential for unexpected behavior in market operations if malicious hook implementations leverage large stored data in decision-making processes.

3. Administrative Function Vulnerabilities:
- Functions like `setProtocolFeeBips` and `nukeFromOrbit` could become more expensive or unpredictable if hook implementations process large stored data.
- Increased vulnerability to out-of-gas errors in critical administrative functions.

4. Protocol Stability:
- While not directly causing state bloat, deployment of multiple markets with large `hooksData` could lead to increased difficulty in processing or querying market data.
- Potential for network congestion if many such deployments are attempted in a short period.

This proof of concept demonstrates a potential vulnerability in the Wildcat protocol related to unbounded data handling during market deployment.

Scenario:
A malicious actor with borrower privileges attempts to disrupt the Wildcat protocol by exploiting the lack of size limits on `hooksData` during market deployment.

Setup:
1. Identify a malicious actor who has been granted borrower privileges in the Wildcat protocol.

2. Prepare a valid set of market deployment parameters.

3. Create an excessively large `hooksData` payload (e.g., 100KB of arbitrary data).

Steps:
1. The attacker calls the `deployMarket` or `deployMarketAndHooks` function on the `HooksFactory` contract.

2. They provide valid market deployment parameters along with the excessively large `hooksData`.

3. Observe the gas consumption and behavior of the transaction.

4. If successful, immediately attempt to deploy another market with normal parameters.

5. Monitor the impact on other protocol operations and gas prices on the network.

Results:

1. The malicious market deployment transaction will consume an unusually high amount of gas.

2. Depending on network conditions and block gas limits, the transaction may:
a. Succeed, but at a very high gas cost.
b. Fail due to exceeding the block gas limit.

3. If successful, it may cause increased gas prices for subsequent transactions on the network.

4. Other users attempting to deploy markets or perform other gas-intensive operations may experience delays or failures.

Impact:
- Denial of Service: By repeatedly deploying markets with large `hooksData`, an attacker could potentially congest the network and prevent legitimate market deployments.

- Increased Costs: Even if transactions don't fail, the excessive gas consumption will increase costs for legitimate users trying to deploy markets or interact with the protocol.

- Protocol Disruption: The ability to reliably deploy new markets could be hampered, potentially slowing the growth and adoption of the Wildcat protocol.

- Resource Exhaustion: If many such markets are successfully deployed, it could lead to increased storage requirements for nodes maintaining the blockchain state.

** While the market deployment process is a significant vector of how an attacker can exploit this vulnerability, it's not the only one. **

The Wildcat protocol's handling of unbounded `hooksData` presents potential vulnerabilities, primarily during the market deployment process. While this proof of concept covers one scenario, there are additional scenarios where this issue could affect the protocol's operations:

1. Market Deployment Vulnerability:
- The `deployMarket` and `deployMarketAndHooks` functions in the `HooksFactory` contract accept arbitrary `hooksData` without size limits.
- This could lead to excessive gas consumption during market deployment, potentially causing deployment failures or unexpectedly high costs.
- In extreme cases, it might be possible to deploy markets with hooks that store large amounts of data, affecting subsequent operations.

2. Resource Consumption and Network Impact:
- An attacker with borrower privileges could potentially deploy multiple markets with large `hooksData`.
- While this wouldn't directly lead to state bloat, it could result in:
a) Increased difficulty in processing or querying market data due to the large amount of information associated with each market.
b) Potential network congestion if many such deployments are attempted in a short period.

3. Indirect Impact on Market Operations:
- While ongoing market operations don't directly handle large `hooksData`, custom hook implementations could potentially use deployment-time data in their logic.
This could lead to:
a) Slightly increased gas costs for operations that interact with hooks (e.g., transfers, interest rate updates).
b) Potential for unexpected behavior if a malicious hook implementation uses large stored data in its decision-making processes.

4. Administrative Function Vulnerabilities:
- Functions like `setProtocolFeeBips` and `nukeFromOrbit` in `WildcatMarketConfig` interact with hooks.
- If a hook implementation processes large stored data in these interactions, it could:
a) Make certain administrative operations more expensive than anticipated.
b) Potentially lead to unexpected behavior or increased vulnerability to out-of-gas errors in these critical functions.

While the PoC demonstrates the impact of large `hooksData` in the deployment phase, the potential for effects throughout the market's lifecycle exists. This underscores the importance of implementing size limits on `hooksData` and carefully auditing hook implementations to ensure they don't introduce vulnerabilities or unexpected behaviors in the protocol's operations.

It's important to note that exploiting these vulnerabilities would require borrower privileges and the ability to deploy custom hook implementations, which somewhat limits the pool of potential attackers. Nevertheless, implementing safeguards against unbounded data inputs would enhance the protocol's robustness, safety, trust, and predictability.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
1. Implement Size Limits for `hooksData`:
- Add a maximum size limit for the `hooksData` parameter in the `deployMarket` and `deployMarketAndHooks` functions in `HooksFactory.sol`. (Recommended)

2. Introduce Gas Limits for Hook Operations:
- Implement a gas limit for hook function calls in relevant contracts (e.g., WildcatMarketConfig.sol, WildcatMarketToken.sol).
- Use a try/catch pattern to handle potential out-of-gas errors in hook calls.
