# Vulnerability details

## Description
The Wildcat protocol contains a critical vulnerability in its interest rate adjustment mechanism. This flaw allows the borrower, who has privileged access to modify contract parameters, to manipulate the interest rate to their advantage, potentially leading to significant financial losses for lenders.

The vulnerability stems from the ability of the borrower to reduce the interest rate immediately before interest accrual periods, without any time delay or additional checks. This design flaw enables the borrower to minimize their interest payments unfairly.

The current implementation allows the interest rate to be changed and take effect immediately. When combined with the borrower's knowledge of when interest is calculated and applied, this creates an exploitable scenario where the borrower can consistently pay less interest than what lenders would expect based on the originally advertised rates.

This vulnerability undermines one of the core principles of lending protocols: the predictable and fair accrual of interest for lenders. It also introduces a significant element of centralization risk, as the economic security of the protocol heavily relies on the borrower's honest behavior.

## Impact
Financial Loss for Lenders: Lenders receive substantially less interest than expected, directly impacting their returns. In the demonstrated scenario, lenders lost approximately 12.5% of their expected interest earnings(see PoC).

- Lenders receive less interest than they would have under the original terms.
- The borrower pays less interest than they should have based on the original agreement.
- This undermines the trust and fairness of the lending platform.
- If exploited repeatedly, it could lead to significant financial losses for lenders over time.


## Proof of Concept
Add the following test to `WildcatMarket.t.sol`.
```javascript
function testExploitInterestRateChanges() public {
        uint256 initialDeposit = 1_000_000e18;

        _deposit(alice, initialDeposit);

        uint16 initialInterestRate = uint16(market.annualInterestBips());

        // Fast forward 1 day
        uint256 timeDelta = 1 days;
        vm.warp(block.timestamp + timeDelta);

        // Calculate expected interest at the initial rate
        uint256 expectedInterestRay = FeeMath.calculateLinearInterestFromBips(initialInterestRate, timeDelta);
        uint256 expectedScaleFactor = uint256(RAY).rayMul(RAY + expectedInterestRay);
        uint256 expectedInterest = initialDeposit.rayMul(expectedScaleFactor) - initialDeposit;

        // Borrower (attacker) reduces interest rate
        vm.startPrank(borrower);
        uint16 newInterestRate = uint16((uint256(initialInterestRate) * 75) / 100); // 25% reduction
        uint16 currentReserveRatio = uint16(market.reserveRatioBips());
        market.setAnnualInterestAndReserveRatioBips(newInterestRate, currentReserveRatio);
        vm.stopPrank();

        // Fast forward another day to allow changes to take effect
        vm.warp(block.timestamp + timeDelta);

        // Update state to trigger interest accrual
        market.updateState();

        // Calculate actual accrued interest
        uint256 actualInterest = market.totalSupply() - initialDeposit;

        console.log("Initial deposit:", initialDeposit);
        console.log("Expected interest at initial rate for 2 days:", expectedInterest * 2);
        console.log("Actual interest after rate reduction:", actualInterest);
        console.log("New interest rate:", market.annualInterestBips());

        // Assert that actual interest is less than expected
        assertLt(
            actualInterest,
            expectedInterest * 2,
            "Actual interest should be less than expected for 2 days at initial rate"
        );

        // Quantify the loss
        uint256 interestLoss = (expectedInterest * 2) - actualInterest;
        console.log("Interest loss due to rate reduction:", interestLoss);

        // Additional assertions to prove the vulnerability
        assertTrue(interestLoss > 0, "There should be a loss of interest");
        assertApproxEqRel(
            actualInterest,
            expectedInterest * 175 / 100,
            1e16,
            "Actual interest should be about 87.5% of expected (average of 100% and 75% for two days)"
        );
    }
```

The test demonstrates that a borrower can manipulate the interest rate to their advantage, reducing the amount of interest paid to lenders. By lowering the interest rate just before interest accrual, the borrower can significantly reduce their interest obligations.

The vulnerability allows direct financial gain for the attacker (borrower) at the expense of lenders.

The test shows that when the interest rate is reduced by 25% (from 1000 bips to 750 bips), the actual interest accrued over two days is substantially less than what would have been accrued if the rate remained unchanged.

How an attacker can exploit it:

An attacker (in this case, the borrower) waits until just before an interest accrual period.
They then use their privileged access to reduce the interest rate significantly.
This results in lower interest being accrued and paid out to lenders.

The test proves this vulnerability by showing:

Actual interest after rate reduction: 479508350534809532745

This loss represents about 12.5% of the expected interest, which aligns with our expectation of a 25% rate reduction applied halfway through the period.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Implement Time Delay: Introduce a mandatory time delay between when an interest rate change is proposed and when it takes effect. This delay should be long enough to allow lenders to react to the change if they disagree with it.

or

Multi-signature Requirement: Require multiple signatures or a governance vote for significant interest rate changes, reducing the risk of unilateral manipulation.
