S-978
Users loses a portion of their deposits when harvesting rewards for Strategies where the rewardToken is the same as the asset minted by the protocol where the strategy deposits funds to.
0xStalin
64
Sufficient
383d ago
Finding description and impact
Impact
Users loses a portion of their deposits.
Vault's accounting is messed up, not able to process withdrawals.
Strategies transfers all the deposits to the vault as if the deposits would be rewards.
Finding Description

All strategies are capable of claiming rewards that are generated on the third party protocols where the strategy's funds gets deposited.

For example, for an AaveV3Strategy, there are times when the AavePools offers extra rewards on top of the normal deposit's interest in order to incentivize deposits.
Same story for other Strategies, for example, MorphoStrategies.

The Rewards can be claimed via the ConcreteMultiStrategyVault.harvestRewards(), which it basically iterates over all the vault's strategies, and, attempts to claim all the rewardTokens from each strategy.

In summary, each Strategy claims the earned rewards, pays a portion of those earned rewards as a fee to the feeRecipient, and the rest of the rewards are sent to the Vault in order for the Vault's depositors to claim them.

The problem is when the rewardToken is the same token as the one that the Strategy receives for its deposits on the third party protocols

For example, on Aave, when the rewardToken is the same aToken as the aToken that the AavePool mints to the strategy for the deposited assets.
When the rewardToken is the same aToken as the one for the deposits, the StrategyBase.harvestRewards() will consider all the existing deposits as if they were rewards, as such (as demonstrated in the PoC of this report):
The computed fee for the feeRecipient will be way bigger than what it should be, also
All the remaining aTokens will be sent from the Strategy to the Vault.
This means that the Strategy will be left without any deposits, completely messing up the withdrawal process.

On the below PoC is demonstrated how harvesting rewards for Strategies where the rewardToken is the same token used to track the deposits messes up the Vault's accounting and causes users to lose a portion of their deposits.

In this case, we are using the USDS AavePool from the Ethereum Main Market as an example, which, at the moment of coding the PoC is offering as rewardToken the same aToken used to track the deposits (aEthUSDS), the steps on the PoC are as follows:

A vault with two AaveV3Strategies, one strategy has a 10% allocation and the other one has a 90% allocation.

The Strategy with the 90% allocation is the one that works with the USDS AavePool mentioned above. (The rewardToken is the same as the aToken used to track the deposits).
Two users deposits on the Vault and their assets are deposited onto the Strategies, which, ends up being deposited on the AavePools.
3 days passes and the rewards are harvested on the Vault.
At this point is where the problem occurs. During the process to harvest the rewards, on the Strategy with the 90% allocation, the rewardToken is the same aToken as the one that the Strategy is holding for all the deposited assets on the AavePool (aEthUSDS), and, because the claimed rewards are not tracked, but, rather, the Strategy only checks its own balance of the rewardToken, all the aTokens the strategy had for the deposited assets are considered as if they were claimed rewards.
As a result of this problem, claimedBalance will be equals to all the deposited assets + the claimed rewards, this causes two problems:
collectedFee is computed based on all the strategy's deposits + the earned rewards (As opposed to only using the earned rewards). This means, the feeRecipient will receive more than x100 times the assets that it should receive. (All those extra assets are actually taken from the depositor's assets, meaning, the vault's depositors will end up losing those assets)
All the aTokens are going to be transferred from the Strategy to the Vault, which, it will mess up the accounting because the strategy will no longer have any deposits to process request withdrawals.
After harvesting the rewards, the accounting is messed up, and the vault won't be able to process withdrawals because the strategy with the 90% allocation was left without deposits.

Recommended Mitigation Steps

The recommendation to mitigate this problem is to track all the claimed rewards, do not rely on the strategy balanceOf() the rewardToken

On the _getRewardsToStrategy(), track all the new claimed rewards, and when the rewards are harvested, update the tracking of the rewards accordingly (substract all the harvested rewards from the tracker).

Hey judge @picodes. I'd like to add a comment to clarify why S-978 is incorrectly duplicated with all the rest of the submissions.

First of all, let me clarify some key concepts that will help us to understand the difference between the reports.

baseAsset/underlyingToken/vaultAsset is the asset that is deposited on the ConcreteVault, and, from there, it is forwarded to a Strategy to be deposited on an external protocol (Aave/Morpho/...).
For example => USDC/USDT/SUSDC.

asset minted by the 3rd party protocol. When a Strategy deposits on a 3d party protocol (Aave), the 3rd party protocol mints a token to track the deposits, and in the future, to allow the redemption of the deposits + earned yield. For example => aUSDT/ aSUDSD.
For example, SUSDC is deposited on the ConcreteVault, and, then it is deployed to the AaveStrategy, which, it is deposited on Aave. => Aave will pull the SUSDC being deposited and will mint aSUSDC to the Strategy.

Now, with the above explanation in mind, let's dive into the differences between S-978 and the rest of the submissions.

S-978 is the only one that demonstrates the impact when the rewardToken is the same as the asset minted by the 3rd party protocol (i.e. aToken).

The impact is that all the deposits the Strategy has made on the 3rd party protocol will be considered rewards. This means, all the aTokens held by the strategy will be lost, and, without those aTokens, the Strategy won't be able to claim the deposited underlying, or in other words, all the deposits will be lost.

The rest of the duplicates only demonstrate the impact when the rewardToken is the same as the baseAsset/underlyingToken/vaultAsset

The impact is that the claimed rewards will be considered deposits of the Strategy, or in other words, the shareValue will be inflated because the Strategy will consider that there are more deposits for the same amount of shares. But, as we can see, the deposits are not lost, the minted tokens by the 3rd party protocol are still on the Strategy, which means, the Strategy can still withdraw/claim/redeem the deposited assets.

The above explanation means that there are 2 different bug families because the bug's root cause for each familiy is totally different, each bug family needs a specific solution for its own:

#978 => root cause is not tracking the harvested rewards and directly claiming all the token's balance held on the Strategy, this includes the aTokens minted for the deposits on Aave.
Rest of duplicates => root cause is the balanceOf(address(this)) on the totalAssets(). This is what causes the rewards being counted as if they were deposits too. But, this alone does not transfers the deposits to the ConcreteVault. The fix for this bug is to simply remove the balanceOf(address(this)) from the totalAssets().

All in all, S-978 is a separate bug than the rest of duplicates on F-35. Summary of the previous explanation:

deposits as rewards => Deposits will be lost . Minted assets by the 3rd party protocol (aTokens) are transferred to the ConcreteVault where users will claim their rewards, and, deposits will inflate the rewards per user.(S-978)

rewards as deposits => Inflates the shareValue. Each share will be worth more because each claimed reward is considered as if it were a deposit on the Strategy. (rest of duplicates)

I'm happy to clarify any further questions about this explanation, please let me know any doubts.
