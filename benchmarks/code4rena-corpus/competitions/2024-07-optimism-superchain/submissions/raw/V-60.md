# Vulnerability details

## Summary 

If a game does not reach `MAX_DEPTH`, it means that either the challenger or the defender has timed out. If there are uncontested claims in the same depth (from both challenger and defender), then the dispute creator will be able to get back their bond instead of the game winner sweeping all the winnings.

## Impact

The winner will not win all the bonds.

## Proof of Concept

Let's say there are 3 moves, and 4 `claimData` in total. Alice is the defendant and Bob is the challenger.

Alice creates the game. `claimData[0]` is her claim.

Bob attacks. `claimData[1]` is his claim. subgames[0] is populated with 1 (the first game). The depth is now 2.

Alice attacks. `claimData[2]` is Alice claim. subgames[1] is populated with 2 (the second game). The depth is now 3.

Bob defends at depth 2. `claimData[3]` is Bob's claim. subgames[1] is populated with 3 (the third game). The depth is now 3.

Now, there are 2 games at depth 3. subgames[2] is not populated since Bob went back to depth 1 and defend.

Alice decides not to play anymore because she realizes that her output root was wrong. She times out and Bob wins. By right, Bob should win both of Alice's bond, but he only wins the first one.

Since subgames[2] and subgames[3] have no value inside, but `claimData[2]` and `claimData[3]` is Alice and Bob's second dispute respectively, when `resolveClaim()` happens, `challengeIndicesLen == 0 && _claimIndex != 0` will pass.

```
 uint256[] storage challengeIndices = subgames[_claimIndex];
        uint256 challengeIndicesLen = challengeIndices.length;

        // Uncontested claims are resolved implicitly unless they are the root claim. Pay out the bond to the claimant
```

Two issue arises. Alice can claim before Bob, although it should be in order. Secondly, Alice can claim back her second bond even though she lost.

Alice calls `resolveClaim()` with `_claimIndex` as 2. `claimData[2]` is Alice claim. `counteredBy` is address(0), so Alice becomes the `recipient`, and gets back her bond.

## Tools Used

Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

If there is a timeout by either the challenger or the defender, they should not be getting any of their bond back.
