# Vulnerability details

## Impact
Failure to consider the aliased sender address prevents the execution of admin or otherwise permissioned functionality on a chain where transactions are batched by a centralized L2 sequencer. 

## Proof of Concept
[Optimism](https://docs.optimism.io/chain/differences#address-aliasing) offers methods for forced transaction inclusion. It is important that the aliased sender address is also checked within access control modifiers when verifying that the sender holds a permissioned role. This allows the functions to which they are applied to be called even in the event of sequencer downtime.

The most pertinent examples include:

`OwnableUpgradeable`::`_checkOwner` which is called in the `onlyOwner` modifier, which itself is applied to `OwnableUpgradeable`::`transferOwnership`, `DisputeGameFactory`::`setImplementation` ,`DisputeGameFactory`::`setInitBond`.

While potentially unlikely, a possible scenario could include:

An attacker identifies an exploit in the protocol on the source chain and plans to bridge stolen funds to the Ethereum mainnet. Assume the source chain is an L2 rollup that batches transactions for publishing onto the L1 chain via a centralized sequencer.

The L2 sequencer goes down; however, transactions can still be executed via forced inclusion on the L1 chain. The Attacker could either have forced or waited for this to happen.

The Attacker exploits the protocol and initiates a native token transfer, kicking off the 24-hour rate limit duration. Assume the outbound rate limit is hit on the L2, but the inbound limit is not exceeded on the Ethereum mainnet.

Access-controlled functions are not callable since they do not check the aliased sender address, so admin transactions cannot be force-included from L1, e.g. pause the protocol.

The rate limit duration passes, and the sequencer is still down – the attacker completes the outbound transfer (assuming attestations are made) and relays the message on the Ethereum mainnet.

Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Validation of the sender address against permissioned owner roles should also consider the aliased equivalents to allow access-controlled functionality to be executed via forced inclusion.
