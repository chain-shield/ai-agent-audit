# Vulnerability details

## Proof of Concept
Untrusted External Calls: The contract interacts with external contracts (like pay or the pool) without sufficient validation of the caller.
The pay function is called with parameters derived from the external _data input. If this input is controlled by a malicious actor, they can manipulate the behavior of the contract.
The function can call other contracts or execute methods that may not have been designed with the correct security assumptions, leading to potential vulnerabilities.
Proof of Concept:
Here's how a proof of concept might look for exploiting this vulnerability:

1. Malicious Contract Setup: A malicious contract could be created that calls uniswapV3SwapCallback and manipulates the amount0Delta or amount1Delta values to execute additional swaps or token transfers.
2. Execution:The attacker could exploit the callback to manipulate funds or trigger additional swaps in a manner that the original contract does not expect or allow, potentially draining tokens.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
1. Validate external contract addresses and their state before invoking functions.
2. Consider using established patterns for secure external calls.
3. When transferring tokens, prefer using transfer instead of call, as transfer forwards a limited amount of gas, minimizing the risk of reentrancy.
4. Implement strict access control measures to ensure only authorized contracts can call sensitive functions.
5. Implement circuit breakers that can halt the contract's functions in the case of detected anomalies or suspicious activity
