# Vulnerability details

## Proof of Concept

The vulnerability lies in Ramses Exchange's FeeDistributor contract, where the reward distribution mechanism fails to reduce the total rewards supply after each claim. This flaw allowed the attacker to repeatedly claim rewards with various NFT tokenIds, leveraging the unchanged tokenTotalSupplyByPeriod to inflate their claims. By resetting or splitting NFTs, the attacker bypassed tracking of claimed rewards per tokenId, effectively draining excess rewards.

**Impact**
The attacker exploited this vulnerability to claim nearly $93,000 in rewards by circumventing checks on the reward limits and period alignment. While liquidity provider funds remained secure, accumulated fees and incentives were significantly reduced.

**Attack Scenario**
The attacker deposited tokens linked to an NFT, claimed rewards, and reset the NFT to a new tokenId to avoid veWithdrawnTokenAmountByPeriod checks. This process was repeated across multiple periods using arbitrary getPeriodReward() calls, inflating available rewards without the necessary decrements in tokenTotalSupplyByPeriod.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
1. Update tokenTotalSupplyByPeriod to decrement after each reward claim to prevent inflated reward calculations.
2. Introduce a nonce or mapping to track claim counts per tokenId, ensuring that each tokenId can only claim rewards once per period.
3. Add a period validation in getPeriodReward() to verify that specified periods match the current time, blocking retroactive claims.
