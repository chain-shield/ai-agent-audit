# Vulnerability details

## Description:
The `collectProtocolFees` function is publicly accessible, meaning any address can call it without restriction. This open access introduces potential risks, as unauthorized users could trigger fund distribution and potentially interfere with protocol operations.

Additionally, if the `setTreasuryFees` function is not called to set an initial treasury fee after the deployment of `Feecollector` contract , the default `treasuryFees` value remains at `zero`. As a result, any funds collected by `collectProtocolFees` would be distributed entirely to `voters`, leaving `the treasury` with `zero` allocation. This could occur due to an oversight or misconfiguration post-deployment, inadvertently depriving the treasury of its intended share.

## Impact:

- Unrestricted Access to `collectProtocolFees`: Unauthorized users can call the function, which may lead to potential disruption in fee collection or unintended distribution of funds.
- Zero Treasury Fee on Uninitialized State: Without an initial `treasuryFees` setting, 0% of funds will be allocated to the treasury, contrary to intended distribution and potentially reducing protocol funds.
This issue may arise from a missed `setTreasuryFees` call, unintentionally resulting in 100% of the protocol’s revenue being diverted to voters instead of being partially allocated to the treasury.

## Proof of Concept
-Unrestricted Access: Any external address can call `collectProtocolFees`, triggering fund distribution.
Zero Treasury Fee:

- If `setTreasuryFees` is not called after deployment, `treasuryFees will default to 0.
When `collectProtocolFees` is called, the entire amount is allocated to voters, as no percentage is reserved for the treasury.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
- Implement Access Control on `collectProtocolFees`:
Restrict access to the function to only authorized addresses such as `onlyTreasury` or any others owner or a designated contract role, to prevent unauthorized calls.
- Set Initial Treasury Fee in Constructor:
To avoid unintended zero-fee settings, initialize `treasuryFees` within the constructor to a predefined value. This ensures the treasury receives a portion of protocol fees immediately upon deployment.
```diff
function initialize( address _treasury, address _voter , + uint256 _initialTreasuryFee) external initializer {
  + require(_initialTreasuryFee <= BASIS, "Invalid fee");
        treasury = _treasury;
        voter = IVoter(_voter);
        + treasuryFees = _initialTreasuryFee;
    }
```
