# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The PrelaunchPoints contract, part of the LoopFi protocol, is designed to handle various user interactions, including staking, claiming, and managing digital assets within a DeFi ecosystem. The contract assumes a standard behavior of ERC-20 tokens for these operations. However the contract does not accommodate the unique characteristics of rebasing tokens, such as those whose supply dynamically adjusts, causing individual balances to rebase up or down periodically without any transfer transactions.

The lack of support for rebasing tokens can lead to several significant issues:
- Incorrect Token Balances - Users holding rebasing tokens might see their balances in the PrelaunchPoints contract become inaccurate after a rebase occurs. This is because the contract does not listen for rebase events or adjust stored balances accordingly.
- Asset Lock-Up - In scenarios where the token balance in the contract is less than expected due to a negative rebase, users may be unable to withdraw the full amount of tokens they originally deposited.
- Financial Discrepancies - Since rebasing can affect the total supply and thus the individual token balances, financial calculations based on static token amounts stored in the contract will become incorrect over time, potentially leading to losses for users or the protocol itself.

## Proof of Concept
The following sections of the PrelaunchPoints contract reveal where the logic fails to account for rebasing tokens:
- Direct link to code: [PrelaunchPoints.sol on GitHub](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-05-loop/blob/main/src/PrelaunchPoints.sol)

The contract interacts with tokens assuming that their balance will remain consistent post-deposit unless actively transferred by the contract. This assumption fails with rebasing tokens as their supply mechanisms adjust balances autonomously. There is no mechanism within the contract to handle or recognize these changes, leading to discrepancies between the recorded balances in the contract and the actual balances available.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
 For rebasing tokens, when they go down in value, you should have a method to update the cached reserves accordingly, based on the balance held. But when they go up in value, you should add a method to actually transfer the excess tokens out of the protocol (possibly directly to users).
