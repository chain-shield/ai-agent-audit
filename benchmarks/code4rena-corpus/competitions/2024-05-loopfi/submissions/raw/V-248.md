# Vulnerability details

## Impact
Impact: High, as the victim loses their funds

Likelihood: Low, as it comes at a cost for the attacker

## Proof of Concept

The function 

```solidity
function lock(address _token, uint256 _amount, bytes32 _referral) external {
        if (_token == ETH) {
            revert InvalidToken();
        }
        _processLock(_token, _amount, msg.sender, _referral);
    }
```

Is used by users to lock their tokens but the function relies on 

```approve(address spender, uint256 amount)``` to allow users to grant PrelaunchPoints contract to transfer the amount of tokens on their behalf.


Here's the attack scenario 

Attacker mint 1000 ether of the ERC20Token

When attacker approve a large amount of tokens to the contract and then revoke the approval, the lock function will revert when the contract tries to transfer the tokens of other users.

When other users try to lock their ERC20Token in the contract,the transaction will reverts, so attacker prevent the contract from transferring tokens from other users account, disrupting their ability to lock their tokens.

Here's a test function that demonstrate the attack.

## Recommended Mitigation
Instead of allowing users to approve the entire lockAmount at once, consider allowing  users to approve tokens incrementally.
Implement additional checks in the ```lock``` Function
