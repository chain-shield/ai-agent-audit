# Vulnerability details

## Impact
Failing to validate that input addresses are non-zero in Solidity functions, especially those handling critical operations like token transfers or withdrawals, can have several significant consequences:

Risk of Fund Loss: Directing transactions to `address(0)` can lead to irreversible loss of funds since the zero address is not meant to hold or transact with tokens.
Reverted Transactions and Gas Wastage: Functions called with `address(0)` might fail, especially if the underlying token standard (like `ERC20`) does not permit operations involving the zero address. This results in transaction reverts, wasting gas and degrading user experience.
Security Vulnerabilities: Malicious users could exploit the absence of this validation to disrupt contract operations, potentially leading to denial of service or other contract manipulations.
