# Vulnerability details

## Impact
I am working under the premise that the backend actively monitors the events emitted by the smart contract, utilizing them for a variety of purposes as outlined in the documentation. A potential security concern arises when a malicious actor manipulates the events by introducing a discrepancy between the `Claimed` and `SwappedTokens` events. This manipulation can be achieved by sending a minimal amount of ETH to the contract moments before a user initiates a claim transaction.

## Proof of Concept

Bob intends to claim the ezETH he has locked up and has provided swap data for converting ezETH to ETH. However, a malicious user sends a small amount of ETH, say 0.0001 ETH, just before Bob's claim transaction is processed. This results in the SwappedTokens event being emitted with the details `SwappedTokens(ezETH, 1ezETH, 1ETH)`, while the Claimed event incorrectly reflects `Claimed(msg.sender, ezETH, 1.001ETH)`. This discrepancy could lead to significant issues on the backend, as the Claimed event suggests a different amount of ETH was claimed compared to the SwappedTokens event.

The root cause of this issue lies in the _fillQuote function, where the initial balance is recorded as `0.0001 ETH`. The calculation for boughtETHAmount results in a final balance of `1.0001 - 0.0001 = 1`, indicating that 1 ETH was bought. However, when the claim is processed, the `claimedAmount` is determined by the contract's balance, which remains at `1.0001 ETH`, leading to the mismatch.

## Tools Used
Manual Review 

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

Return the value from the `_fillQuote` and use that as the claimedAmount instead
