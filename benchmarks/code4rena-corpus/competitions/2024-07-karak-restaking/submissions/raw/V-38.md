# Vulnerability details

## Vulnerability

In `requestUpdateVaultStakeInDSS` when the ignoreFailure parameter is set to `!requestStakeUpdate.toStake`, it means that if the operator is staking (toStake is true), the hook cannot revert the transaction, and if the operator is unstaking (toStake is false), the hook can revert the transaction.

## Impact

DSS cannot enforce rule setting, malicious operators may be able to stake against rules. - Assets not a risk, but the function of the protocol or its availability could be impacted (See PoC Scenario #1)

Malicious DSS can hold operator funds hostage - Assets can be stolen/lost/compromised directly (See PoC Scenario #2)

## Proof of Concept
Scenario 1

1. A malicious operator, Eve, wants to stake in a DSS that has specific staking requirements and rules.
The DSS has implemented the `requestUpdateStakeHook` to enforce these rules and validate staking requests.
However, the `requestUpdateVaultStakeInDSS` function has the ignoreFailure parameter set to `!requestStakeUpdate.toStake`, allowing Eve to bypass the DSS's rule enforcement when staking.

2. Eve calls the `requestUpdateVaultStakeInDSS` function, passing the necessary parameters to initiate a staking request.
Since Eve is staking (`toStake` is true), the `ignoreFailure` parameter is set to true due to the bug.
The `requestUpdateStakeHook` is called on the DSS contract, but even if the DSS tries to revert the transaction due to Eve not meeting the staking requirements, the revert is ignored.
The staking request proceeds, and Eve successfully stakes in the DSS, bypassing the intended rule enforcement.

3. The DSS is unable to enforce its staking rules and requirements effectively.
Malicious operators like Eve can stake in the DSS even if they don't meet the specified criteria.

Scenario 2 

1. A malicious DSS wants to prevent operators from unstaking their funds, effectively holding their funds hostage.
The `requestUpdateVaultStakeInDSS` function has the `ignoreFailure` parameter set to `!requestStakeUpdate.toStake`, allowing the malicious DSS to revert unstaking requests.
An honest operator, Alice, has staked funds in the malicious DSS and now wants to unstake.

2. Alice calls the `requestUpdateVaultStakeInDSS` function, passing the necessary parameters to initiate an unstaking request.
Since Alice is unstaking (toStake is false), the `ignoreFailure` parameter is set to false due to the bug.
The `requestUpdateStakeHook` is called on the malicious DSS contract.
The malicious DSS takes advantage of this vulnerability and reverts the unstaking request in the hook, preventing Alice from unstaking her funds.
Alice's unstaking request fails, and her funds remain staked in the malicious DSS against her will.

3. The malicious DSS can hold operator funds hostage by reverting unstaking requests.
Operators like Alice are unable to unstake their funds when desired, losing control over their staked assets.
This vulnerability creates a significant risk for operators and undermines the trust and reliability of the staking ecosystem.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

it is crucial to correct the `ignoreFailure` parameter logic in the  `requestUpdateVaultStakeInDSS` function. The parameter should be set to `requestStakeUpdate.toStake` to ensure that the DSS can enforce its rules when staking and that operators can freely unstake their funds when desired.
