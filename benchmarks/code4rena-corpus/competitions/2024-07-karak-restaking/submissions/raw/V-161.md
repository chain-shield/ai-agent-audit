# Vulnerability details

## Impact
Users while redeeming their corresponding assets from the vault, while buring their shares, may not get their exact assets out due to low slippage

## Proof of Concept

The problem is block.timestamp is the  executed timestamp in the mempool & in the mempool the transactions are arranged and executed in an arbitrary order , so any  particular withdrwal initiated by the user may get executed first than the one's that should have been executed.

```
 function finishRedeem(bytes32 withdrawalKey)
        external
        nonReentrant
        whenFunctionNotPaused(Constants.PAUSE_VAULT_FINISH_REDEEM)
    {
        (VaultLib.State storage state, VaultLib.Config storage config) = _storage();

        WithdrawLib.QueuedWithdrawal memory startedWithdrawal = state.validateQueuedWithdrawal(withdrawalKey);

        uint256 shares = startedWithdrawal.shares;
        if (shares > maxRedeem(address(this))) revert RedeemMoreThanMax();
        uint256 redeemableAssets = convertToAssets(shares);

        delete state.withdrawalMap[withdrawalKey];

        _withdraw({
            by: address(this),
            to: startedWithdrawal.beneficiary,
            owner: address(this),
            assets: redeemableAssets,
            shares: shares
        });
```
So any user can see their exact amount not getting redeemed, due to lack of slippage protection .
Their also lies another problem with two step withdrawal problem, any user can block a particular block with stuffing small withdrawl transactions which might result in any user failure to withdraw their assets or in worst case scenario their operator get slashed

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Have a slippage tolerance for a amount of buffer amount they are willing to accept .
And also have a check for minimum amount required to withdraw , so that users don't spam / block withdraw process
