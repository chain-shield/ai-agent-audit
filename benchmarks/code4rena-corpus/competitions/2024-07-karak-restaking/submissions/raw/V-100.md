# Vulnerability details

Centralization Risk for trusted owners

Contracts have owners with privileged rights to perform admin tasks and need to be trusted to not perform malicious updates or drain funds


Key Points of the Vulnerability
Privileged Rights: The owner or administrator has special rights that ordinary users do not possess. These rights are necessary for maintaining and managing the contract but also pose a risk if not handled correctly.

Trust Requirement: Users of the contract must trust the owner to act in their best interests and not abuse their privileges. If the owner is malicious or becomes compromised, they could perform harmful actions, such as:

Draining funds from the contract.
Deploying malicious updates.
Changing important configurations to benefit themselves.
Centralized Control: This ownership model introduces a level of centralization to an otherwise decentralized system. While this is sometimes necessary for practical purposes, it contradicts the ethos of decentralization and can be a single point of failure.

Mitigating the Risk
Multi-Signature Wallets: Use a multi-signature (multi-sig) wallet for administrative tasks, where multiple parties must approve a transaction before it can be executed. This reduces the risk of a single point of failure.

Timelocks: Implement timelocks on critical functions to delay their execution, giving users time to react if a malicious action is initiated.

Role-Based Access Control: Use more granular role-based access control instead of a single owner. Different administrative tasks can be assigned to different roles with limited scopes.

Transparent Governance: Use decentralized governance models where contract changes are proposed and voted on by the community or token holders.
