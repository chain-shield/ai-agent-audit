# Vulnerability details

## Impact
A malicious `Operator` would not get the slashing penalty when they behave maliciously towards a `DSS` 

## Proof of Concept
A `vault` is usually staked to any `DSS` of the `Operator's` choice and can be staked to a max of `32 DSS`. Staking a `vault` to a large number of `DSS's` (maybe 15, 16, 19, 24 or more) could be seen as "Overleveraging". 
   These `DSS's` have a slashable percentage in the event of an `Operator` operating maliciously. The `DSS` can jail a certain vault for overleveraging. As the docs mentioned:
> The effects of jailing are arbitrary and can be decided by the `DSS`. 
  This can range from no rewards to not allowing the operator to perform any tasks and forcing them to unallocate from the DSS.

The issue here is that a `vault` might not necessarily be overleveraging but can be registered to only 3-4 `DSS's` that have a high slashing percentage. By just checking for the max `DSS` registered to a vault, it won't be seen as "Overleveraging" and in the case where the `Operator` performs malicously across the `DSS's` and tries to be slashed atleast one or two `DSS` will lose out thereby leaving the malicious `Operator` unslashed. 
From the code we can see that the 
```solidity
`MAX_SLASHING_PERCENT_WAD = HUNDRED_PERCENT_WAD`
```
 Meaning that a `DSS` can set a slashing percentage of up to a 100%.
Lets say a `Vault` is registered to 4 `DSS's` with about 35% slashing percentage each, in the event of malicious activity by the `Operator`, all of them can't and won't be able to slash the Vault as at least one or two will loose out and this violates the intended design on what and how slashing should work.  

## Tools Used
Manual Review

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Asides from checking if a vault is "overleveraged", it should be able to check or oversee the slashing percentage a couple of DSS's registered to a vault have so as to prevent this type of occurences.
