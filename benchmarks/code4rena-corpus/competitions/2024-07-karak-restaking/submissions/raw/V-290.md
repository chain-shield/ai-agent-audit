# Vulnerability details

## Impact

The startRedeem and finishRedeem functions in the vault contract enable users to redeem their assets. However, there is a significant issue due to the way assets are calculated at different times during the process. The startRedeem function uses the convertToAssets function to determine the amount of assets equivalent to the shares being redeemed, based on the totalAssets at the time of the call. This amount is recorded and emitted in an event.

However, the `finishRedeem` function, which can be called after a delay (e.g., 9 days), also calls `convertToAssets` to determine the redeemable assets based on the current totalAssets. Since the totalAssets can change over time due to deposits, withdrawals, and other activities, the amount of assets calculated during `finishRedeem` can differ significantly from what was calculated during `startRedeem`.

As the time constraint is used to stop the staker to not front-run the slashing process but totalSupply can also effect the asset calculations.
also the amount of asset event emitted in `startredeem` can be different from the the actual asset minted in `finishredeem`

Inaccurate Redemption: Users may receive a different amount of assets than what was calculated and recorded during startRedeem, leading to potential losses

## Proof of Concept

Step 1: User Starts Redeem:
A user initiates the redeem process by calling `startRedeem` with a certain number of shares.
The `convertToAssets` function calculates the amount of assets based on the totalAssets at that time.
```javascript
uint256 assets = convertToAssets(shares);

```

Step 2: totalAssets Changes:
Over the next few days, other users deposit and withdraw assets, changing the totalAssets of the vault.

Step 3: User Finishes Redeem:
After the required delay, the user calls finishRedeem to complete the redemption.
The convertToAssets function calculates the redeemable assets based on the current totalAssets.
```javascript
uint256 redeemableAssets = convertToAssets(shares);

```
Result:

If totalAssets has increased due to new deposits, the user will receive more assets than initially calculated.
If totalAssets has decreased (e.g., due to withdrawals), the user will receive fewer assets, potentially leading to a revert if the assets are insufficient.
