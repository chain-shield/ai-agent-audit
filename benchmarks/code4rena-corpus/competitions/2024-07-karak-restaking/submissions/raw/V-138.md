# Vulnerability details

### [H-1] Lack of checks allows a `DSS` contract to register as `Operator` to itself

**Description:** According to the contest page "`Operators` perform tasks for the `DSS` in exchange for rewards, and the `DSS` has the ability to `slash` the funds that `Operators` have delegated". Furthermore, a `DSS` has the ability to `Jail` an `Operator` if they have "valid reasons" not to accept a certain `Operator`. I also went through the official docs of Karak and studied the architecture of the protocol of both [v1](https://docs.karak.network/protocol/v1/overview) and [v2](https://docs.karak.network/protocol/v2/dss) and I didn't find any instance where the sponsor mentions that this is deemed as an intended design decision.

Judging based on the architecture of the protocol, the natspec of the `Core::registerOperatorToDSS` function, the official documentation, the contest page, and the Code Walkthrough video that the sponsor provided for C4, I believe it is a reasonable assumption that `DSS` and `Operator` should be two different entities and a `DSS` shouldn't be allowed to register as an `Operator` to itself.

**Impact:** This can be seen as a "conflict of interests". In a normal scenario, the `DSS` would want to reward the `Operators` that perform the tasks that are provided to them, and `slash` or `jail` malicious `Operators` if needed. If a `DSS` is registered as an `Operator` to itself, there is no incentive for the `DSS` to punish the `Operator` as that means that it would punish itself. This also implies that the `Operator` can behave maliciously, because it will never get `slashed` or `jailed`. Furthermore, the `DSS` is allowed to distribute the rewards arbitrarily. Suppose a `DSS` has 10 `Operators` registered, one of them being itself. In that case, it can distribute up to 100% of the rewards back to itself, since it also has the `Operator` role, which will impact the revenue of honest `Operators`.

This doesn't seem to be an intended design choice and even if it was, it may need to be reconsidered because of this conflict of interests.
