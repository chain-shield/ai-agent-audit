# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The `finishRedeem` function in [Vault.sol](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-07-karak/blob/f5e52fdcb4c20c4318d532a9f08f7876e9afb321/src/Vault.sol#L157) is vulnerable to a front-running attack due to the lack of a time-based delay check. This issue allows malicious actors to exploit the immediate processing of withdrawal requests, potentially leading to financial losses for legitimate users.

https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-07-karak/blob/f5e52fdcb4c20c4318d532a9f08f7876e9afb321/src/Vault.sol#L157C5-L189C1

The `finishRedeem` function processes queued withdrawal requests by converting shares to assets and transferring them to the beneficiary. However, it does not include a time-based delay check, which creates a vulnerability that can be exploited through front-running and affects the users.

## Proof of Concept
The function deletes the withdrawal entry once processed, preventing someone from sitting on the queued withdrawal. However, this doesn't directly address front-running slashes.

Front-running slashes, in this context, refer to the risk where an attacker or malicious user might manipulate the timing and sequence of transactions to benefit from(griefing) or avoid penalties (slashes). This can happen if someone can predict and exploit the order of transactions to their advantage.


Front-running slashes, in this context, refer to the risk where an attacker or malicious user might manipulate the timing and sequence of transactions to benefit from or avoid penalties (slashes). This can happen if someone can predict and exploit the order of transactions to their advantage. In a vault or staking system, this often involves manipulating withdrawal requests to avoid penalties or to gain an unfair advantage.

Here’s a detailed explanation of how the provided finishRedeem function and overall withdrawal process might be vulnerable to such attacks:
- 1: An attacker observes that a legitimate user (UserA) has initiated a withdrawal (`startRedeem`), the attacker predicts that UserA's withdrawal will soon be processed (finishRedeem) since it can be calleded by anyone as seen [here.sol](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-07-karak/blob/f5e52fdcb4c20c4318d532a9f08f7876e9afb321/src/Vault.sol#L151C4-L156C86).

- 2: The withdrawal is queued with the current timestamp recorded in `
state.withdrawalMap[withdrawalKey].start`.

- 3: AttackerB observes `UserA's` withdrawal initiation and knows the exact timestamp.
AttackerB predicts that `UserA's` withdrawal will be processed soon.

Without the time-based delay check, `AttackerB` can immediately call `finishRedeem` for their own withdrawal or perform other actions that depend on the immediate processing of UserA's withdrawal.

Now let's see how this can affect user;
An asset is slashed in the vault primarily when an operator that has delegated native staked ETH is found to be at fault, such as failing to perform assigned tasks adequately or engaging in malicious behavior.

The fault could be due to operator misbehavior or failure in fulfilling the required tasks as per the Distributed Secure Services (DSS) guidelines.
Once a fault is detected, the slashing process is initiated. 

This is handled by the `slashAssets` function in the vault's smart contract. The function allows the core contract to specify the amount of assets to be slashed and the address of the slashing handler.

The slashed assets are then calculated and transferred to the slashStore. The slashStore acts as a common address to accumulate all slashed assets from different operators, making it easier for the slashing handler to decide what to do with these assets.

However slashes can happen anytime as long it finds a fault then it comes to, an attacker exploits this function during a slashing period by griefing(no profit for himself but damage to users) example(Front-running). The initiator of `startReedem` puts the user at risk since when an operator is slashed, the total assets in the `Vault.sol` decrease by the slashing amount. This is emulated as assets being transferred out of the vault to the `slashingHandler`.

According to the documentation, the `slashAssets` function in the vault allows the core contract to transfer specified asset amounts to the `slashingHandler`. The function is triggered when there is a slashing event, which can occur due to various reasons such as operator misconduct or failure to perform tasks adequately as determined by the DSS (Distributed Secure Services).

When an operator is slashed, the total assets in the Vault.sol decrease by the slashing amount. This is emulated as assets being transferred out of the vault to the `slashingHandler`. 
Therefore, if a slashing event occurs before or during the redemption process, the assets involved in that redemption could indeed be affected by slashing, reducing the amount that can be withdrawn or redeemed.

So users who can finish redeem on behalf of another user can obviously put them at risk by redeeming when slashing of assets is going on.

>It was clearly stated that the function can be called by anyone on behalf of the staker after a required delay period has passed, but this was never don in the `finishRedeem` function.

>Scenerio
`UserA` and `AttackerB` initiates a withdrawal by calling `startRedeem` with 100 shares. The withdrawal is queued with the current timestamp recorded.
`AttackerB` observes the initiation of `UserA's` withdrawal and predicts the exact timestamp. Knowing that the `finishRedeem` function does not have a time-based delay, `AttackerB` quickly calls `finishRedeem` for his own withdrawal.
A slashing event occurs `AttackerB` knows about that and can still call `finishRedeem` for `userA`, reducing the total assets in the vault. This event affects the assets that `UserA` can withdraw.
`UserA's` withdrawal is processed after the slashing event. The assets that `UserA` can withdraw are reduced due to the slashing, resulting in a financial loss for `UserA`, since a small portion has been reduced from the [totalAsset](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-07-karak/blob/f5e52fdcb4c20c4318d532a9f08f7876e9afb321/src/Vault.sol#L198) slashed.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps      
Incorporate a withdrawal time delay from when the msg.sender who initiated `redeeem`
```
startedWithdrawal.start
```
Plus
```
config.withdrawalDelay
```
something like this:
```
require(block.timestamp >= startedWithdrawal.start + config.withdrawalDelay, "Withdrawal too early");
```
However this might not save the user from the ongoing slashes though but reduce the risk of front-running.
