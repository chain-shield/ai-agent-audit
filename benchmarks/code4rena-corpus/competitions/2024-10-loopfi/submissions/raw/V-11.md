# Vulnerability details

## Proof of Concept
 Overview :

* In the `_doTransferOutRewards `function on `RewardManager` contract, the accrued rewards for users are reset to zero immediately after attempting to transfer those rewards. This design poses significant risks, particularly if the transfer fails due to various issues such as insufficient gas or re-entrancy attacks. Below, we analyze the implications of this design choice and recommend mitigation strategies.

* The following line of code is responsible for resetting the accrued rewards for users:

```
userReward[tokens[i]][user].accrued = 0;
```

Impacts :
* Loss of Accrued Rewards:

Issue: If the transfer of rewards fails for any reason (e.g., insufficient gas, contract execution error, or a re-entrancy attack), the accrued rewards are reset to zero. This means that users may lose their rewards without ever receiving them.
Example: A user who has accrued 100 tokens may initiate a withdrawal, but if the transfer fails, their accrued amount will be set to zero, effectively erasing their balance. This situation can lead to significant user dissatisfaction and loss of trust in the contract.

* Reentrancy Attack Vector:

Issue: The immediate resetting of accrued rewards opens a window for potential reentrancy attacks. If an attacker exploits the transfer function before the rewards are reset, they may drain additional rewards while leaving the user with zero accrued rewards.
Example: An attacker could exploit the reward transfer function to re-enter before the state is updated, allowing them to extract rewards multiple times while leaving the legitimate user with nothing.

* User Experience Degradation:

Issue: The possibility of losing accrued rewards without receiving them diminishes the overall user experience and may deter users from interacting with the contract in the future.
Example: Users might become wary of interacting with the contract, fearing that any failed transaction could result in permanent loss of their earned rewards.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

* Implement a Pull-over-Push Withdrawal Pattern:

Recommendation: Instead of automatically transferring rewards and resetting the accrued amount immediately, implement a withdrawal pattern. This pattern allows users to claim their rewards voluntarily, reducing the risk of losing accrued amounts due to failed transfers.

Implementation:

* Create a separate function for users to claim their rewards. This function should validate the user's accrued rewards before transferring them.

* Update the accrued rewards only after the transfer is confirmed successful.
