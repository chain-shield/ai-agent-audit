# Vulnerability details

## Vulnerbility Details

In `Cred.sol`'s `buyShareCredFor` function:
```solidity
function buyShareCredFor(uint256 credId_, uint256 amount_, address curator_, uint256 maxPrice_) public payable {
    if (curator_ == address(0)) revert InvalidAddressZero();
    _handleTrade(credId_, amount_, true, curator_, maxPrice_);
}
```

The function only checks whether `curator_` (which is the account that `msg.sender` is buying the share for) is equals to `address(0)`. It does not check whether `curator_` has given permission to `msg.sender` or whatsoever. 

Hence the attacker can buy 1 share for the victim in order to lock their shares for `SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD`=`10 minutes`. 

Which leads to the following 2 senarios that the attacker can exploit to affect the victim and manipulate the market price of the share due to low float (`MAX_SUPPLY` only `999`).

## Senario 1 (Market manipulation while letting the victim take on unnecessary risk):

1. Suppose `Alice` is a big shareholder of a cred.
2. `Alice` no longer believes in that particular cred's popularity and wants to sell her shares.
3. Attacker `Bob` (who is also a shareholder) on the other hand is extremely bullish on the price of the shares in the next `10 minutes` (which he could have came into conclusion through either monitoring the mempool for buy orders or having knowledge about an upcoming social publicity of the cred)
4. Attacker `Bob` frontruns `Alice`'s sell order, with a `buyShareCredFor` order for `curator_ = Alice`, with 1 share.
5. After `Bob` buys 1 share for `Alice`, `Alice`'s `lastTradeTimestamp` will be updated to `block.timestamp` and `Alice`'s sell orders (or any other sell orders that she send within the next `SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD` duration) will revert resulting in her shares being temporarily locked.
6. Suppose `Bob` is right, and many buyers came along to buy shares of the cred, raising the price siginificantly due to the rather quadratic nature of the bonding curve.
7. Right before `SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD` ends, `Bob` can then sell his own shares before `Alice`'s shares unlock.
<hr/>

Note that operation 7 is **different** from simply frontrunning `Alice`'s sale of her shares as `Bob` is able to both benefit from the desperate buyers pushing the share price up during the `SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD` as well as being able to sell before big shareholder `Alice`.

Whereas if this bug did not exist, `Bob` would not be able to benefit from the price caused by both events, but rather will have to <ins>choose between **either**</ins>
* Frontrunning big shareholder `Alice` to sell before she pushes down the price of the shares through selling. (`Bob` will not be unable to benefit from the buyers buying since this happens **before** the buying)
* <ins>**Or**</ins> Letting `Alice` sell first, hold his shares, and bet on the fact that buyers will push the price of the shares back higher.

Given the quadratic nature of the bonding curve, being able to benefit from both events will bring about quite a significant profit for `Bob` and will outweigh the cost needed by him to buy 1 share for `Alice`.

This senario is also unfair to the buyers buying as `Alice` has already intended to sell her shares, and now `Bob` has successfully carried out market manipulation and took advantage of desperate buyers who are willing to buy at a higher price.

<ins>**Impact for Alice includes:**</ins>
1. Although she is able to benefit from buyers pushing the share price up, `Bob` selling near the end of `Alice`'s `SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD` means `Alice` is helpless against how much `Bob` can dump the shares during `SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD`.
2. If `Bob` is wrong about this bullish bet, and the share price crashes, `Alice` will lose alot of money which is unfair to `Alice` as she never intended to activate `SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD` on her own will but rather it was `Bob` who activated her lock period maliciously to carry out his bullish bet on the share price.

Hence, this senario has shown how `Bob` forces `Alice` to participate in this bullish bet of his, beyond her will, risking her money.

## Senario 2 (Griefing Attacks):

1. `Alice` is a big shareholder and `Bob` hates `Alice`.
2. Something bearish happens to the cred and now everyone is rushing to sell their shares of the cred as the price starts to crash.
3. `Bob` frontruns `Alice`'s intended sale with a `buyShareCredFor` with `curator_` set to `Alice` to buy her 1 share.
4. Now, her `lastTradeTimestamp` gets updated, and her shares are locked for `SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD` causing her sale and any of her future transaction within `SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD` to revert.
5. The share price crashes rapidly (due to the quadratic nature of the bonding curve)
6. At the end of `SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD`, `Alice` is left with shares at a price quadratically lower than before. Resulting in significant losses.

## Proof Of Concept
Add this function to `test/Cred.t.sol`:
```solidity
function test_shareLockBug() public {
  _createCred("BASIC", "SIGNATURE", 0x0);

  address Alice = address(0x123);
  address Bob = address(0x987);
  vm.deal(Alice, 10 ether);
  vm.deal(Bob, 10 ether);

  uint256 buyPrice = bondingCurve.getBuyPriceAfterFee(1, 1, 100);//credid,supply,amount
  vm.prank(Alice);
  cred.buyShareCred{ value: buyPrice }(1, 100, 0); //Alice becomes shareholder

  buyPrice = bondingCurve.getBuyPriceAfterFee(1, 101, 50);//credid,supply,amount
  vm.prank(Bob);
  cred.buyShareCred{ value: buyPrice }(1, 50, 0); //Bob becomes shareholder
  
  skip(2 days); //After some time has passed, both Alice and Bob's shares arent locked and are free to sell

  buyPrice = bondingCurve.getBuyPriceAfterFee(1, 151, 1);//credid,supply,amount
  vm.prank(Bob);
  cred.buyShareCredFor{ value: buyPrice }(1, 1, Alice, 0);//Bob buys 1 share for Alice to lock her shares

  vm.expectRevert(abi.encodeWithSignature("ShareLockPeriodNotPassed(uint256,uint256)", block.timestamp, block.timestamp + cred.SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD()));
  vm.prank(Alice);
  cred.sellShareCred(1, 100, 0); //Alice is unable to sell her shares (confirmed by the vm.expectRevert)
}
```

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Introduce a mapping:
```solidity
mapping(address curator => mapping(address buyer => bool trusted)) public trusted_buyer;
```
As well as a approval function where shareholders can give approval to their other trusted accounts to buy shares under their name.

Then, in `buyShareCredFor`, `trusted_buyer[curator_][msg.sender] == true` can be checked.
