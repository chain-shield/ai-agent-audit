# Vulnerability details

## Impact

The `Cred` contract allows users to buy and sell shares associated with a specific `credId`. Additionally, users can purchase shares on behalf of other accounts, providing a smoother experience for those managing multiple wallets. However, there is a flaw in how the contract updates the timestamp after a trade. 

When a user buys shares, they are unable to sell any of their shares for that `credId` for 10 minutes ([source](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-08-phi/blob/8c0985f7a10b231f916a51af5d506dd6b0c54120/src/Cred.sol#L625)). The issue arises because shares can be purchased on behalf of others. A malicious actor can exploit this by buying a single share on behalf of a target user, preventing that user from selling any of their shares for 10 minutes ([source](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-08-phi/blob/8c0985f7a10b231f916a51af5d506dd6b0c54120/src/Cred.sol#L644)).

In theory, an attacker could write a bot to monitor the mempool and frontrun any `sellShareCred` transaction by sending a `buyShareCredFor` transaction that would be mined first, causing the user’s sell transaction to fail. The maximum limit of this griefing attack, assuming all shares for a given `credId` are available, would be 7 days or 997 transactions. The user would need to send 997 transactions to eventually become the sole owner of all shares for that `credId`, at which point they could sell everything. However, sending 997 transactions is highly impractical, meaning the user would likely be unable to sell any of their shares until all other shares for the `credId` are owned by someone else.
