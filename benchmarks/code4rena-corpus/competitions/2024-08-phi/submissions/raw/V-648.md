# Vulnerability details


## Description
The functions `handleRewardsAndGetValueSent`, `batchClaim`, and `depositBatch` across different contracts use a strict equality (`!=`) check to compare the `msg.value` (the amount of ETH sent by the caller) against the expected amount required by the function logic. This approach can cause unnecessary transaction reverts due to minor discrepancies in the transferred ETH amount, especially when interacting with fee-on-transfer tokens or dealing with imprecise calculations. 

Exact matching of `msg.value` can be challenging, especially when dealing with varying gas fees or slight calculation differences. This may cause transactions to revert unnecessarily, frustrating users. In cases where fee-on-transfer tokens are used, the sender may unknowingly send less than the required amount due to the token's internal fee mechanism. The strict equality check would cause the transaction to revert in such scenarios. 
## Impact
The strict equality check can cause unnecessary reverts if the msg.value is slightly higher than the expected value, possibly due to rounding errors or fee-on-transfer mechanisms in certain tokens. This increases user frustration and the likelihood of failed transactions. Users might find it difficult to send the exact msg.value, especially when interacting with different dApps or wallets that may add small amounts to cover gas fees or similar adjustments. This strict condition can lead to a poor user experience. 

If a contract is deployed in a high-frequency transaction environment, such as automated market makers (AMMs), the strict equality check could expose the contract to frontrunning attacks. Attackers might exploit this by creating transactions that cause reverts, potentially leading to loss of funds or denial-of-service (DoS) conditions.
## Tools Used
Manual Review

## Recommendation
Replace the `!=` checks with `<` to allow the contract to accept any amount greater than or equal to the required value. This ensures that minor discrepancies or overpayments do not cause the transaction to revert. Also refund the remaing amount to `msg.sender` at the end of the function.
