# Vulnerability details

## Impact

A malicious actor can intercept an incoming transaction, extract the `signedData` and `signature` parameters, and modify the `createConfig` properties such as `artist`, `receiver`, `endTime`, `startTime`, `maxSupply`, `mintFee`, and `soulBounded` to their advantage. They can then submit a front-running transaction with these malicious configurations, assuming the signature is valid for one-time use. For example, they could use someone else’s credentials to create art with an `artist` attribute different from the intended one. This could lead to denial of service (DoS) for the actual artist, ig. overriding the actual `artist` param with malicious address, disrupting their ability to manage and update their art.

## Proof of Concept

The root cause of the vulnerability is that the `createConfig` parameters are not validated against the signature, allowing any caller with valid `signedData` and `signature` to override these parameters. **`PhiFactory:createArt` can be called by anyone with valid `signedData` and `signature`, allowing them to override `createConfig` parameters since the function does not validate this parameter against the signature.**

- Found in [src/PhiFactory.sol at Line 199](https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-08-phi/blob/main/src/PhiFactory.sol#L199)

Let us walk through the issue with the following scenario:

1. Alice creates a valid signed transaction with `createConfig` including parameters for `artist`, `receiver`, `endTime`, `startTime`, `maxSupply`, `mintFee`, and `soulBounded`.
2. Bob, a malicious actor, intercepts the transaction, extracts the `signedData` and `signature`, and modifies the `createConfig` parameters to their own benefit.
3. Bob then submits a front-running transaction with the modified `createConfig`, potentially causing incorrect art creation settings and disrupting the intended use of the `createArt` function.
