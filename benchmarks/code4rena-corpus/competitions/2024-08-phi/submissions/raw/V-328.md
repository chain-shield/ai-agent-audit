# Vulnerability details

## Impact
buyShareCredFor() enables you to gift someone a share, but it runs through _handleTrade(), which 
**does not differentiate between buying with gifting**, so user will have all of its credentials locked, which can be exploited via front-run if he has a reasonable amount of creds to sell.

## Proof of Concept
We have Alice and Bob, they both have 250 shares each of the same credential.

Alice starts a tx to sell all of her shares, so she can get the ETH in her wallet. Bob sees the tx in the mempool, so he decides to gift Alice one share **via frontrunning her with buyShareCredFor()**, so that when Alice's tx is about to execute, **it will revert** due to the following LOC -> https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-08-phi/blob/main/src/Cred.sol#L625-L629. 
After that, Bob can freely sell his cred share without the price impact of Alice's sell, which will be much much more than the 1 share that Bob had gifted her.
Alice will be left with her share + the gift, but it will be worth **much much less in ETH** after Bob's sell of his shares

Steps:
1. Alice starts selling her shares
2. Bob sees that and executes a buyShareCredFor()  
 that front-runs her to gift her 1 share
3. Alice tx is starting to get executed, but reverted due to Bob's gift
4. Bob can then sell his shares without the big price impact  
of Alice's shares sell.
5. Alice takes the impact of Bob's selling even though, 
 she first initiated the sell transaction and takes **direct loss in ETH amount**
 (Credential shares are to be sold for ETH).
 if she sells after all of the things that've been described above.

This gives Bob a huge advantage, he can scan the other major shareholders and if some of them makes a call to sell, he can just gift him 1 share to lock his funds via front-running, and sell his shares instead. 
**As I said, one share will be worth much much less than a major shareholder selling his entire stake in ETH**
