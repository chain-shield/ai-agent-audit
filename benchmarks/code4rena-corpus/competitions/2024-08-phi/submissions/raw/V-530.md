# Vulnerability details

## Impact
When handling extremely small or large transaction volumes, the design of the curve function may result in anomalous pricing, such as excessively high or even negative prices. This can cause users to pay unreasonable fees or prevent transactions from occurring, thus undermining market stability and fairness.

## Proof of Concept
https://github.com/code-423n4/2024-08-phi/blob/main/src/curve/BondingCurve.sol#L117

In the contract, the `_curve` function calculates the price based on supply and transaction volume changes. However, in extreme cases (e.g., very small transaction volumes or very large supply), the price calculation may behave unexpectedly.

For instance, suppose the current supply is `1 ether`, and a user attempts to purchase `0.0001 ether`.

- Normally, the price should be very small, around `0.00001 ether`.
- However, due to precision issues or discontinuities in the curve function, the price might be unusually high, such as `0.1 ether`, which is far above the expected value.

This anomaly can lead to users paying excessive fees or transaction failures due to negative or other abnormal values.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

1. Implement minimum thresholds for very small transaction volumes to prevent anomalous price calculations.
2. Optimize the curve function to ensure stability and reasonableness in price calculations under extreme transaction conditions.
