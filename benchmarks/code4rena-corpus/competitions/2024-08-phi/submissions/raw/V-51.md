# Vulnerability details

## Impact
A critical access control vulnerability has been identified in the `_removeCredIdPerAddress` function, allowing an unauthorized attacker to remove a credential ID (credId) from any user's account. This results in the user being unable to manage their shares correctly, specifically preventing them from selling their shares. The issue poses a significant risk to the integrity of the protocol, as it enables attackers to manipulate the state of user accounts, leading to potential financial losses.

## Proof of Concept

## Tools Used
Foundry, Solidity

## Exploit Scenario
1. Initial Setup:

- A user (`anyone`) purchases a share of a credential (credId).
- The user's share is recorded in their account.

2. Attack Execution:

- An attacker calls the _removeCredIdPerAddress function, targeting the credId owned by `anyone`.
- The function removes the credId from `anyone's` account, effectively reducing their share balance.

Impact:

- `anyone` attempts to sell the exact amunt of share as bought earlier, but due to the manipulation, the contract incorrectly identifies that they do not own the share.
- The transaction fails, resulting in prevention of the rightful owner from selling their shares, directly affecting the user's shares / assets and protocol's integrity.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
1. Implement strict access control checks to ensure that only the rightful owner of the credential ID (credId) can call the `_removeCredIdPerAddress` or `_addCredIdPerAddress` function. Consider using an onlyOwner modifier or a similar access control mechanism to restrict function access. Ensure that the `credId` can only be added or removed by the user who owns it. 

2. Secondly, making the function private is of utmost importance since it needs to be called only in the Cred.sol contract while updating internal private state variables.
