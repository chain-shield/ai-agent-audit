# Vulnerability details

## Impact

The `Cred` contract does not provide reentrancy protection and does not follow the CEI pattern, resulting in a situation where a user can reenter the contract before state changes take effect.

1. In `_handleTrade` the user gets a refund for any ether they sent over the shares price.
2. `lastTradeTimestamp[credId_][curator_]` is updated only after the refund call in `msg.sender`.
3. This allows the user to reenter and effectively bypass `SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD`
4. Impact: The user can buy any amount of shares without holding them and risking losing value (value depends on the total supply of shares and can shift during `SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD` period).
5. This allows the user to perform various "unintentional flashloan" attacks, one of which is proposed in the PoC section as an example. 
5.1. The proposed PoC strategy focuses on the attacker making a profit, but he can act destructively and deprive others of all rewards, despite losing money himself (by buying all available shares, claiming reward, and then selling them).

## Proof of Concept

The PoC is quite long, so I've posted it as a secret gist:
[High01PhiPoC.sol](https://gist.github.com/PFAhard/7b23d449c519c55b69b461142a071457)

Most of the PoC is just setup and helpers to run complex protocol functions.

The main "entry point" of the PoC is the `test_Reentrancy` function.

`test_Reentrancy` go through:
1. It creates a cred with 500 as a buy/sell fee
2. It buys another 9 shares for some user, just to make the setup more realistic (10 shares currently in total)
3. It creates art with the cred ID from the first step and the chain ID of the current chain
4. It claims 100 pieces of art, again to simulate the protocol and so that the reward is not zero
5. The attacker contract then runs his helper to:
    1. Buy 4 shares
    2. Distribute the reward to the curators
    3. Withdraw the curator reward
    4. Sell 4 shares at the same price (actually minus the fee)

The attacker's profit and, accordingly, the cost of the attack are limited by 4 factors:
1. Amount of arts minted since last distribution - high direct impact on attacker's profit
2. Amount of shares held by others - very small invert impact (more shares held by others, less profit for attacker)
3. Protocol fee - medium  invert impact (higher fee - lower profit)
4. Creator fee - medium  invert impact (higher fee - lower profit)

I tried to display this relationship on a graph, where X is `amount of shares to buy` * 10^18 and Y is profit in Ethers. The vertical line shows the optimal number of shares to buy.
[Desmos Graph](https://www.desmos.com/calculator/wumgoprvef)
![ProfitForReenter](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8ba870bd-fdfc-4a55-b714-bfebc42fce0a)


For the PoC, a realistic setup was chosen with 100 minted art, 500 for protocol and creator fees, and 10 total shares before the attacker enters.

For this setup, we need to buy 4 shares (both 3 and 5 will give a smaller profit).

For this setup, the attacker's profit is `0.000468739993385027 ether`

If the attacker waits for `SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD`, he risks losing the value of 4 shares (if other users sell their shares, the value of the attacker's shares will fall).

But since he sells them in the same block where he buys them, he does not risk any value and makes a pure profit.
