# Vulnerability details

## Impact
The `createCred`function in the Cred contract and `CreateArt` are marked as payable, allowing it to receive ETH, but it doesn't actually require or handle any payments. This discrepancy between the function's ability to receive ETH and its lack of ETH handling logic can lead to several issues:

Locked Funds: Any ETH sent to this function will be locked in the contract, as there's no mechanism to retrieve or use these funds.
User Error: Users might mistakenly send ETH when creating a credential, thinking it's required, and lose their funds.
Inconsistent Design: This creates confusion about the intended behavior of the function and the overall contract design.
Potential for Unintended Behavior: Future updates or interactions with this contract might assume that the ETH sent to this function is handled in some way, leading to bugs or vulnerabilities.
Gas Wastage: Unnecessary gas is consumed in transactions that send ETH to this function, as the ETH transfer is processed but not used.

While this issue doesn't present an immediate security risk in terms of fund theft or contract manipulation, it represents a significant design flaw that could lead to loss of user funds and confusion about the contract's functionality.

## Proof of Concept
```cred.createCred{value: 1 ether}(
    creator,
    signedData,
    signature,
    signalRoyalty,
    unSignalRoyalty
);```
A user can call this and it'll succeed
