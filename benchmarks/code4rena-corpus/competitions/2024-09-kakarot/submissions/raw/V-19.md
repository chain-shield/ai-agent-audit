# Vulnerability details

This line uses the unsigned_div_rem function to divide the high value by 2 ** 32 (which is 4294967296 in decimal) and discards the remainder. The result of the division (the quotient) is assigned back to high.

Detailed Breakdown:

unsigned_div_rem(high, 2 ** 32) returns two values:
Quotient: The result of integer division (high // 2 ** 32).
Remainder: The result of the modulo operation (high % 2 ** 32).
The _ symbol indicates that the remainder is ignored.
The quotient is assigned to high, effectively reducing high by dividing it into smaller chunks.
In this context, it is essentially shifting the high value by 32 bits to the right, i.e., moving 32 bits out of the high variable.
Here's a breakdown of the issue:

Unchecked unsigned_div_rem: The function uses unsigned_div_rem to truncate the high 12 bytes of the high half:
While this is intended to truncate the high 12 bytes, it doesn't explicitly check if the result of the division is within the expected range (0-2^32).
Potential Overflow: If an attacker provides a very large high value, the division result might exceed 2^32, causing an overflow. This could lead to unexpected behavior or even vulnerabilities, as the subsequent operations would be based on incorrect values.
Potential Vulnerability
No Range Check for high:
Issue: There's no validation to ensure that high fits within an acceptable range before performing the division. Specifically, if high is too large (greater than 2 ** 256 for instance), this operation might result in undefined or unexpected behavior, particularly in environments where arithmetic overflow is not handled well.

To address this vulnerability, you can introduce a check to ensure the result of unsigned_div_rem is within the expected range:

let (_, high) = unsigned_div_rem(high, 2 ** 32);
assert_le_felt(high, 2 ** 32 - 1);

This assertion will fail if the result of the division is greater than or equal to 2^32, preventing potential issues caused by overflow.
