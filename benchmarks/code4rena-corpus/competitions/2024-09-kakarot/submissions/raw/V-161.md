# Vulnerability details

## Description
Contracts that are selfdestructed after Cancun will have all their ether balance sent to a destination after using the `SELFDESTRUCT` opcode. However, Kakarot will also burn any ether that has been sent after selfdestruct has been invoked. 
For example suppose selfdestruct is called on a recently created contract: https://github.com/kkrt-labs/kakarot/blob/7411a5520e8a00be6f5243a50c160e66ad285563/src/kakarot/instructions/system_operations.cairo#L852-L868
Then the entire eth balance will be sent to the recipient. However, suppose there is a transfer of ether to the exact same address later. After the transaction ends and `Starknet.commit` is called, we hit the following line of code: https://github.com/kkrt-labs/kakarot/blob/7411a5520e8a00be6f5243a50c160e66ad285563/src/backend/starknet.cairo#L189-L201
This burns the leftover ether that the account has although the account is still supposed to have ether at the beginning of the next transaction. As a result this ETH is lost permanently. This completely breaks EVM semantics and many use cases (i.e. sending ether to a deterministic contract address that can be created later with create2) 

## Recommended Mitigation Steps
Don't remove the entire balance at the end of the transaction in `Starknet.commit`.
