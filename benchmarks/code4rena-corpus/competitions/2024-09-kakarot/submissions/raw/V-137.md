# Vulnerability details

## Proof of Concept
Recursive function calls, particularly in resource-constrained environments like Starknet (which Cairo leverages), can lead to issues such as stack overflow or gas exhaustion when recursion depth grows too large. Starknet imposes limits on transaction resources, meaning that deep or unbounded recursion risks causing the entire transaction to fail.
The identified functions, including _commit_accounts, _emit_events, _transfer_eth, and _save_storage, exhibit recursion that could cause out-of-gas (OOG) errors or inefficient execution under certain conditions.
 
Possible Bug:
Recursion Depth Limit / Gas Exhaustion
Starknet’s architecture, while supporting recursion, is heavily constrained in terms of resources like gas and recursion depth.
Recursion in functions like _commit_accounts can fail if the number of entries grows beyond the gas limit, leading to the transaction being aborted.

## Recommended Mitigation Steps

Refactor to Iterative Logic: Instead of relying on recursion, refactor the logic in these functions to use loop-based constructs. Iterative approaches are generally more gas-efficient and safer from the perspective of gas limit exhaustion.
Limit Recursion Depth: If recursion is essential for the logic, ensure there's a safe guard on recursion depth or gas usage to prevent crashes due to excessive recursion.
Gas Efficiency Checks: Implement gas checks within recursive functions to abort early if the gas usage approaches dangerous levels, preventing the full transaction from failing.
